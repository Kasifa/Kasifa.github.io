Abstract

I give an explicit real algebraic trigonometric-polynomial initial datum for the unforced incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on the standard three-dimensional torus at viscosity one. In the same full smooth solution, an initially empty Fourier band is generated and its actual quadratic self-interaction supplies three linearly independent target frequency pairs. On one explicit positive rational time interval, the Wiener norm of each target minus this Duhamel source is at most one seventh of the source norm; the source and target are nonzero at every positive time in that interval. The construction cancels the complete mixed four-leaf leading coefficient while retaining a nonzero balanced coefficient. Exact rational computation certifies the coefficients, both real roots of the parameter polynomial, and the original time window. A full Wiener-series construction and explicit heat-tree and infinite-tail bounds transfer those finite identities to the original equations. The result is finite two-layer source selection from one fixed datum; it asserts neither a net energy transfer nor an iterated regeneration or singularity mechanism.

1. Introduction

A nonzero coefficient in a finite nonlinear expansion need not identify the dominant source of the corresponding mode in a solution of the full equations. Other ordered interactions can reach the same frequency, and their leading terms can be of the same size. Heat propagation and the remainder of the expansion must also be controlled. This paper constructs one explicit example in which this source comparison can be made rigorously for three target directions at once.

The datum consists of three cyclically related low-frequency pairs and three cyclically related high-frequency pairs. Each target first appears at four leaves. The complete four-leaf contribution splits into a balanced \(2+2\) part and the sum of the \(1+3\) and \(3+1\) parts. A real algebraic choice of the polarizations cancels the entire mixed leading term, without cancelling the balanced term. The source studied below is nevertheless defined from the actual generated band of the same Navier–Stokes solution, not from its leading coefficient. Two separate remainder estimates establish the comparison on a common interval, including its positive endpoint.

The main contribution is this explicit fixed-datum construction and its quantitative source attribution. Local smooth existence, the heat semigroup, Wiener estimates, ordered binary trees, cyclic symmetry, and real-root isolation are used as tools. We make no claim of a new local existence principle or of priority for those tools. For the classical analytic setting see [1]; for the real algebraic algorithms used in the finite certificate see [2]. The analytic estimates needed here are proved below, and the exact finite arithmetic is specified in Section 4 and Appendix A.

Source selection has a precise meaning in this paper: the norm of a target minus one specified Duhamel source is small relative to that source. This is not an orthogonal decomposition of kinetic energy. In particular, the factor \(1/7\) is not an energy-transfer fraction. The construction concerns two finite layers; no repeated input structure or infinite-scale evolution is asserted.

Section 2 states the explicit datum and the main theorem. Section 3 proves the full-solution and source estimates for general finite data. Section 4 gives the finite coefficient and real-root certificate. Section 5 verifies the support conditions and proves the theorem for the stated datum.

2. The fixed datum and the main theorem

We use \(\T^3=(\R/2\pi\Z)^3\), Fourier coefficients \(f_k=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\T^3}f(x)e^{-ik\cdot x}\dd x\), and

\[ \norm{f}_{\A_m}=\sum_{k\in\Z^3}(1+|k|)^m|f_k|, \qquad \norm{f}_{\A}=\norm{f}_{\A_0},\qquad |f|_{\dot\A_1}=\sum_k|k|\,|f_k|. \]

Vector lengths are Hermitian Euclidean lengths. A frequency dot product inside a Fourier convolution is complex bilinear. For divergence-free inputs define the full ordered bilinear operator

\begin{equation*} B(f,z)_k=-i\PP_k\sum_{r+s=k}(f_r\cdot s)z_s, \quad \PP_k=I-\frac{k\otimes k}{|k|^2}\ (k\ne0), \qquad B(f,z)_0=0. \tag{1}\label{zh-math-eq-1}\end{equation*}

For \(k=0\) the ordered summands vanish by \(f_r\cdot r=0\). For a set \(H\subset\Z^3\), \(P_H\) retains precisely its Fourier coefficients.

Define the rational polynomials

\begin{align*} P(X)={}&\frac{12379608041588}{607290075} +\frac{129169813631527}{1012150125}X +\frac{111813118362046}{337383375}X^2\\ &+\frac{1377133612024132}{3036450375}X^3 +\frac{207263796033514}{607290075}X^4\\ &+\frac{401161881122159}{3036450375}X^5 +\frac{4124532235492}{202430025}X^6,\tag{2}\label{zh-math-eq-2}\\ r(X)={}&\frac{1807613971}{12393675} +\frac{9523582624}{20656125}X +\frac{27816146846}{61968375}X^2 +\frac{196585982}{1770525}X^3,\tag{3}\label{zh-math-eq-3}\\ s(X)={}&\frac{536919079}{2699067} +\frac{9469267576}{13495335}X +\frac{10524885968}{13495335}X^2 +\frac{646271818}{2699067}X^3. \tag{4}\label{zh-math-eq-4}\end{align*}

Lemma 4.1 proves that \(P\) has exactly two real roots, both simple, one in each of

\begin{equation*} (-75/32,-125/64],\qquad(-125/64,-25/16]. \tag{5}\label{zh-math-eq-5}\end{equation*}

Let \(\alpha_*\) be the first root and set

\begin{equation*} v_*=-s(\alpha_*),\qquad w_*=r(\alpha_*),\qquad U=(0,1,\alpha_*),\qquad V=(3v_*+5w_*,-2v_*,-2w_*). \tag{6}\label{zh-math-eq-6}\end{equation*}

There is no further scaling of \((v_*,w_*)\). Put \(R(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(x_3,x_1,x_2)\) and \(p_0=(2,3,5)\), and define

\begin{equation*} \begin{split} a_*(x)=\sum_{j=0}^2\big[&R^jU \{e^{i(R^je_1)\cdot x}+e^{-i(R^je_1)\cdot x}\}\\ &+R^jV \{e^{i(R^jp_0)\cdot x}+e^{-i(R^jp_0)\cdot x}\}\big]. \end{split} \tag{7}\label{zh-math-eq-7}\end{equation*}

This fixes twelve Fourier coefficients, including both signs. In particular, it fixes a single real, smooth, divergence-free, mean-zero initial datum.

Here and below \(F=\supp\widehat a_*\), \(L=(F+F)\setminus\{0\}\), \(\kappa=(3,4,6)\), \(D_j=\{\pm R^j\kappa\}\), and \(D=D_0\cup D_1\cup D_2\). The three positive target vectors have determinant \(91\). The constants used to state the time interval are

\begin{align*} \bar A&=\frac{13990473994187561}{829153382400}, & A_\alpha&=\frac{75}{32}, & \ell&=\frac{32000398665717304027531027}{477317255069800857600}, \tag{8}\label{zh-math-eq-8}\\ \bar b&=28\bar A, &\bar E&=80\bar A^4 7^5+3\bar A\bar b^4, &D_{\mathrm f}&=2(1+A_\alpha^2)+2\bar A\bar b^2. \end{align*}

Define the explicit positive rational number

\begin{equation*} T_*=\min\left\{\frac1{2\bar b},\frac\ell{8\bar E}, \frac1{2D_{\mathrm f}}\right\} =\frac\ell{8\bar E}. \tag{9}\label{zh-math-eq-9}\end{equation*}

The equality and all rational inequalities used below are included in Lemma 4.1. This is a conservative local interval obtained from absolute remainder estimates, not a claimed time scale of amplification.

Theorem 2.1 (Finite two-layer source selection).

For viscosity one and zero external force, the datum (7) has a unique smooth Navier–Stokes solution \(u\) on \([0,T_*]\). Set \(g=P_Lu\) and, for \(j=0,1,2\), define

\begin{equation*} S_j(t)=\int_0^t e^{(t-s)\Delta}P_{D_j}B(g(s),g(s))\dd s. \tag{10}\label{zh-math-eq-10}\end{equation*}

Then \(g(0)=0\). For every \(0<t\le T_*\) and every \(j\),

\begin{gather*} |g_{(1,1,0)}(t)|\ge t/2, \qquad \norm{S_j(t)}_{\A}>0, \qquad \norm{P_{D_j}u(t)}_{\A}>0,\tag{11}\label{zh-math-eq-11}\\ \frac{\norm{P_{D_j}u(t)-S_j(t)}_{\A}} {\norm{S_j(t)}_{\A}}\le\frac17<\frac14. \tag{12}\label{zh-math-eq-12}\end{gather*}

The same ratio bound holds for \(P_Du\) and \(S_D=\sum_{j=0}^2S_j\).

The source in (10) uses the actual projection of the same complete solution at every integration time. No leading-order field or frequency projection is reset as a new initial datum. The remainder \(P_{D_j}u-S_j\) retains all other contributions. The assertion concerns three frequency directions, not the rank of three velocity vectors or a repeated input profile.

3. Full solutions and quantitative four-leaf bounds

This section proves the analytic estimates used for the fixed datum. Let \(a\) be any nonzero real, divergence-free, mean-zero trigonometric polynomial, let \(F=\supp\widehat a\), and put \(A=\norm{a}_{\A}\) and \(K=\max_{k\in F}|k|\). In this section the viscosity is any fixed \(\nu>0\). Define the full, untruncated recursion

\begin{equation*} v_0(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}a,\qquad v_n(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta} B(v_j(s),v_{n-1-j}(s))\dd s\quad(n\ge1). \tag{13}\label{zh-math-eq-13}\end{equation*}

Each \(v_n\) contains \(n+1\) leaves. In particular, if \(\Sigma_m\) is the set of sums of \(m\) elements of \(F\), then

\begin{equation*} \supp\widehat v_n(t)\subset\Sigma_{n+1},\qquad |k|\le(n+1)K\quad(k\in\Sigma_{n+1}). \tag{14}\label{zh-math-eq-14}\end{equation*}

All coincident frequencies are added in this recursion.

Proposition 3.1 (Full-series construction).

Put \(q(t)=4AKt\) and \(T_{\rm a}=(8AK)^{-1}\). On \([0,T_{\rm a}]\), \(u=\sum_{n\ge0}v_n\) is the unique smooth solution of the unforced Navier–Stokes equations with datum \(a\). For every integer \(m\ge0\) and \(N\ge0\),

\begin{align*} \norm{v_n(t)}_{\A}&\le A q^n, &\norm{v_1(t)}_{\A}&\le Aq/4,\tag{15}\label{zh-math-eq-15}\\ \norm{u(t)}_{\A}&\le\frac{A}{1-q}, &\left\lVert u(t)-\sum_{n=0}^Nv_n(t)\right\rVert_{\A} &\le\frac{Aq^{N+1}}{1-q},\tag{16}\label{zh-math-eq-16}\\ \norm{u(t)}_{\A_m}&\le\frac{A(1+K)^m m!}{(1-q)^{m+1}},\tag{17}\label{zh-math-eq-17}\\ \left\lVert u(t)-\sum_{n=0}^Nv_n(t)\right\rVert_{\A_m} &\le\frac{A[1+(N+2)K]^m m!q^{N+1}}{(1-q)^{m+1}}. \tag{18}\label{zh-math-eq-18}\end{align*}

Here and below \(q=q(t)\) in time-dependent estimates.

Proof.

The Leray projections have norm at most one, so

\begin{equation*} \norm{B(f,z)}_{\A}\le\norm{f}_{\A}|z|_{\dot\A_1},\qquad \norm{B(f,z)}_{\A_m}\le\norm{f}_{\A_m}\norm{z}_{\A_{m+1}}. \tag{19}\label{zh-math-eq-19}\end{equation*}

The second bound uses \(1+|r+s|\le(1+|r|)(1+|s|)\). By (14),

\[ \norm{v_n(t)}_{\A}\le K\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j) \int_0^t\norm{v_j(s)}_{\A}\norm{v_{n-1-j}(s)}_{\A}\dd s. \]

Let \(c_0=1\) and \(c_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}c_jc_{n-1-j}\). These count plane binary trees with \(n\) internal vertices; encoding such a tree by a balanced parenthesis word of length \(2n\) gives \(c_n\le4^n\). Symmetry in \(j\) gives \(\sum_j(n-j)c_jc_{n-1-j}=(n+1)c_n/2\). Since integration of \(s^{n-1}\) divides by \(n\), and \((n+1)/(2n)\le1\), induction yields \(\norm{v_n}_{\A}\le c_n A^{n+1}(Kt)^n\). The \(n=1\) estimate directly gives \(A^2Kt=Aq/4\). Thus the series and its tails satisfy (15)–(16) when \(q\le1/2\).

The support bound also gives \(\norm{v_n}_{\A_m}\le A[1+(n+1)K]^m q^n\). Use \((r+1)^m\le m!\binom{r+m}{m}\) and sum the resulting power series. For the tail set \(n=N+1+r\) and use \(1+(N+2+r)K\le[1+(N+2)K](r+1)\). This proves (17)–(18).

In every \(\A_m\) the Laplacian series and the double nonlinear series are absolutely and uniformly convergent: apply the preceding estimates with \(m+2\) and (19) with \(m+1\). Differentiating (13) and summing therefore gives \(u\in C^1([0,T_{\rm a}];\A_m)\) and \(u_t=\nu\Delta u+B(u,u)\), \(u(0)=a\); at zero take the right derivative. Repeated differentiation of this equation uses only finitely many higher spatial norms and proves smoothness on the same closed interval.

For completeness, incompressibility moves the differentiated frequency from the second input to the output. Since \(\sup_{x\ge0}xe^{-\nu\tau x^2}\le(\nu\tau)^{-1/2}\),

\[ \norm{e^{\nu\tau\Delta}B(f,z)}_{\A} \le(\nu\tau)^{-1/2}\norm{f}_{\A}\norm{z}_{\A}. \]

For two continuous mild solutions with the same initial value, their difference on a subinterval of length \(h\) is bounded by its supremum norm times \(2\sqrt{h/\nu}(M_u+M_{\wt u})\). Choosing this factor below one and covering the common compact interval by finitely many subintervals proves uniqueness. This comparison is used after existence has been established.

The recursion preserves reality, incompressibility, and zero mean. Set

\begin{equation*} p_k=-\frac1{|k|^2}\sum_{r+s=k}(k\cdot u_r)(k\cdot u_s)\quad(k\ne0), \qquad p_0=0. \tag{20}\label{zh-math-eq-20}\end{equation*}

The weighted bounds imply smoothness of this pressure and recover \(u_t-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0\).

Proposition 3.2 (Actual source and complete tails).

Under (21), for \(0\le t\le T_{\rm a}\),

\begin{align*} \norm{S-W}_{\A}&\le C_St^4,&C_S&=\tfrac{752}{5}A^5K^4,\tag{24}\label{zh-math-eq-24}\\ \norm{P_Hu-W-M}_{\A}&\le C_Dt^4,&C_D&=512A^5K^4,\tag{25}\label{zh-math-eq-25}\\ \norm{W-t^3W_0}_{\A}&\le C_Wt^4,&C_W&=\tfrac{32}{3}\nu A^4K^5,\tag{26}\label{zh-math-eq-26}\\ \norm{M-t^3M_0}_{\A}&\le C_Mt^4,&C_M&=32\nu A^4K^5. \tag{27}\label{zh-math-eq-27}\end{align*}

In particular, if \(M_0=0\), then

\begin{align*} \norm{P_Hu-S}_{\A}&\le C_Et^4, &C_E&=\tfrac{3312}{5}A^5K^4+32\nu A^4K^5,\tag{28}\label{zh-math-eq-28}\\ \norm{S-t^3W_0}_{\A}&\le C_{\rm src}t^4, &C_{\rm src}&=\tfrac{752}{5}A^5K^4+\tfrac{32}{3}\nu A^4K^5. \tag{29}\label{zh-math-eq-29}\end{align*}

Proof.

The first two conditions in (21) give \(e=g-v_1=P_L\sum_{n\ge2}v_n\). Hence

\[ \norm e_{\A}\le2Aq^2,\qquad |e|_{\dot\A_1}\le AK\frac{q^2(3-2q)}{(1-q)^2}\le8AKq^2, \qquad |v_1|_{\dot\A_1}\le AKq/2. \]

The three ordered terms \(B(e,v_1)+B(v_1,e)+B(e,e)\) have total norm at most \(A^2K(3q^3+16q^4)\). Integrating, with \(q(s)=q(t)s/t\), yields

\[ \norm{S-W}_{\A}\le Aq^4(3/16+4q/5) \le(47/80)Aq^4=C_St^4. \]

The last condition in (21) and (16) give \(\norm{P_Hu-W-M}_{\A}\le Aq^4/(1-q)\le2Aq^4\). These are different remainders: the first includes every higher-order contribution to the actual field \(g\).

There are five plane ordered trees with four leaves: one balanced tree for \(W\), and four chain trees for \(M\). For fixed leaf frequencies and admissible vertex times, the product of all leaf, intermediate, and output heat factors is \(e^{-\nu\mathcal H}\), where \(\mathcal H=\sum_{\text{edges }e}|k_e|^2\Delta t_e\). At any intervening time the propagating edges partition the four leaves. If their descendant counts are \(m_e\), then \(|k_e|\le m_eK\) and \(\sum_e m_e=4\). Consequently

\begin{equation*} 0\le\mathcal H\le16K^2t,\qquad |e^{-\nu\mathcal H}-1|\le16\nu K^2t. \tag{30}\label{zh-math-eq-30}\end{equation*}

This accounts for every heat factor, including those before the first interaction.

Take absolute values before any coefficient cancellation. Each vertex costs at most the leaf count of its second subtree times \(K\), and the sum of absolute leaf products is \(A^4\). The balanced tree has derivative factor \(2K^3\) and time volume \(t^3/3\). The four chains have factors \(6K^3,3K^3,2K^3,K^3\) and volume \(t^3/6\) each. Their absolute bounds are therefore \((2/3)A^4K^3t^3\) and \(2A^4K^3t^3\), respectively. Multiplying by (30) proves (26)–(27). The fractions in (23) are precisely these vertex-time integrals with the heat factors set equal to one. Finally combine the four estimates. In (28), \(W\) cancels exactly between target and source, so \(C_W\) need not be added there.

4. Exact finite coefficient and root certification

We first regard \(X,v,w\) as formal real variables, replace \(\alpha_*\) by \(X\), and use \(V=(3v+5w,-2v,-2w)\). Write \(B=-iQ\) at every vertex. The three-vertex physical phase is \((-i)^3=i\). At \(\kappa\), define real polynomial vectors by

\begin{equation*} (W_0)_\kappa=iC(X;v,w),\qquad (M_0)_\kappa=iZ(X;v,w),\qquad Z=M_{13}+M_{31}. \tag{31}\label{zh-math-eq-31}\end{equation*}

The subscripts \(13,31\) specify the numbers of leaves of the two root subtrees. Every vector in (31) has three components, each linear in \((v,w)\) and of degree at most three in \(X\). Appendix A prints all coefficients of \(C,M_{13},M_{31},Z\), without deleting mixed terms. Write, for example, \(C_j=C_{jv}v+C_{jw}w\).

4.1. A completely specified finite coefficient algorithm

Here is one way to reproduce the table using only rational polynomial arithmetic. Give the four labelled leaves the frequencies \((e_1,e_2,e_3,p_0)\) and amplitudes \((U,RU,R^2U,V)\), with \(U=(0,1,X)\). For each of the five plane binary tree shapes

\[ ((ab)(cd)),\quad (a(b(cd))),\quad(a((bc)d)),\quad ((a(bc))d),\quad(((ab)c)d), \]

run all \(24\) permutations of these four distinct labels. A leaf returns its frequency and amplitude. A vertex whose subtrees return \((r,A)\) and \((s,B)\), with \(m\) total descendant leaves, returns frequency \(k=r+s\) and the polynomial vector

\begin{equation*} \frac1{m-1}\left(I-\frac{k\otimes k}{|k|^2}\right) \{(A\cdot s)B\}\quad(k\ne0), \qquad 0\quad(k=0). \tag{32}\label{zh-math-eq-32}\end{equation*}

The dot product is bilinear. Sum the balanced shape for \(C\), the two shapes whose first root subtree is a leaf for \(M_{13}\), and the remaining two for \(M_{31}\). To obtain the two columns separately use \(V=(3,-2,0)\) and \(V=(5,0,-2)\). Each complete expression is linear in that one high leaf; the common low leaves must not be added twice. The factors \(1/(m-1)\) give weight \(1/3\) to the balanced tree and \(1/6\) to each chain.

For a target \(\varepsilon R^j\kappa\), where \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), rotate all four leaf frequencies and amplitudes by \(R^j\) and multiply the frequencies, but not the real amplitudes, by \(\varepsilon\). The same algorithm gives \(\varepsilon R^jC\), \(\varepsilon R^jM_{13}\), and \(\varepsilon R^jM_{31}\). This also follows directly from \(\PP_{Rk}=R\PP_kR^{-1}\) and the three differentiated vertices. After the factor \(i\) is restored, the two signs are complex conjugates. Section 5 proves that these four labelled leaves exhaust all possible contributions to the targets through four leaves in the full twelve-mode problem.

4.2. Polynomial identities and finite arithmetic conclusions

Set

\[ \mathsf M(X)=\begin{pmatrix}Z_{1v}&Z_{1w}\\ Z_{2v}&Z_{2w}\end{pmatrix}. \]

Direct rational polynomial addition and multiplication in the printed table give

\begin{equation*} \det\mathsf M=P,\quad Z_{1v}=r,\quad Z_{1w}=s, \quad 3Y_1+4Y_2+6Y_3=0 \tag{33}\label{zh-math-eq-33}\end{equation*}

for each column \(Y\) of each of \(C,M_{13},M_{31},Z\). Define the normalized polynomials

\begin{equation*} \mathcal C_j=-C_{jv}s+C_{jw}r,\qquad \mathcal Z_j=-Z_{jv}s+Z_{jw}r. \tag{34}\label{zh-math-eq-34}\end{equation*}

In particular,

\begin{equation*} \mathcal Z=(0,P,-2P/3). \tag{35}\label{zh-math-eq-35}\end{equation*}

All objects used in the following finite calculation are thus specified by rational numbers in this paper.

Lemma 4.1 (Rational certificate).

Let \(Q=P/(4124532235492/202430025)\). The following exact arithmetic statements hold.

  1. \(\gcd(Q,Q')=1\). All real roots lie in \((-25,25)\), and the Sturm variation numbers at \(-25,-75/32,-125/64,-25/16,25\) are respectively \(4,4,3,2,2\). None of those endpoints is a root. Thus (5) contains exactly the two real roots of \(P\).

  2. Each of \(Z_{1v},Z_{1w},Z_{2v},Z_{2w}\), the three \(\mathcal C_j\), and the three components of \((-3s+5r,2s,-2r)\) has greatest common divisor one with \(Q\). Thus the original matrix has rank one at both roots, the chosen \((v,w)\) is nonzero, and all three normalized balanced components are nonzero there.

  3. On \(I=[-75/32,-125/64]\), interval evaluation gives

For the greatest common divisor use Euclid's successive remainders and make the last nonzero remainder monic. This gives the gcd values in items 1 and 2. In particular the monic \(Q\) is square-free; neither irreducibility nor a number-field representation is needed.

5. Application to the fixed twelve-mode datum

We verify the support hypotheses for the full recursion, then apply the exact algebra and analytic bounds. These steps also prove Theorem 2.1.

5.1. All possible short-leaf target contributions

The coordinate sums of the positive low and high frequencies are \(1\) and \(10\), respectively. Modulo nine every initial frequency has coordinate sum \(1\) or \(-1\), whereas every member of \(F+F\) has sum \(0,2\), or \(-2\). Thus \(L\cap F=\varnothing\). All two-leaf nonzero frequencies belong to \(L\), and zero output vanishes by incompressibility. Therefore \(P_Lv_1=v_1\) as an identity for the entire time interval, and \(g(0)=0\).

Consider a representation of \(\kappa\) by at most four signed initial frequencies. If \(h\) is the number of positive high leaves minus negative high leaves and \(l\) the analogous signed number of low leaves, its coordinate sum satisfies \(10h+l=13\) and \(|h|+|l|\le4\). The only possibility is \(h=1,l=3\). The representation must therefore use exactly one positive high leaf and three positive low leaves. The differences from the three possible high leaves are

\[ \kappa-p_0=(1,1,1),\quad \kappa-Rp_0=(-2,2,3),\quad \kappa-R^2p_0=(0,-1,4). \]

Only the first is a sum of three positive coordinate vectors. Thus the four leaves are precisely \(p_0,e_1,e_2,e_3\), each once. Rotation and sign reversal prove the corresponding statement for all six targets. In particular \(P_{D_j}v_n=0\) for \(n=0,1,2\) at all times. These support identities follow from (14), not from a test of only the initial Taylor coefficients.

It follows that the algorithm of Section 4 computes the complete four-leaf leading terms at the targets. By (35), \(M_0=0\) on every \(D_j\). By (36) and the restored phase, the common pair norm is

\begin{equation*} \omega:=\norm{P_{D_j}W_0}_{\A}=2|\mathcal C(\alpha_*)|\ge\ell>0. \tag{38}\label{zh-math-eq-38}\end{equation*}

Here \(W_0\) can equivalently be defined with \(H=D\) and then restricted to \(D_j\). The factor two counts both conjugate Fourier coefficients.

5.2. Full-data bounds and the original time interval

The actual norm and support radius of (7) are

\[ A=6\left(\sqrt{1+\alpha_*^2} +\sqrt{(3v_*+5w_*)^2+4v_*^2+4w_*^2}\right),\qquad K=\sqrt{38}<7. \]

The upper bounds of Lemma 4.1 give \(A\le\bar A\); in particular every one of the twelve modes is included. Consequently \(4AK\le\bar b\), and \(T_*\le(2\bar b)^{-1}\le(8AK)^{-1}\). Proposition 3.1 constructs the unique full smooth solution throughout \([0,T_*]\). Rotation invariance and uniqueness imply \(u(Rx,t)=Ru(x,t)\), although the following bounds require only the coefficient symmetries already established.

Since

\[ \bar E=80\bar A^4 7^5+768\bar A^5 7^4, \]

it dominates both \(C_E\) and \(C_{\rm src}\) in (28)–(29), with \(\nu=1\). For each \(D_j\), Proposition 3.2 therefore gives

\begin{equation*} \norm{S_j-t^3P_{D_j}W_0}_{\A}\le\bar E t^4, \qquad \norm{P_{D_j}u-S_j}_{\A}\le\bar E t^4. \tag{39}\label{zh-math-eq-39}\end{equation*}

For \(0<t\le T_*\), \(\bar Et\le\ell/8\le\omega/8\). The reverse triangle inequality gives

\[ \norm{S_j}_{\A}\ge\tfrac78\omega t^3,\qquad \norm{P_{D_j}u}_{\A}\ge\tfrac34\omega t^3, \qquad \frac{\norm{P_{D_j}u-S_j}_{\A}}{\norm{S_j}_{\A}}\le\frac17. \]

The \(D_j\) are disjoint, so summing their Wiener norms proves the same ratio for \(D\).

5.3. Nonzero generation of the initially empty band

Set \(r_0=(1,1,0)\). Its only two-leaf input pair is \(e_1,e_2\) and its reverse. Indeed coordinate sums for two leaves force two positive low leaves. With \(U=(0,1,\alpha_*)\) and \(RU=(\alpha_*,0,1)\), the ordered sum before projection is \((\alpha_*,\alpha_*,1+\alpha_*^2)\). Projection onto \(r_0^\perp\) removes the first two coordinates. Both input frequencies have squared length one and the output has squared length two. Thus the complete first response is exactly

\begin{equation*} (v_1)_{r_0}(t)=-it e^{-2t}(1+\alpha_*^2)e_3. \tag{40}\label{zh-math-eq-40}\end{equation*}

The initial coefficient at \(r_0\) is zero. Using \(1-e^{-2t}\le2t\) and (16),

\[ \left|g_{r_0}(t)+it(1+\alpha_*^2)e_3\right| \le2(1+A_\alpha^2)t^2+2\bar A\bar b^2t^2 =D_{\rm f}t^2. \]

Since \(D_{\rm f}T_*<1/2\), this proves \(|g_{r_0}(t)|\ge t/2\) on the whole positive interval. All conclusions of Theorem 2.1 now follow.

6. Scope and verification

The conclusion identifies a specified Duhamel contribution to three target frequency pairs in one full solution. The datum is fixed before time evolves, and the source uses the actual generated band throughout the integral. Exact cancellation concerns the complete mixed leading coefficient. The mixed positive-time trees need not vanish; their heat correction is included in the fourth-order error.

The time \(T_*\) is obtained from absolute majorants and is deliberately conservative. It supplies an explicit nonempty interval and a relative source estimate, without optimizing the interval. A lower bound on a target Fourier coefficient is not a statement about the sign of the energy flux. Nor does the linear independence of the target frequencies show that their velocity polarizations form a reusable seed. The theorem does not establish repeated regeneration, an infinite cascade, norm growth, or finite-time singularity.

Abstract

I give an explicit real algebraic trigonometric-polynomial initial datum for the unforced incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on the standard three-dimensional torus at viscosity one. In the same full smooth solution, an initially empty Fourier band is generated and its actual quadratic self-interaction supplies three linearly independent target frequency pairs. On one explicit positive rational time interval, the Wiener norm of each target minus this Duhamel source is at most one seventh of the source norm; the source and target are nonzero at every positive time in that interval. The construction cancels the complete mixed four-leaf leading coefficient while retaining a nonzero balanced coefficient. Exact rational computation certifies the coefficients, both real roots of the parameter polynomial, and the original time window. A full Wiener-series construction and explicit heat-tree and infinite-tail bounds transfer those finite identities to the original equations. The result is finite two-layer source selection from one fixed datum; it asserts neither a net energy transfer nor an iterated regeneration or singularity mechanism.

1. Introduction

A nonzero coefficient in a finite nonlinear expansion need not identify the dominant source of the corresponding mode in a solution of the full equations. Other ordered interactions can reach the same frequency, and their leading terms can be of the same size. Heat propagation and the remainder of the expansion must also be controlled. This paper constructs one explicit example in which this source comparison can be made rigorously for three target directions at once.

The datum consists of three cyclically related low-frequency pairs and three cyclically related high-frequency pairs. Each target first appears at four leaves. The complete four-leaf contribution splits into a balanced \(2+2\) part and the sum of the \(1+3\) and \(3+1\) parts. A real algebraic choice of the polarizations cancels the entire mixed leading term, without cancelling the balanced term. The source studied below is nevertheless defined from the actual generated band of the same Navier–Stokes solution, not from its leading coefficient. Two separate remainder estimates establish the comparison on a common interval, including its positive endpoint.

The main contribution is this explicit fixed-datum construction and its quantitative source attribution. Local smooth existence, the heat semigroup, Wiener estimates, ordered binary trees, cyclic symmetry, and real-root isolation are used as tools. We make no claim of a new local existence principle or of priority for those tools. For the classical analytic setting see [1]; for the real algebraic algorithms used in the finite certificate see [2]. The analytic estimates needed here are proved below, and the exact finite arithmetic is specified in Section 4 and Appendix A.

Source selection has a precise meaning in this paper: the norm of a target minus one specified Duhamel source is small relative to that source. This is not an orthogonal decomposition of kinetic energy. In particular, the factor \(1/7\) is not an energy-transfer fraction. The construction concerns two finite layers; no repeated input structure or infinite-scale evolution is asserted.

Section 2 states the explicit datum and the main theorem. Section 3 proves the full-solution and source estimates for general finite data. Section 4 gives the finite coefficient and real-root certificate. Section 5 verifies the support conditions and proves the theorem for the stated datum.

2. The fixed datum and the main theorem

We use \(\T^3=(\R/2\pi\Z)^3\), Fourier coefficients \(f_k=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\T^3}f(x)e^{-ik\cdot x}\dd x\), and

\[ \norm{f}_{\A_m}=\sum_{k\in\Z^3}(1+|k|)^m|f_k|, \qquad \norm{f}_{\A}=\norm{f}_{\A_0},\qquad |f|_{\dot\A_1}=\sum_k|k|\,|f_k|. \]

Vector lengths are Hermitian Euclidean lengths. A frequency dot product inside a Fourier convolution is complex bilinear. For divergence-free inputs define the full ordered bilinear operator

\begin{equation*} B(f,z)_k=-i\PP_k\sum_{r+s=k}(f_r\cdot s)z_s, \quad \PP_k=I-\frac{k\otimes k}{|k|^2}\ (k\ne0), \qquad B(f,z)_0=0. \tag{1}\label{en-math-eq-1}\end{equation*}

For \(k=0\) the ordered summands vanish by \(f_r\cdot r=0\). For a set \(H\subset\Z^3\), \(P_H\) retains precisely its Fourier coefficients.

Define the rational polynomials

\begin{align*} P(X)={}&\frac{12379608041588}{607290075} +\frac{129169813631527}{1012150125}X +\frac{111813118362046}{337383375}X^2\\ &+\frac{1377133612024132}{3036450375}X^3 +\frac{207263796033514}{607290075}X^4\\ &+\frac{401161881122159}{3036450375}X^5 +\frac{4124532235492}{202430025}X^6,\tag{2}\label{en-math-eq-2}\\ r(X)={}&\frac{1807613971}{12393675} +\frac{9523582624}{20656125}X +\frac{27816146846}{61968375}X^2 +\frac{196585982}{1770525}X^3,\tag{3}\label{en-math-eq-3}\\ s(X)={}&\frac{536919079}{2699067} +\frac{9469267576}{13495335}X +\frac{10524885968}{13495335}X^2 +\frac{646271818}{2699067}X^3. \tag{4}\label{en-math-eq-4}\end{align*}

Lemma 4.1 proves that \(P\) has exactly two real roots, both simple, one in each of

\begin{equation*} (-75/32,-125/64],\qquad(-125/64,-25/16]. \tag{5}\label{en-math-eq-5}\end{equation*}

Let \(\alpha_*\) be the first root and set

\begin{equation*} v_*=-s(\alpha_*),\qquad w_*=r(\alpha_*),\qquad U=(0,1,\alpha_*),\qquad V=(3v_*+5w_*,-2v_*,-2w_*). \tag{6}\label{en-math-eq-6}\end{equation*}

There is no further scaling of \((v_*,w_*)\). Put \(R(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(x_3,x_1,x_2)\) and \(p_0=(2,3,5)\), and define

\begin{equation*} \begin{split} a_*(x)=\sum_{j=0}^2\big[&R^jU \{e^{i(R^je_1)\cdot x}+e^{-i(R^je_1)\cdot x}\}\\ &+R^jV \{e^{i(R^jp_0)\cdot x}+e^{-i(R^jp_0)\cdot x}\}\big]. \end{split} \tag{7}\label{en-math-eq-7}\end{equation*}

This fixes twelve Fourier coefficients, including both signs. In particular, it fixes a single real, smooth, divergence-free, mean-zero initial datum.

Here and below \(F=\supp\widehat a_*\), \(L=(F+F)\setminus\{0\}\), \(\kappa=(3,4,6)\), \(D_j=\{\pm R^j\kappa\}\), and \(D=D_0\cup D_1\cup D_2\). The three positive target vectors have determinant \(91\). The constants used to state the time interval are

\begin{align*} \bar A&=\frac{13990473994187561}{829153382400}, & A_\alpha&=\frac{75}{32}, & \ell&=\frac{32000398665717304027531027}{477317255069800857600}, \tag{8}\label{en-math-eq-8}\\ \bar b&=28\bar A, &\bar E&=80\bar A^4 7^5+3\bar A\bar b^4, &D_{\mathrm f}&=2(1+A_\alpha^2)+2\bar A\bar b^2. \end{align*}

Define the explicit positive rational number

\begin{equation*} T_*=\min\left\{\frac1{2\bar b},\frac\ell{8\bar E}, \frac1{2D_{\mathrm f}}\right\} =\frac\ell{8\bar E}. \tag{9}\label{en-math-eq-9}\end{equation*}

The equality and all rational inequalities used below are included in Lemma 4.1. This is a conservative local interval obtained from absolute remainder estimates, not a claimed time scale of amplification.

Theorem 2.1 (Finite two-layer source selection).

For viscosity one and zero external force, the datum (7) has a unique smooth Navier–Stokes solution \(u\) on \([0,T_*]\). Set \(g=P_Lu\) and, for \(j=0,1,2\), define

\begin{equation*} S_j(t)=\int_0^t e^{(t-s)\Delta}P_{D_j}B(g(s),g(s))\dd s. \tag{10}\label{en-math-eq-10}\end{equation*}

Then \(g(0)=0\). For every \(0<t\le T_*\) and every \(j\),

\begin{gather*} |g_{(1,1,0)}(t)|\ge t/2, \qquad \norm{S_j(t)}_{\A}>0, \qquad \norm{P_{D_j}u(t)}_{\A}>0,\tag{11}\label{en-math-eq-11}\\ \frac{\norm{P_{D_j}u(t)-S_j(t)}_{\A}} {\norm{S_j(t)}_{\A}}\le\frac17<\frac14. \tag{12}\label{en-math-eq-12}\end{gather*}

The same ratio bound holds for \(P_Du\) and \(S_D=\sum_{j=0}^2S_j\).

The source in (10) uses the actual projection of the same complete solution at every integration time. No leading-order field or frequency projection is reset as a new initial datum. The remainder \(P_{D_j}u-S_j\) retains all other contributions. The assertion concerns three frequency directions, not the rank of three velocity vectors or a repeated input profile.

3. Full solutions and quantitative four-leaf bounds

This section proves the analytic estimates used for the fixed datum. Let \(a\) be any nonzero real, divergence-free, mean-zero trigonometric polynomial, let \(F=\supp\widehat a\), and put \(A=\norm{a}_{\A}\) and \(K=\max_{k\in F}|k|\). In this section the viscosity is any fixed \(\nu>0\). Define the full, untruncated recursion

\begin{equation*} v_0(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}a,\qquad v_n(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta} B(v_j(s),v_{n-1-j}(s))\dd s\quad(n\ge1). \tag{13}\label{en-math-eq-13}\end{equation*}

Each \(v_n\) contains \(n+1\) leaves. In particular, if \(\Sigma_m\) is the set of sums of \(m\) elements of \(F\), then

\begin{equation*} \supp\widehat v_n(t)\subset\Sigma_{n+1},\qquad |k|\le(n+1)K\quad(k\in\Sigma_{n+1}). \tag{14}\label{en-math-eq-14}\end{equation*}

All coincident frequencies are added in this recursion.

Proposition 3.1 (Full-series construction).

Proof.

The Leray projections have norm at most one, so

\begin{equation*} \norm{B(f,z)}_{\A}\le\norm{f}_{\A}|z|_{\dot\A_1},\qquad \norm{B(f,z)}_{\A_m}\le\norm{f}_{\A_m}\norm{z}_{\A_{m+1}}. \tag{19}\label{en-math-eq-19}\end{equation*}

The second bound uses \(1+|r+s|\le(1+|r|)(1+|s|)\). By (14),

\[ \norm{v_n(t)}_{\A}\le K\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j) \int_0^t\norm{v_j(s)}_{\A}\norm{v_{n-1-j}(s)}_{\A}\dd s. \]

Let \(c_0=1\) and \(c_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}c_jc_{n-1-j}\). These count plane binary trees with \(n\) internal vertices; encoding such a tree by a balanced parenthesis word of length \(2n\) gives \(c_n\le4^n\). Symmetry in \(j\) gives \(\sum_j(n-j)c_jc_{n-1-j}=(n+1)c_n/2\). Since integration of \(s^{n-1}\) divides by \(n\), and \((n+1)/(2n)\le1\), induction yields \(\norm{v_n}_{\A}\le c_n A^{n+1}(Kt)^n\). The \(n=1\) estimate directly gives \(A^2Kt=Aq/4\). Thus the series and its tails satisfy (15)–(16) when \(q\le1/2\).

The support bound also gives \(\norm{v_n}_{\A_m}\le A[1+(n+1)K]^m q^n\). Use \((r+1)^m\le m!\binom{r+m}{m}\) and sum the resulting power series. For the tail set \(n=N+1+r\) and use \(1+(N+2+r)K\le[1+(N+2)K](r+1)\). This proves (17)–(18).

In every \(\A_m\) the Laplacian series and the double nonlinear series are absolutely and uniformly convergent: apply the preceding estimates with \(m+2\) and (19) with \(m+1\). Differentiating (13) and summing therefore gives \(u\in C^1([0,T_{\rm a}];\A_m)\) and \(u_t=\nu\Delta u+B(u,u)\), \(u(0)=a\); at zero take the right derivative. Repeated differentiation of this equation uses only finitely many higher spatial norms and proves smoothness on the same closed interval.

For completeness, incompressibility moves the differentiated frequency from the second input to the output. Since \(\sup_{x\ge0}xe^{-\nu\tau x^2}\le(\nu\tau)^{-1/2}\),

\[ \norm{e^{\nu\tau\Delta}B(f,z)}_{\A} \le(\nu\tau)^{-1/2}\norm{f}_{\A}\norm{z}_{\A}. \]

For two continuous mild solutions with the same initial value, their difference on a subinterval of length \(h\) is bounded by its supremum norm times \(2\sqrt{h/\nu}(M_u+M_{\wt u})\). Choosing this factor below one and covering the common compact interval by finitely many subintervals proves uniqueness. This comparison is used after existence has been established.

The recursion preserves reality, incompressibility, and zero mean. Set

\begin{equation*} p_k=-\frac1{|k|^2}\sum_{r+s=k}(k\cdot u_r)(k\cdot u_s)\quad(k\ne0), \qquad p_0=0. \tag{20}\label{en-math-eq-20}\end{equation*}

The weighted bounds imply smoothness of this pressure and recover \(u_t-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0\).

Proposition 3.2 (Actual source and complete tails).

\[ \norm{S-W}_{\A}\le Aq^4(3/16+4q/5) \le(47/80)Aq^4=C_St^4. \]

There are five plane ordered trees with four leaves: one balanced tree for \(W\), and four chain trees for \(M\). 4.1. A completely specified finite coefficient algorithm

Here is one way to reproduce the table using only rational polynomial arithmetic. Give the four labelled leaves the frequencies \((e_1,e_2,e_3,p_0)\) and amplitudes \((U,RU,R^2U,V)\), with \(U=(0,1,X)\). For each of the five plane binary tree shapes

\[ ((ab)(cd)),\quad (a(b(cd))),\quad(a((bc)d)),\quad ((a(bc))d),\quad(((ab)c)d), \]

run all \(24\) permutations of these four distinct labels. A leaf returns its frequency and amplitude. A vertex whose subtrees return \((r,A)\) and \((s,B)\), with \(m\) total descendant leaves, returns frequency \(k=r+s\) and the polynomial vector

\begin{equation*} \frac1{m-1}\left(I-\frac{k\otimes k}{|k|^2}\right) \{(A\cdot s)B\}\quad(k\ne0), \qquad 0\quad(k=0). \tag{32}\label{en-math-eq-32}\end{equation*}

To obtain the two columns separately use \(V=(3,-2,0)\) and \(V=(5,0,-2)\). Each complete expression is linear in that one high leaf; the common low leaves must not be added twice. 5. Application to the fixed twelve-mode datum

We verify the support hypotheses for the full recursion, then apply the exact algebra and analytic bounds. These steps also prove Theorem 2.1.

5.1. All possible short-leaf target contributions

The coordinate sums of the positive low and high frequencies are \(1\) and \(10\), respectively. Modulo nine every initial frequency has coordinate sum \(1\) or \(-1\), whereas every member of \(F+F\) has sum \(0,2\), or \(-2\). Thus \(L\cap F=\varnothing\). All two-leaf nonzero frequencies belong to \(L\), and zero output vanishes by incompressibility. Therefore \(P_Lv_1=v_1\) as an identity for the entire time interval, and \(g(0)=0\).

Consider a representation of \(\kappa\) by at most four signed initial frequencies. If \(h\) is the number of positive high leaves minus negative high leaves and \(l\) the analogous signed number of low leaves, its coordinate sum satisfies \(10h+l=13\) and \(|h|+|l|\le4\). The only possibility is \(h=1,l=3\). The representation must therefore use exactly one positive high leaf and three positive low leaves. The differences from the three possible high leaves are

\[ \kappa-p_0=(1,1,1),\quad \kappa-Rp_0=(-2,2,3),\quad \kappa-R^2p_0=(0,-1,4). \]

Only the first is a sum of three positive coordinate vectors. Thus the four leaves are precisely \(p_0,e_1,e_2,e_3\), each once. Rotation and sign reversal prove the corresponding statement for all six targets. In particular \(P_{D_j}v_n=0\) for \(n=0,1,2\) at all times. These support identities follow from (14), not from a test of only the initial Taylor coefficients.

It follows that the algorithm of Section 4 computes the complete four-leaf leading terms at the targets. By (35), \(M_0=0\) on every \(D_j\). By (36) and the restored phase, the common pair norm is

\begin{equation*} \omega:=\norm{P_{D_j}W_0}_{\A}=2|\mathcal C(\alpha_*)|\ge\ell>0. \tag{38}\label{en-math-eq-38}\end{equation*}

Here \(W_0\) can equivalently be defined with \(H=D\) and then restricted to \(D_j\). The factor two counts both conjugate Fourier coefficients.

5.2. Full-data bounds and the original time interval

The actual norm and support radius of (7) are

\[ A=6\left(\sqrt{1+\alpha_*^2} +\sqrt{(3v_*+5w_*)^2+4v_*^2+4w_*^2}\right),\qquad K=\sqrt{38}<7. \]

The upper bounds of Lemma 4.1 give \(A\le\bar A\); in particular every one of the twelve modes is included. Consequently \(4AK\le\bar b\), and \(T_*\le(2\bar b)^{-1}\le(8AK)^{-1}\). Proposition 3.1 constructs the unique full smooth solution throughout \([0,T_*]\). Rotation invariance and uniqueness imply \(u(Rx,t)=Ru(x,t)\), although the following bounds require only the coefficient symmetries already established.

Since

\[ \bar E=80\bar A^4 7^5+768\bar A^5 7^4, \]

it dominates both \(C_E\) and \(C_{\rm src}\) in (28)–(29), with \(\nu=1\). For each \(D_j\), Proposition 3.2 therefore gives

\begin{equation*} \norm{S_j-t^3P_{D_j}W_0}_{\A}\le\bar E t^4, \qquad \norm{P_{D_j}u-S_j}_{\A}\le\bar E t^4. \tag{39}\label{en-math-eq-39}\end{equation*}

For \(0<t\le T_*\), \(\bar Et\le\ell/8\le\omega/8\). The reverse triangle inequality gives

\[ \norm{S_j}_{\A}\ge\tfrac78\omega t^3,\qquad \norm{P_{D_j}u}_{\A}\ge\tfrac34\omega t^3, \qquad \frac{\norm{P_{D_j}u-S_j}_{\A}}{\norm{S_j}_{\A}}\le\frac17. \]

The \(D_j\) are disjoint, so summing their Wiener norms proves the same ratio for \(D\).

5.3. Nonzero generation of the initially empty band

Set \(r_0=(1,1,0)\). Its only two-leaf input pair is \(e_1,e_2\) and its reverse. Indeed coordinate sums for two leaves force two positive low leaves. With \(U=(0,1,\alpha_*)\) and \(RU=(\alpha_*,0,1)\), the ordered sum before projection is \((\alpha_*,\alpha_*,1+\alpha_*^2)\). Projection onto \(r_0^\perp\) removes the first two coordinates. Both input frequencies have squared length one and the output has squared length two. Thus the complete first response is exactly

\begin{equation*} (v_1)_{r_0}(t)=-it e^{-2t}(1+\alpha_*^2)e_3. \tag{40}\label{en-math-eq-40}\end{equation*}

The initial coefficient at \(r_0\) is zero. Using \(1-e^{-2t}\le2t\) and (16),

\[ \left|g_{r_0}(t)+it(1+\alpha_*^2)e_3\right| \le2(1+A_\alpha^2)t^2+2\bar A\bar b^2t^2 =D_{\rm f}t^2. \]

Since \(D_{\rm f}T_*<1/2\), this proves \(|g_{r_0}(t)|\ge t/2\) on the whole positive interval. All conclusions of Theorem 2.1 now follow.

6. Scope and verification

The conclusion identifies a specified Duhamel contribution to three target frequency pairs in one full solution. The datum is fixed before time evolves, and the source uses the actual generated band throughout the integral. Exact cancellation concerns the complete mixed leading coefficient. The mixed positive-time trees need not vanish; their heat correction is included in the fourth-order error.

The time \(T_*\) is obtained from absolute majorants and is deliberately conservative. It supplies an explicit nonempty interval and a relative source estimate, without optimizing the interval. A lower bound on a target Fourier coefficient is not a statement about the sign of the energy flux. Nor does the linear independence of the target frequencies show that their velocity polarizations form a reusable seed. The theorem does not establish repeated regeneration, an infinite cascade, norm growth, or finite-time singularity.
