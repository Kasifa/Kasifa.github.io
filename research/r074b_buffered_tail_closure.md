# R0.74B — buffered closure of the Gaussian annular tails

## Status and scope

This note proves a buffered Caccioppoli estimate for the R0.74A quadratic
exterior tails. The main proof keeps the original R0.73X Gaussian weight
and pays enlarged shells at radius \(2R\). Suitability and a strictly
earlier time slab are used explicitly.

Labels are literal: **PROVED**, **FINITE**, **OPEN**, and **NOT CLAY**.
This is a positive-scale size lemma, not a regularity result.

For \(\square\in\{{\rm std},\nu\}\), put
\[
 \kappa_{\rm std}=1,\qquad \kappa_\nu=\nu,
 \qquad I_\rho^\square=(t_0-\rho^2/\kappa_\square,t_0).
\tag{0.1}
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal E^\square(z_0,\rho)
 =\rho^{-1}\mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_{I_\rho^\square}\int_{B_\rho}|u|^2
 +\nu\rho^{-1}\int_{I_\rho^\square}\!\int_{B_\rho}|\nabla u|^2.
\tag{0.2}
\]
Assume
\[
 0<R<\pi/16,\qquad 0<\theta\le1,\qquad
 I_{8R}^\square\Subset(0,T),
\tag{0.3}
\]
and let \((u,p)\) be a periodic suitable weak solution with
\[
 u\in L_t^\infty L_x^2\cap L_t^2H_x^1,
 \qquad p\in L_{t,x}^{3/2}
 \quad\hbox{on }I_{8R}^\square\times\mathbb T^3.
\tag{0.3a}
\]
The smaller radius licenses the frozen R0.73X definitions at \(2R\); the
\(8R\) energy pays the local pressure generated by the \(2R\)-scale split.

In the lift put
\[
 A_m(R)=\{2^mR\le |y-\widetilde x_0|<2^{m+1}R\},\qquad
 \gamma_m(\theta)=\theta^{-2}e^{-4^{m-1}/(32\theta)}.
\tag{0.4}
\]
Define
\[
 U_\gamma(t)=\sum_{m\ge1}\gamma_m\int_{A_m(R)}|\widetilde u(t)|^2,\quad
 G_\gamma(t)=\sum_{m\ge1}\gamma_m\int_{A_m(R)}|\nabla\widetilde u(t)|^2,
\tag{0.5}
\]
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal U_{\rm ext}^{\infty,\square}
 =\mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_{I_R^\square}R^{-1}U_\gamma(t),\qquad
 \mathcal D_{\rm ext}^{\square}
 =\nu R^{-1}\int_{I_R^\square}G_\gamma(t)\,dt.}
\tag{0.6}
\]
At \(2R\), retain exactly
\[
 \mathcal A_{\rm ext}^\square(z_0,2R;\theta)
 =\mathcal G_{u,p}^\square(z_0,2R;\theta)
 +\mathcal H_u^\square(z_0,2R),
\tag{0.7}
\]
with gauge \(c_{2R}(t)\).

## 1. Canonical doubled-radius shift

The exact identity
\[
 \boxed{A_k(R)=A_{k-1}(2R),\qquad k\ge2}
\tag{1.1}
\]
removes the superexponential shift obstruction. A target row weighted by
\(\gamma_m\), enlarged by one \(R\)-annulus, meets
\(A_{m+1}(R)=A_m(2R)\) with the same weight. Current and inward neighbors
meet \(2R\)-annuli with larger weights. More precisely, for \(m\ge3\),
\[
 \gamma_m1_{A_{m-1}(R)\cup A_m(R)\cup A_{m+1}(R)}
 \le \sum_{j=m-2}^m\gamma_j1_{A_j(2R)}.
\tag{1.2}
\]
The inner pieces generated when \(m=1,2\) meet \(B_{4R}\), so they are
kept as core rows rather than hidden in (1.2).
At a fixed radius, the false comparison
\(\gamma_m\lesssim\gamma_{m+1}\) would lose superexponentially.

## 2. Lifted cutoffs and both clocks

Choose radial \(\psi_m\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\), \(0\le\psi_m\le1\),
with
\[
 \psi_m=1\ {\rm on}\ A_m(R),\quad
 \operatorname{supp}\psi_m
 \subset\{\operatorname{dist}(y,A_m(R))<R/8\},
\tag{2.1}
\]
\[
 |\nabla\psi_m|\le C/R,\qquad |\Delta\psi_m|\le C/R^2.
\tag{2.2}
\]
The supports overlap boundedly in the lift. Define the legitimate torus
test
\[
 \Phi_m(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^3}\psi_m(\widetilde x+2\pi n).
\tag{2.2a}
\]
The sum is locally finite, smooth, nonnegative, and independent of the
chosen lift of \(x\). For every periodic integrable \(F\), unfolding gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb T^3}F\Phi_m
 =\int_{\mathbb R^3}\widetilde F\psi_m,
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb T^3}F\nabla^j\Phi_m
 =\int_{\mathbb R^3}\widetilde F\nabla^j\psi_m
 \quad(j=1,2).
\tag{2.2b}
\]
For \(m\ge3\), (1.2) pays the lifted support. For \(m=1,2\),
\[
 \operatorname{supp}\psi_m
 \subset B_{4R}\cup A_1(2R)\cup A_2(2R),
\tag{2.2c}
\]
so the inner collars are paid explicitly by the core.

Choose \(\chi^\square\) vanishing near the left endpoint of
\(I_{2R}^\square\), equal to one on \(I_R^\square\), with
\[
 |\partial_t\chi^\square|
 \le C\kappa_\square/R^2.
\tag{2.3}
\]
Thus the displayed coefficient is \(C/R^2\) for the standard clock and
\(C\nu/R^2\) for the viscosity clock. The transition has width comparable
to \(3R^2/\kappa_\square\); no endpoint value is used.
For suitable-weak testing, add a terminal cutoff equal to one up to
\(\tau<t_0\), apply the inequality at \(\tau\), and let
\(\tau\uparrow t_0\). Constants may depend on \(\nu\) and the clock; the
clocks are not interchanged.

## 3. Summed suitable local energy inequality

Suitability, with viscosity displayed, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int |u(t)|^2\phi(t)+2\nu\int_{-\infty}^t\!\int|\nabla u|^2\phi
 \le{}&\int_{-\infty}^t\!\int |u|^2
   (\partial_t\phi+\nu\Delta\phi)\\
 &+\int_{-\infty}^t\!\int (|u|^2+2p)u\cdot\nabla\phi .
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Use \(\phi=\chi^\square\Phi_m\), take the essential supremum over
\(I_R^\square\), multiply by \(\gamma_m/R\), and sum. The left side
dominates
\[
 \mathcal U_{\rm ext}^{\infty,\square}
 +2\mathcal D_{\rm ext}^{\square}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
First sum over \(1\le m\le M\). The time and Laplacian rows are bounded by
\[
 C(\kappa_\square+\nu)R^{-3}\sum_m\gamma_m
 \int_{I_{2R}^\square}\!\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi_m}|u|^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]
The pressure flux is gauge invariant:
\[
 \int_{\mathbb T^3}c(t)u\cdot\nabla\Phi_m
 =-c(t)\int_{\mathbb T^3}\Phi_m\nabla\cdot u=0.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Thus use \(p-c_{2R}(t)\). Since
\[
 |p-c_{2R}|\,|u|
 \le C(|p-c_{2R}|^{3/2}+|u|^3),
\tag{3.5}
\]
unfolding shows that the flux is bounded by
\[
 CR^{-2}\sum_m\gamma_m
 \int_{I_{2R}^\square}\!\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi_m}
 (|u|^3+|p-c_{2R}|^{3/2}).
\tag{3.6}
\]
All right-hand sides are nonnegative after this majorization. Fatou and
monotone convergence therefore license \(M\to\infty\); no infinite test
function is inserted into the local energy inequality.

### Lemma 3.1 — lower-order \(L^2\) row

Let
\[
 S_q=\sum_m\gamma_m\int_{I_{2R}^\square}
       \int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi_m}|u|^q.
\tag{3.6a}
\]
Weighted Hölder gives
\[
 S_2\le S_3^{2/3}
 \left(\sum_m\gamma_m|I_{2R}^\square|
 |\operatorname{supp}\psi_m|\right)^{1/3}.
\tag{3.7}
\]
Since \(|\operatorname{supp}\psi_m|\le C2^{3m}R^3\) and
\(\sum_m2^{3m}\gamma_m(\theta)\le C\) uniformly on \(0<\theta\le1\),
the last factor is at most
\(C\kappa_\square^{-1/3}R^{5/3}\). Hence
\[
 \boxed{R^{-3}S_2
 \le C\kappa_\square^{-1/3}(R^{-2}S_3)^{2/3}.}
\tag{3.8}
\]
After multiplication by the coefficient in (3.3), the exact clock factor
is \((\kappa_\square+\nu)\kappa_\square^{-1/3}\): it is
\(1+\nu\) for the standard clock and \(2\nu^{2/3}\) for the
viscosity clock.

For completeness, the uniform sum follows without a compactness shortcut.
The first three summands have a finite supremum in \(0<\theta\le1\). For
\(m\ge4\), \(4^{m-1}\ge64\), so each summand is maximal at \(\theta=1\),
and the ratio of consecutive majorants is
\[
 8\exp[-3\,4^{m-1}/32]\le8e^{-6}<1/2.
\tag{3.8a}
\]
The remaining tail is therefore geometric.

### Lemma 3.2 — cubic and pressure rows

For \(m\ge3\), (1.1)--(1.2) and bounded overlap give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &R^{-2}\sum_{m\ge3}\gamma_m
 \int_{I_{2R}^\square}\!\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi_m}
 (|u|^3+|p-c_{2R}|^{3/2})\\
 &\qquad\le C\mathcal G_{u,p}^\square(z_0,2R;\theta).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3.9}
\]
For \(m=1,2\), the parts outside \(B_{4R}\) are paid by the first two
\(2R\)-annuli. The velocity flux inside \(B_{4R}\), and the larger ball
needed for its local pressure, are paid by
\[
 R^{-2}\int_{I_{2R}^\square}\!\int_{B_{8R}}|u|^3
 \le C_{\nu,\square}\mathcal E^\square(z_0,8R)^{3/2}.
\tag{3.10}
\]
Here \(\sup_{0<\theta\le1}(\gamma_1+\gamma_2)<\infty\), so the two core
weights are absorbed into the universal constant.
Use the R0.73X pressure split
\[
 p-c_{2R}=p_{2R}^{\rm loc}
 +h_{2R}-(h_{2R})_{B_{4R}}\quad{\rm on}\ B_{4R}.
\tag{3.11}
\]
Calderón--Zygmund bounds the local part on \(B_{4R}\) by the
\(|u|^3\) integral on \(B_{8R}\), which is exactly (3.10). The harmonic
part is paid by
\[
 (2R)^{-2}\int_{I_{2R}^\square}\!\int_{B_{4R}}
 |h_{2R}-(h_{2R})_{B_{4R}}|^{3/2}
 \le C\mathcal H_u^\square(z_0,2R).
\tag{3.12}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &R^{-2}\sum_m\gamma_m
 \int_{I_{2R}^\square}\!\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi_m}
 (|u|^3+|p-c_{2R}|^{3/2})\\
 &\quad\le C_{\nu,\square}
 [\mathcal E^\square(z_0,8R)^{3/2}
 +\mathcal A_{\rm ext}^\square(z_0,2R;\theta)].
 \end{aligned}}
\tag{3.13}
\]
One gauge \(c_{2R}\) is used throughout.

## 4. Closure and the R0.74A observable

Put
\[
 \boxed{P^\square
 =\mathcal E^\square(z_0,8R)^{3/2}
 +\mathcal A_{\rm ext}^\square(z_0,2R;\theta).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — unconditional buffered tail closure

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal U_{\rm ext}^{\infty,\square}
 +\mathcal D_{\rm ext}^{\square}
 \le C_{\nu,\square}[(P^\square)^{2/3}+P^\square].}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, Lemma 3.1 and (3.13) bound the quadratic cutoff row by
\(C(P^\square)^{2/3}\), while (3.13) bounds the direct flux by
\(CP^\square\). The present proof retains \(+P\). Whether additional NSE
structure permits its removal for large \(P\) remains open; amplitude
multiplication is not an NSE symmetry and supplies no no-go argument.

The unchanged R0.74A four-block proof gives
\[
 \mathcal K_D^\square
 \le C\theta^{1/4}
 [\mathcal E^\square(z_0,4R)
 +\mathcal U_{\rm ext}^{\infty,\square}
 +\mathcal D_{\rm ext}^{\square}]^{3/2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Since \(\mathcal E(z_0,4R)\le C\mathcal E(z_0,8R)\le CP^{2/3}\),
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_D^\square
 \le C_{\nu,\square}\theta^{1/4}
 [P^\square+(P^\square)^{3/2}].}
\tag{4.4}
\]
If \(P^\square\le1\), then
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal U_{\rm ext}^{\infty,\square}
 +\mathcal D_{\rm ext}^{\square}\le C(P^\square)^{2/3},\qquad
 \mathcal K_D^\square\le C\theta^{1/4}P^\square.}
\tag{4.5}
\]
This is a small-payment size corollary, not absorption.

### Lemma 4.2 — the inherited pressure payment also shifts

The same doubled-radius payment controls the older exterior functional at
the target radius:
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal A_{\rm ext}^\square(z_0,R;\theta)
 \le C_{\nu,\square}P^\square.}
\tag{4.6}
\]
For the algebraic row, the exact annular identity gives
\[
 \Lambda_R(t)
 \le {1\over16R^3}\int_{A_1(R)}|\widetilde u(t)|^2
 +{1\over2}\Lambda_{2R}(t).
\tag{4.7}
\]
Raising to \(3/2\), integrating over \(I_R^\square\), and using
\(I_R^\square\subset I_{2R}^\square\) yields
\[
 \mathcal H_u^\square(z_0,R)
 \le C_{\nu,\square}\left[
  \mathcal E^\square(z_0,8R)^{3/2}
  +\mathcal H_u^\square(z_0,2R)\right].
\tag{4.8}
\]

For the Gaussian velocity row, \(A_m(R)=A_{m-1}(2R)\) for \(m\ge2\),
\(\gamma_m\le\gamma_{m-1}\), and the first annulus lies in \(B_{4R}\).
Consequently,
\[
 \mathcal G_u^\square(z_0,R;\theta)
 \le C_{\nu,\square}\left[
  \mathcal E^\square(z_0,8R)^{3/2}
  +\mathcal G_u^\square(z_0,2R;\theta)
 \right].
\tag{4.9}
\]

The pressure gauges at the two radii are not silently identified. From the
frozen definitions,
\[
 c_R-c_{2R}
 =(p-c_{2R})_{B_{2R}}-(p_R^{\rm loc})_{B_{2R}}.
\tag{4.10}
\]
On \(B_{4R}\), split \(p-c_{2R}\) into \(p_{2R}^{\rm loc}\) and the
centered harmonic part. Calderon--Zygmund, the frozen harmonic estimate at
radius \(2R\), and Jensen give
\[
 R\int_{I_R^\square}|c_R-c_{2R}|^{3/2}\,dt
 \le C_{\nu,\square}\left[
  \mathcal E^\square(z_0,8R)^{3/2}
  +\mathcal H_u^\square(z_0,2R)\right].
\tag{4.11}
\]
Finally,
\[
 \sum_{m\ge1}\gamma_m(\theta)|A_m(R)|
 \le CR^3\sum_{m\ge1}2^{3m}\gamma_m(\theta)\le CR^3,
\tag{4.12}
\]
uniformly for \(0<\theta\le1\). Using (4.10)--(4.12) for the gauge
difference, the first annulus as a core row, and the exact annular shift
for \(m\ge2\), gives
\[
 \mathcal G_p^\square(z_0,R;\theta)
 \le C_{\nu,\square}\left[
  \mathcal E^\square(z_0,8R)^{3/2}
  +\mathcal G_p^\square(z_0,2R;\theta)
  +\mathcal H_u^\square(z_0,2R)
 \right].
\tag{4.13}
\]
Equations (4.8), (4.9), and (4.13) prove (4.6).

### Corollary 4.3 — closure of the R0.74A pressure interface

Let \(s:I_R^\square\to(0,\theta R^2]\) be measurable and let
\(\eta_R\in W_0^{1,\infty}(B_R)\) satisfy
\(\|\nabla\eta_R\|_\infty\le C_\eta/R\). For
\(Q_s=P_s(pu)-P_sp\,P_su\), the R0.74A pressure row and (4.6) imply
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathcal K_D^\square
 +{1\over R}\int_{I_R^\square}\int_{B_R}
  |Q_{s(t)}\cdot\nabla\eta_R|\,dx\,dt\\
 &\qquad\le C_{\mathbb T^3,\theta,\nu,C_\eta,\square}
 \left[P^\square+(P^\square)^{3/2}\right].
 \end{aligned}}
\tag{4.14}
\]
In particular, the right side is \(C P^\square\) when \(P^\square\le1\).
This closes a size-payment interface only; it does not prove that
\(P^\square\) is small or absorbable.

## 5. Optional sharper Gaussian reserve

The canonical proof needs no new target. Nevertheless
\[
 \eta_m(\theta)=\theta^{-2}e^{-4^{m-1}/(6\theta)}
\tag{5.1}
\]
has a valid kernel bound. With \(a=4^{m-1}\), \(r=R^2/s\ge\theta^{-1}\),
and \(d=|\widetilde x-y|/R\), annular geometry gives
\(a^{1/2}\le d\le6a^{1/2}\), and
\[
 R^3g_s=C r^{3/2}e^{-d^2r/4},\qquad
 R^4|\nabla g_s|=C d r^{5/2}e^{-d^2r/4}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
For \(q=r\theta\ge1\),
\[
 q/4-1/6\ge q/12,
\tag{5.3}
\]
Put \(X=aq/\theta\). After division by \(\eta_m\), the heat prefactor is
bounded by \(CX^{3/2}e^{-X/12}\), and the gradient prefactor by
\(CX^{5/2}e^{-X/12}\); here the powers of \(\theta\) and \(a\) left by
the substitution are at most one. Thus the exponential reserve uniformly
absorbs \(d\), \(r^{5/2}\), and \(\theta^{-1/2}\), proving
\[
 R^3g_s+R^4|\nabla g_s|\le C\eta_m.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Also, for fixed \(N\),
\[
 (1+2^m)^N\eta_m\le C_N\gamma_{m+1},\qquad
 \eta_m/\gamma_{m+1}=e^{-4^{m-1}/(24\theta)}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
This must be derived from the exact \(1/4\) exponent, not the coarsened
R0.73X \(1/8\) derivative bound. It is **PROVED** but unnecessary above.

## 6. Exact same-window no-go

Let
\[
 u_N(t,x)=A e^{-\nu N^2(t-t_-)}\sin(Nx_2)e_1,\qquad p_N=0.
\tag{6.1}
\]
This is a smooth exact periodic NSE solution. Fix
\(A\ne0,R,\theta,\nu\) and a positive window length beginning at \(t_-\),
and let the integer \(N\to\infty\). Then
\[
 \mathcal U_{\rm ext}^{\infty}\asymp A^2,\qquad
 \mathcal D_{\rm ext}\asymp A^2,
\tag{6.2}
\]
but
\[
 \mathcal G_{u,p}=O(A^3N^{-2}),\qquad
 \mathcal H_u=O(A^3N^{-2}).
\tag{6.3}
\]
The weighted spatial coefficients tend to strictly positive constants by
Riemann--Lebesgue, applied to the integrable Gaussian annular weight and to
the algebraic \(\Lambda_R\) weight. The time integrals of the cubic rows
are \(O(N^{-2})\), while the factor \(\nu N^2\) in the dissipation row
cancels its \(O(N^{-2})\) time integral. Hence a same-window integrated
cubic payment alone cannot control the quadratic endpoint uniformly in
frequency. A buffer, an initial annular \(L^2\) row, or another quadratic
payment is necessary for this argument. This does not prove that every
possible closure must use a buffer, and it does not contradict Theorem 4.1,
whose payment sees the earlier amplitude and retains local energy.
Arbitrary energy fields have the stronger R0.74A packet and spike
obstructions.

## 7. Audit ledger

### PROVED

1. Original-\(\gamma\) targets at \(R\) are shift-stable against the
   canonical \(2R\) exterior payment.
2. Both clocks admit the strict buffered cutoff.
3. Weighted Hölder pays the \(R^{-3}L^2\) row.
4. The first two shells, indices, gauge, local pressure, and harmonic pressure
   are included; local pressure requires \(\mathcal E(z_0,8R)\).
5. Equations (4.2), (4.4), and (4.5) follow.
6. The target-radius pressure payment (4.6) and combined interface (4.14)
   follow from the same doubled-radius payment.
7. The optional sharper-\(\eta\) bounds are valid.
8. The exact shear proves the same-window cubic-only no-go, but not a
   universal buffer no-go.

### FINITE

All tails are finite at fixed positive scale under the stated assumptions.
Finiteness is not smallness.

### OPEN

1. Removal of \(+P\) from (4.2) for arbitrary large payment.
2. Absorption or scale-uniform smallness of \(P\).
3. Weak stability and lower semicontinuity.
4. A lower bound after quotienting the first-jet near-kernel.
5. Any epsilon-regularity or regularity consequence.

### NOT CLAY

This buffered positive-scale estimate does not resolve three-dimensional
Navier--Stokes regularity.
