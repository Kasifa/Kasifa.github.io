# R0.74Z -- cancellation-cell gate for the two-coordinate route

## 0. Decision and exact boundary

R0.74Y leaves one explicit construction target: cancel the outer packet on
its target spacetime box by an exponentially accurate passive correction,
but leave the same packet's adjacent-inward remote tail available as a
second clock coordinate.  This note tests that target rather than changing
its exponent ledger.

There are two different conclusions, depending on the time quantifier at
the remote strip.

1. **A remote tail retained on an \(R^3\)-length spacetime tube is not a
   W-kinetic payment escape.**  The remote shell itself lies one dyadic level inward.
   Its exterior weight at scale \(2R\) is the fourth root of its clock
   weight.  Spatial Hölder on that tube gives an exact cubic coercivity
   estimate.  For the rational parameters of R0.74Y, its exponential gap is

   \[
   \Delta_{\rm rem}
   :=\frac5{24}c_\gamma-\frac\rho6
   =\frac{64279}{238140000}>0.
   \tag{Z.1}
   \]

  Thus the W-type remote kinetic witness is exponentially smaller than
  the two-thirds power of the payment generated by the same persistent
  remote field.  Cancelling the outer target lobe does not remove this
   payment.  Since Y.57 needs both remote coordinates, failure already at
   the outer packet's remote coordinate is sufficient; no adverse estimate
   for the inner remote strip is needed.

2. **The literal endpoint-only version is not closed by that argument.**
   Smoothness gives some persistence interval, but not a uniform
   \(R^3\)-interval.  A corrector with sufficiently violent temporal
   concentration could in principle retain the remote field at one time
   and cancel it for almost every neighboring time.  Proving that this
   necessarily enlarges the local-energy or exterior payment is a new
   quantitative proposition.  It is not a consequence of qualitative
   analyticity or unique continuation alone.

Coefficient-tame, fixed-complexity \(R\)-width Gaussian translates,
fixed-order finite-difference/Hermite cells, and comparable-age time-offset
packets satisfy the time-scale calculation behind the first regime when
they obey the moving-frame envelope (Z.22) and endpoint condition (Z.23).
Polynomially growing Hermite order has the same conditional implication
when its coefficient condition number is subexponential on the \(L^2\)
scale.  Completing this implication for any chosen cell still requires the
moving-strip all-winding uniformity stated below.  Within this model, a
possible endpoint-only kinetic escape would require exponential temporal
complexity, quantified below.

Accordingly the gate verdict is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\textbf{TIME-TAME W-KINETIC ESCAPE: CONDITIONALLY BLOCKED;}\\
\textbf{FULL-CLOCK Y.57 CANCELLATION CELL: OPEN;}\\
\textbf{ARBITRARY FINITE ENDPOINT-FOCUSED FAMILY: OPEN;}\\
\textbf{NO PAYMENT-COMPATIBLE CELL IS CONSTRUCTED.}
\end{gathered}}
\tag{Z.2}
\]

The word ``finite'' is important.  A fixed number of correctors and a
number which is merely finite for each \(R\), but grows like
\(\exp(cL^2)\), are analytically different hypotheses.  This note does not
identify them.

No assertion below promotes a strip estimate to a whole-shell upper bound,
and no assertion concerns arbitrary suitable weak solutions.  This is not
a regularity or singularity result.  \(\mathbf{NOT\ CLAY}\).

## 5. Two-center and finite multi-center Gaussian attempts

### 5.1 The identical negative copy

Adding a coefficient \(-1\) copy of the outer primary is allowed by the
linear passive equation and cancels it exactly on the target box.  It also
cancels it exactly on the remote strip and at every other point and time.
Thus it destroys the desired second coordinate.  Inversion pairing does
not change this conclusion.

### 5.2 One displaced restoring center

The next natural ansatz is

\[
 C=-G_2+H_s,
 \tag{Z.26}
\]

where \(H_s\) is meant to restore the remote tail while remaining small at
the target.  In the constant-platform free comparator, with normalized
vertical variable \(\eta=(x_3-h_2)/R\), equal heat age
\(\mathsf a_0\), and a center displaced by \(sR\),

\[
 \frac{e^{-(\eta-s)^2/(4\mathsf a_0)}}
      {e^{-\eta^2/(4\mathsf a_0)}}
 =\exp\left(\frac{2\eta s-s^2}{4\mathsf a_0}\right).
 \tag{Z.27}
\]

Choosing \(s=-dL+O(1)\) and normalizing this ratio to one at one remote
point makes its target value exponentially small with rate

\[
 \zeta_{\rm 2c}
 =\frac{d^2}{2\mathsf a_0}
 =\frac{49}{134144},
 \qquad
 \zeta_{\rm 2c}-\frac1{5000}
 =\frac{13857}{83840000}>0.
 \tag{Z.28}
\]

Thus a two-center interpolation works at the level of two selected
points.  It does **not** solve the required strip problem.  Its logarithmic
slope is

\[
 \partial_\eta\log(H_s/G_2)=\frac{s}{2\mathsf a_0}
 =-\Theta(L),
 \tag{Z.29}
\]

so across a fixed-width remote strip the ratio changes by
\(e^{\Theta(L)}\), rather than being \(1+o(1)\).  Re-evolving the displaced
vertical datum under the common shear, as required by (Z.9), changes the
free formula by the already controlled platform error but does not remove
this leading log-slope.

Even if the remote requirement is weakened to one small substrip or one
point, \(H_s\) is an ordinary \(R\)-width packet.  Its restored remote
field persists on an \(R^3\) interval and falls under (Z.16).  Its own
canonical lobe provides an additional nonnegative cubic-payment witness,
not a cancellation of the remote payment.

### 5.3 More centers

For equal-age free Gaussians, division by the primary turns an \(M\)-center
restoring field into the exponential polynomial

\[
 \frac{H(\eta)}{G_2(\eta)}
 =\sum_{j=1}^M a_j
   \exp\left(\frac{2\eta s_j-s_j^2}{4\mathsf a_0}\right).
 \tag{Z.30}
\]

Additional centers can flatten more derivatives at the remote interval.
Equation (Z.30) alone does not give a uniform no-go when \(M\), the
condition number of the coefficients, and the separations may all depend
arbitrarily on \(R\).  Claiming otherwise would require a quantitative
Remez, observability, or unique-continuation estimate with those parameters
visible.

For fixed \(M\), or more generally for a time-tame family satisfying
(Z.22), such an estimate is unnecessary: even a hypothetically successful
spatial interpolation is blocked by (Z.25).  If one displaced center is
exposed on its own canonical lobe after all coefficients are inserted,
the same Hölder proof applied there gives an additional exact lobe-payment
obstruction.  The remaining possibility is an ill-conditioned cancellation
network which hides every individual lobe and is sharply focused in time;
that is precisely the endpoint-only open branch, not a completed
multi-center construction.

### 5.4 What analyticity does and does not prove

For any explicitly chosen finite heat-kernel/derivative-heat subclass whose
positive-time spatial profile has been proved real analytic, exact vanishing
of the total residual on a nonempty target box forces exact vanishing on the
connected spatial chart, and then the desired nonzero remote endpoint cannot
remain.  This is a conditional structural observation for the present
variable-shear construction: a uniform analytic-smoothing statement for
every re-evolved same-\(b\) cell class has not been supplied here, so this
observation is not used as a gate theorem.

Y.57 asks only for \(e^{-\zeta L^2}\) smallness.  Propagating that
quantitative smallness across normalized distance \(dL\) requires a
doubling-frequency, coefficient-condition, or global norm bound.  With no
such bound, the propagation constant may itself be \(e^{CL^2}\), and its
sign relative to the small value \(\zeta=1/5000\) is unknown.  Qualitative
analyticity and unique continuation therefore do not close the
endpoint-only, exponentially ill-conditioned branch.  Equations
(Z.16)--(Z.25) close the time-tame branch without assuming such a
propagation theorem.

## 6. Hermite and high-order finite-difference attempts

A vertical finite difference of separately re-evolved initial translates
is an exact passive corrector.  Replacing it after evolution by
\(\partial_3^nG\) is not exact because of (Z.9).  On the positive shear
platform its central free comparator nevertheless has the expected
Gaussian-times-polynomial form, with all noncentral windings retained as
errors.

The elementary point-interpolation model is

\[
 R_N(\eta)=\left(-\frac{\eta}{dL}\right)^N.
 \tag{Z.31}
\]

It is one at the remote center \(\eta=-dL\), while on the target interval
\(|\eta|\le1\),

\[
 |R_N(\eta)|\le(dL)^{-N}.
 \tag{Z.32}
\]

A fixed order gives only \(e^{-O(\log L)}\), not the desired
\(e^{-\zeta L^2}\).  This model needs at least

\[
 N\ge\frac{\zeta L^2}{\log(dL)}.
 \tag{Z.33}
\]

Flattening a whole remote interval rather than one point requires further
conditions, but still suggests polynomial, not \(R^{-1}\), complexity.
This is a spatial approximation heuristic, not a proved common-shear
construction.

For a normalized Hermite mode, the heat-generator contribution grows at
most polynomially in the order.  Thus an order satisfying

\[
 \log N=o(L^2)
 \tag{Z.34}
\]

remains time-tame **provided** the coefficient condition number and the
total normalized profile envelope are also \(e^{o(L^2)}\); then the
heat-generator factor is absorbed in \(e^{o(L^2)}\), and \(NR\to0\).
Therefore, if such a coefficient-tame Hermite or finite-difference cell
succeeds spatially and preserves the remote endpoint, it also preserves it
for an \(R^3\) tube and is blocked by (Z.25).  Polynomial order by itself
does not control an exponentially ill-conditioned finite-difference
representation.

High Hermite derivatives have additional side lobes, and backward recovery
of a large terminal high-order mode amplifies earlier amplitudes.  These
facts strongly suggest further cubic and local-energy payment.  They are
not used as a theorem here: a general lower bound must allow cancellations
among all high-order lobes, and no such bound is presently proved.

## 7. Time-offset attempts and the required escape complexity

There are three distinct notions of a time offset.

1. A literal translate of an evolved packet fails the exact PDE by
   (Z.10).
2. Changing the horizontal entrance point is exact, but it only changes
   the crossing time.  Under the positive common platform the speed is
   \(\asymp R^{-2}\); an \(R\)-width packet therefore has an
   \(R^3\)-length passage and is subject to (Z.16).
3. Changing the initial heat width and re-evolving from zero is exact.  If
   all initial and terminal heat ages remain comparable to \(R^2\), the
   normalized generator is still \(R^{-2}\) up to powers of \(L\), so the
   family is time-tame.

To evade (Z.16), the normalized remote residence must satisfy (Z.19).
Let \(\mathcal N_L\) denote the normalized time-derivative/condition-number
factor multiplying the right side of (Z.22).  The persistence estimate
behind (Z.24) gives, at exponential accuracy,

\[
 \theta_L\gtrsim
 \min\left\{1,
 \frac{\exp[(\rho/4-\beta)L^2+o(L^2)]}{\mathcal N_L}
 \right\}.
 \tag{Z.35}
\]

Combining (Z.19), (Z.21), and (Z.35) shows, at exponential accuracy and
within this moving-frame derivative/condition-number model, that an
endpoint-focused kinetic escape requires at least

\[
 \boxed{
 \log\mathcal N_L
 \ge
 \left(\frac\rho4-\beta+\kappa_*+o(1)\right)L^2
 =\left(\frac{476239}{1064835072}+o(1)\right)L^2.}
 \tag{Z.36}
\]

In particular, fixed \(M\), polynomial \(M\), and the polynomial Hermite
order in (Z.33), all with polynomially bounded conditioning, lie strictly
below the necessary exponential rate and cannot reach the required temporal
concentration.  Exponentially large coefficients are part of
\(\mathcal N_L\), not a free exception to (Z.36).  Equality in the
exponential coefficient in (Z.36) is a critical layer and is not resolved by
this rate calculation alone.

A width \(\varepsilon\ll R\) packet could have a shorter diffusion time,
but to focus within \(\theta_LR^3\) one expects

\[
 \varepsilon^2\lesssim\theta_LR^3.
 \tag{Z.37}
\]

Transporting a nontrivial mode of that width from the beginning of the
Version-M \(I_{8R}\) interval to the endpoint incurs backward heat
amplification on the scale
\(\exp(cR^2/\varepsilon^2)\).  For an isolated Fourier or Hermite mode this
is exact and makes the earlier local-energy/payment enormous.  Extending
it to an arbitrary ill-conditioned finite sum requires a spectral
observability estimate and is recorded as open; it is not silently inferred
from (Z.37).

## 8. Inversion, periodic windings, and corrector lobes

All admissible cells in this note use the periodic heat kernel at the data
level and are re-evolved by (Z.6).  Hence no winding is deleted.  In the
central chart, a noncentral winding carries the inherited
\(e^{-c/R^2}\) factor.  For fixed or subexponential-complexity cells with
coefficient logarithms \(O(L^2)\), their complete winding sum is still
negligible compared with every \(e^{-c'L^2}\) ledger term.  Periodic copies
therefore neither create the cancellation nor defeat (Z.16).

Inversion pairing is likewise exact but cannot lower a nonnegative cubic
row.  A positive corrector lobe has a mirrored negative lobe; the two
contributions to \(|u|^3\) add.  More generally:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{if the total field has a persistent kinetic floor on any corrector}\\
 \text{lobe in shell }j,\text{ then (Z.15)--(Z.16), with }
 \omega=\gamma_j,\text{ applies.}
 \end{gathered}}
 \tag{Z.38}
\]

This is the exact ``unavoidable exposed lobe'' statement.  What is not
proved is that an arbitrary ill-conditioned collection must expose one of
its individual canonical lobes.  It may try to cancel them all in the
total field.  Such a collection still has to pass the remote persistence
or temporal-concentration dichotomy.
