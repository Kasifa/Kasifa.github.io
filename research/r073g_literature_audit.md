# R0.73G primary-source literature boundary audit

**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Scope:** nonlinear instability and transition near steady or evolving shear
flows; bootstrap from an unstable linear mode or bundle; Grenier-type
approximate solutions; and the limits of a one-row linear growth theorem  
**Evidence class:** bounded primary-source audit; not an originality or
priority claim

## 1. Exact question inherited from R0.73F

R0.73F conditionally proves, on the single row
\(\gamma=1/2,\ \beta=\xi=0\), a moving finite-rank unstable bundle and the
linear lower bound

\[
 G_{1/2}(\Lambda;D)
 \ge K_1^{-1}
 \exp\!\left((\alpha+\eta)\min\{D,d_0\}|\Lambda|\right)
 \tag{1.1}
\]

for the heat-decaying profile

\[
 W(d,x)=-\frac12e^{-d}\sin x+\frac14e^{-4d}\sin2x,
 \qquad \varepsilon=|\Lambda|^{-1}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

The R0.73G question is narrower than “does linear instability imply
nonlinear instability?” in the abstract.  It is whether (1.1) can seed a
closed, unforced nonlinear Navier--Stokes bootstrap for the exact evolving
background, in a specified full perturbation space, with constants and an
exit statement uniform as \(|\Lambda|\to\infty\).

For an exact perturbation \(v\), the relevant identity is schematically

\[
 v(d)=\mathcal U_\Lambda(d,0)v_0
 -\int_0^d \mathcal U_\Lambda(d,s)
       \mathbb P(v\cdot\nabla v)(s)\,ds.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The first term is addressed only on one Fourier row by R0.73F.  The second
term couples rows.  Consequently, neither the invariant-bundle lower bound
nor frozen spectral instability alone estimates (1.3).

## 2. Source-status rule

The labels below are literal.

- **Published theorem** means that the stated result appears in a published
  paper.  It does not mean that its hypotheses hold for (1.2).
- **Corrected published theorem** means that the original theorem must be read
  together with a published corrigendum.
- **Current preprint theorem** means that a theorem is stated in the cited
  arXiv version, with no peer-reviewed publication verified in this audit.
- **Preprint heuristic/conjecture** means that a formal expansion, computation,
  or proposed saturation law is not a proved nonlinear theorem.
- **Mismatch/open** marks a missing implication, not an error in the source.

Only original articles, author manuscripts, arXiv records, and official
publisher pages are used.  “Transition threshold” is not treated as a single
universal exponent: its value depends on the domain, boundary conditions,
dimension, norm, background, and whether the result is a stability bound, an
instability construction, or both.

## 5. Primary-source notes and exact boundaries

### 5.1 Autonomous Navier--Stokes bootstrap

Susan Friedlander, Nataša Pavlović, and Roman Shvydkoy,
*Nonlinear instability for the Navier--Stokes equations*, Communications in
Mathematical Physics 264 (2006), 335--347,
[arXiv:math/0508173](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0508173),
[DOI 10.1007/s00220-006-1526-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-006-1526-7).

Theorem 2.2 starts from a steady state and a fixed autonomous generator.  Its
bootstrap uses the spectral mapping/analytic-semigroup structure and
fractional smoothing to dominate the quadratic Duhamel term in a weaker norm.
The theorem is therefore a usable template only after R0.73G establishes a
full-space nonautonomous analogue with uniform constants.  A positive frozen
eigenvalue, or even the one-row estimate (1.1), is not a substitute for those
hypotheses.

### 5.2 Grenier expansions and viscous boundary layers

Emmanuel Grenier, *On the nonlinear instability of Euler and Prandtl
equations*, Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 53 (2000),
1067--1091,
[publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/1097-0312%28200009%2953%3A9%3C1067%3A%3AAID-CPA1%3E3.0.CO%3B2-Q).

Benoît Desjardins and Emmanuel Grenier, *Linear instability implies nonlinear
instability for various types of viscous boundary layers*, Annales de
l'Institut Henri Poincaré C 20 (2003), 87--106,
[primary archive and full text](https://numdam.org/item/AIHPC_2003__20_1_87_0/).

The 2003 theorem makes the extra structure visible: A1 is an energy estimate,
A2 is an unstable frequency packet with regularity and a nondegenerate maximal
growth rate, A3 controls the frequency interaction algebra, and A4 supplies
resolvent bounds.  Iterated approximate solutions then make the residual
higher order than the growing seed.  R0.73F proves no analogue of A1--A4 for
the full Navier--Stokes perturbation, so “apply Grenier” is not yet a proof
step.

### 5.3 Unstable blocks and the corrected Euler manifold theorem

Zhiwu Lin and Chongchun Zeng, *Unstable Manifolds of Euler Equations*,
Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 66 (2013), 1803--1836,
[arXiv:1112.4525](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.4525),
[DOI 10.1002/cpa.21457](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.21457); see also the
[published corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.21521), Communications on
Pure and Applied Mathematics 67 (2014), 1215--1217.

The useful structural precedent is that the unstable object may be a
finite-dimensional subspace rather than one simple eigenvector.  The theorem
nevertheless assumes a steady Euler flow, an exponential dichotomy, Sobolev
regularity, and a quantitative gap over the Lyapunov exponent of the base-flow
trajectories.  Its Lagrangian unstable-manifold proof neither gives the
parabolic estimates in (1.3) nor transports the R0.73F bundle through the
nonlinear Fourier convolution.

### 5.4 The closest published evolving-shear comparisons

Emmanuel Grenier and Toan T. Nguyen, *\(L^\infty\) instability of Prandtl
layers*, Annals of PDE 5 (2019), article 18,
[arXiv:1803.11024](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11024),
[DOI 10.1007/s40818-019-0074-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40818-019-0074-3).

The base shear evolves by the heat equation, so this source is genuinely
nonautonomous.  The nonlinear construction, however, is a no-slip half-plane
boundary-layer result based on a simple unstable Rayleigh mode and analytic
profile.  The theorem's nearby solutions carry an arbitrarily small forcing;
it therefore cannot be cited as an unforced nonlinear instability theorem for
the periodic profile (1.2).

Jacob Bedrossian, Vlad Vicol, and Fei Wang, *The Sobolev stability threshold
for 2D shear flows near Couette*, Journal of Nonlinear Science 28 (2018),
2051--2075,
[arXiv:1604.01831](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01831),
[DOI 10.1007/s00332-016-9330-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00332-016-9330-9).

This theorem treats an exact heat-decaying shear in the full nonlinear
equation, but only in a sufficiently small near-Couette stability regime on
\(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R\).  Its \(\nu^{1/2}\) scale is a sufficient
Sobolev stability scale for that setting, not a universal transition threshold
and not an instability amplitude for (1.2).

### 5.5 Threshold results are setting-dependent

Jacob Bedrossian, Pierre Germain, and Nader Masmoudi, *On the stability
threshold for the 3D Couette flow in Sobolev regularity*, Annals of Mathematics
185 (2017), 541--608,
[official journal page](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2017/185-2/p04),
[arXiv:1511.01373](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01373).

The theorem gives a sufficient high-regularity stability scale
\(O(\mathrm{Re}^{-3/2})\) for 3D Couette and tracks lift-up, streak formation,
and enhanced dissipation.  It does not prove that every larger perturbation is
unstable.  Importing its exponent into R0.73G would conflate dimension,
geometry, norm, and mechanism.

Jacob Bedrossian, Siming He, Sameer Iyer, Linfeng Li, and Fei Wang, *Stability
threshold of close-to-Couette shear flows with no-slip boundary conditions in
2D*,
[arXiv:2510.16378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16378) (submitted 2025;
**current preprint** in this audit).

The preprint treats an exact heat-evolving channel shear and uses a frozen-time
scheme, but proves stability near Couette under wall compatibility conditions.
It is relevant to nonlinear organization, not to promotion of the R0.73F
unstable block.

### 5.6 A primary-source statement of the spectral-to-nonlinear gap

Maria Colombo, Michele Dolce, Riccardo Montalto, and Paolo Ventura,
*Long-wave instability of periodic shear flows for the 2D Navier--Stokes
equations*,
[arXiv:2509.18070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18070) (2025;
**current preprint**).

The paper proves a simple unstable eigenvalue in a long-wave class for a
forced steady periodic shear.  Remark 1.2 explains that the result does not by
itself yield nonlinear instability because dominance relative to the spectrum
outside the controlled long-wave regime is unknown; existing promotion
arguments require more.  The geometry is closer to R0.73G than boundary-layer
papers, but the source itself marks the missing implication.

### 5.7 Heuristic saturation is not a theorem

Dongfen Bian and Emmanuel Grenier, *Onset of nonlinear instabilities in
monotonic viscous boundary layers*,
[arXiv:2206.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01318) (2022;
**preprint heuristic/conjecture**).

The cubic interaction calculation motivates an \(O(\nu^{1/4})\) saturation
scenario in its boundary-layer setting.  The proposed saturation law is not a
proved nonlinear theorem.  It supplies neither a threshold nor a coefficient
for the periodic, heat-decaying family (1.2).

## 6. What the literature licenses for R0.73G

The sources support the following bounded conclusions.

1. **R0.73F is a linear seed, not a nonlinear theorem.**  The quadratic term
   in (1.3) generates sums of Fourier rows.  The permitted row is therefore
   not a nonlinear invariant subsystem.
2. **A simple eigenline is not logically necessary.**  Lin--Zeng and the
   R0.73F backward estimate make an unstable block a viable linear seed, but
   a full-space nonlinear construction still has to be supplied.
3. **Dominant growth and upper bounds matter.**  A lower bound on one seed
   does not bound the propagator acting on the nonlinear remainder.  The
   long-wave preprint gives a direct example of authors declining the
   spectral-to-nonlinear step for this reason.
4. **A time-dependent heat background is not, by itself, prohibitive.**
   Published and current-preprint results control such backgrounds in other
   regimes.  Their estimates depend strongly on the domain, boundary layer,
   mixing, analyticity, and norm.
5. **“Transition” requires an explicit definition.**  An exit from a small
   ball in one norm is nonlinear instability.  It is not automatically
   turbulence, a sharp threshold, energy cascade, or finite-time singularity.

No cited theorem closes R0.73G by substitution.

## 7. Minimum proof obligations after this audit

Before a nonlinear-instability statement is admissible, a new proof must at
least provide all of the following.

1. **Exact nonlinear background and perturbation equation.**  Verify in the
   original variables that the heat-decaying shear is an exact unforced
   Navier--Stokes solution on the stated domain and derive the Leray-projected
   perturbation equation with all scaling factors.
2. **Full phase space.**  Choose a divergence-free, real-valued Sobolev or
   analytic space in which the local solution, linear evolution, and bilinear
   map are simultaneously controlled.  Include the conjugate row required by
   real-valuedness.
3. **Admissible seed.**  Lift a vector in the R0.73F moving/frozen top block to
   an actual sufficiently regular perturbation.  Record any
   \(\varepsilon\)-dependence of this seed and normalize it in the same norm
   used by the exit statement.
4. **Linear upper estimates in the nonlinear norm.**  Prove full-space
   evolution bounds, with explicit \(|\Lambda|\)-dependence, for every row
   generated before exit.  The one-row lower estimate (1.1) is insufficient.
5. **Bilinear convolution estimate.**  Bound
   \(\mathbb P(v\cdot\nabla v)\) in the selected norm, including derivative
   loss, row summation, zero modes, and the dependence of constants on
   \(\varepsilon\) and \(|\Lambda|\).
6. **Correctors or a bootstrap remainder.**  Either construct enough nonlinear
   harmonics to make the residual negligible, as in Grenier-type expansions,
   or close a direct Duhamel inequality.  State which estimate prevents the
   remainder from sharing or exceeding the seed's growth.
7. **Uniform exit calculation.**  Select the initial amplitude, exit time, and
   target radius so that local existence persists, the linear component has a
   non-cancelling lower bound, and the nonlinear remainder is strictly
   smaller, all uniformly in the intended parameter range.
8. **Conclusion at the proved strength.**  State the output as instability in
   one named pair of norms unless separate upper and lower results establish a
   sharp threshold.  Do not infer turbulence or a 3D regularity consequence.

The first efficient R0.73G gate is therefore not another one-row eigenvalue
calculation.  It is a scaling audit of (1.3): determine whether a full-space
linear upper bound and one bilinear estimate leave any nonempty amplitude/time
window in which the R0.73F lower bound dominates the nonlinear remainder.

## 8. Bounded non-collision statement; no priority claim

The primary-source search was bounded through **2026-08-30** to the named
authors and to direct searches for nonlinear instability, unstable-mode
bootstrap, evolving or decaying shear, Grenier expansions, Couette transition
thresholds, and periodic long-wave instability.

Within that bounded search, no primary theorem was located that simultaneously
has all five features below:

1. the exact fully periodic heat-decaying two-harmonic shear (1.2);
2. the singular family \(\varepsilon=|\Lambda|^{-1}\) with background
   amplitude \(|\Lambda|\);
3. the R0.73F moving unstable block and fixed-physical-window gain
   \(\exp(c|\Lambda|)\);
4. an unforced, full nonlinear Navier--Stokes bootstrap closing every generated
   Fourier interaction in a stated topology; and
5. a quantitative exit-time or threshold conclusion uniform in
   \(|\Lambda|\).

This is a **bounded non-collision finding**, not proof of novelty.  **No
priority claim is made.**  A submission-level novelty statement would require
broader MathSciNet and zbMATH searches, backward and forward citation tracing,
coverage of adjacent hydrodynamic-stability terminology, and independent
expert review.
