## 2. Yang 2022

### 2.1 Admissibility

**Claim tested.** Yang defines an admissible skewed cylinder by a condition of
the form

\[
 \varepsilon^2\fint_{Q_\varepsilon}\mathcal M(|\nabla u|)<\eta.
\]

**Primary-source check.** Final Definition 2 uses the spatial
Hardy--Littlewood maximal function and precisely the scale-invariant factor

\[
 \varepsilon^2\fint_{Q_\varepsilon}\mathcal M(\nabla u)<\eta.
\]

The paper's maximal function already takes the magnitude inside its definition,
so writing \(\mathcal M(|\nabla u|)\) is harmless notation rather than a change of
hypothesis.

**Verdict: PASS.**

### 2.2 Comparable trajectories and the factor \(9\)

**Claim tested.** Intersecting admissible cylinders at comparable scales have
controlled mollified trajectories and quantitative containment.

**Primary-source check.** In the final journal version, Lemma 6 assumes
\(\varepsilon_\beta<2\varepsilon_\alpha\), admissibility of both cylinders, and a
point in their intersection. It proves, throughout the common lifespan,

\[
 \left|X_{\varepsilon_\beta}(t_*,x_*;t)
       -X_{\varepsilon_\alpha}(t_*,x_*;t)\right|
 \le \varepsilon_\alpha.
\]

Final Proposition 7 then proves

\[
 B_\beta(t)\subset 9B_\alpha(t)
\]

for times in the common lifespan, equivalently

\[
 Q_\beta\cap\{S_\alpha<t<T_\alpha\}\subset 9Q_\alpha.
\]

It does not assert containment of the portions of \(Q_\beta\) lying before or
after the lifespan of \(Q_\alpha\).

**Verdict: PASS.** The bound boundary and report say "during the common
lifespan" and preserve the restricted containment above.

### 2.3 Same-centre neighboring-scale \(3/4\)-containment

**Claim tested.** A stronger admissibility threshold on a larger, same-centre
cylinder gives \(3/4\)-containment for the neighboring scale band.

**Primary-source check.** In the proof of final Proposition 11, if the larger
cylinder is \(4^{-d-1}\eta\)-admissible and

\[
 \frac{\varepsilon_\alpha}{4}
 \le \varepsilon_\beta
 \le \frac{\varepsilon_\alpha}{2},
\]

then the centre trajectories remain within
\(\varepsilon_\alpha/4\), and

\[
 Q_{\varepsilon_\beta}(t_0,x_0)
 \subset \frac34 Q_{\varepsilon_\alpha}(t_0,x_0).
\]

**Verdict: PASS.** The band, same-centre condition, and strengthened threshold
are essential; this is not arbitrary-scale monotonicity.

### 2.4 Almost-everywhere admissibility is not prescribed-point admissibility

**Claim tested.** Yang's eventual-admissibility result does not guarantee
admissibility at a preassigned possible singular point.

**Primary-source check.** Under \(\operatorname{div}u=0\) and
\(\mathcal M(\nabla u)\in L^p\), final Proposition 11 proves that for almost every
spacetime point every sufficiently small cylinder is admissible and its
spacetime diameter tends to zero.

An almost-everywhere conclusion cannot be evaluated at an arbitrarily
preassigned point unless that point is separately shown to avoid the exceptional
null set. The theorem neither says that a singular point is exceptional nor
that it is nonexceptional.

**Verdict: PASS.** This is a quantifier distinction, not a counterexample and
not an impossibility theorem.

### 2.5 Citation-number verification

The final EMS paper numbers the relevant results as Lemma 6, Proposition 7,
and Proposition 11. The arXiv v2 HTML numbers the corresponding results as
Lemma 9, Proposition 10, and Proposition 14. The bound boundary and report use
this correct crosswalk, including the common-lifespan qualifier. No mismatched
Yang cross-reference remains in the bound sources.

### 2.6 Non-novelty consequence

The 2022 paper is a conclusive prior source for the admissibility definition,
comparable-scale trajectory control, factor-\(9\) shared-lifespan containment,
and the stated neighboring-band \(3/4\)-containment. R0.74J therefore cannot
claim any of those components alone as new.

## 3. Vasseur--Yang 2021

### 3.1 Suitable-weak recentering and local theorem

**Claim tested.** Vasseur--Yang already use mollified-flow recentering, local
vorticity regularity, and the skewed-cylinder maximal function in suitable-weak
three-dimensional Navier--Stokes theory.

**Primary-source check.** Their introduction writes the Galilean/parabolic
blow-up along a trajectory \(X(t)\). They choose \(X\) from the mollified flow so
that the rescaled velocity has zero mollified spatial mean. Their Theorem 1.3
is a local theorem for suitable weak solutions: a mixed smallness condition on
\(\nabla u\) and \(\omega=\operatorname{curl}u\), together with the zero-mean
normalization, yields \(L^\infty\) bounds for all spatial derivatives of
\(\omega\) on smaller cylinders. No pressure norm occurs in that smallness
hypothesis.

Section 2.1 quotes the admissible skewed-cylinder maximal function; the
local-to-global argument applies the local theorem on those recentered
cylinders. Theorem 1.1 includes suitable weak solutions for the derivative
level \(n=1\), and Corollary 1.2 gives

\[
 \nabla^2u\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{4/3,q},\qquad q>4/3.
\]

The official journal abstract independently states the suitable-weak setting,
the pressure-free local vorticity study, the maximal-function local-to-global
step, and this Lorentz improvement.

**Verdict: PASS.**

### 3.2 Non-novelty consequence

Moving mollified cylinders, recentering to remove the local mean velocity, and
their use in suitable-weak derivative estimates were all present by 2021.
Those ingredients alone are not a new R0.74J framework.

This source does not define the R0.74J complete-payment functional or prove an
exact-family lower bound of order \(B_j^3R_j^3\).

## 4. Lei--Ren 2024

### 4.1 Logarithmic CKN gauge

**Claim tested.** Lei--Ren prove

\[
 \mathcal P^f(\mathcal S[v])=0,
 \qquad f(r)=r|\log r|.
\]

**Primary-source check.** This is exactly Theorem A, equation (1.13), for a
local suitable weak solution in \(Q(1)\). Remark 7 records a slightly stronger
optional gauge and a forced extension under an additional integrability
hypothesis; neither is needed for the boundary statement.

**Verdict: PASS.**

### 4.2 Nonoverlapping hollow dissipation layers

**Claim tested.** A pigeonhole argument on nonoverlapping hollow dissipation
layers is a key input.

**Primary-source check.** Equation (1.14) introduces

\[
 E_k=\int_{U_k\setminus U_{k+1}}|g|,
 \qquad \sum_kE_k\le\int_\Omega|g|,
\]

and takes \(g=|\nabla v|^2\). The proof of Theorem A implements the construction
in equations (3.24)--(3.26). The proof of Theorem B has the analogous disjoint
layers in equations (4.14)--(4.16). In the axisymmetric specialization,
equation (5.1) writes literal radial hollow annuli

\[
 E_k'=\int_{R_k<\varrho<R_{k+1}}|\nabla v|^2.
\]

**Verdict: PASS.** For the general, non-axisymmetric results, "hollow layers"
means differences of nested spacetime sets; equation (5.1)'s simple radial
annuli are an axisymmetric specialization.

### 4.3 Meaning of "quantitative regular intervals"

Theorem B gives a quantitatively long interval in one spatial coordinate and
regularity in the corresponding spacetime strip. Proposition 9 separately
gives a regular time epoch. The boundary phrase is correct but should identify
which of these two statements is intended when used later.

Lei--Ren also prove an axisymmetric one-point criterion in Theorem D under an
additional logarithmically weighted dissipation hypothesis. Therefore the
paper should not be summarized as containing no prescribed-point result at all.

### 4.4 Prescribed moving core

None of Theorem A, Theorem B, Proposition 9, or Theorem D states

\[
 \text{finite total energy}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{R0.74I-small normalized moving core at a prescribed point}.
\]

The hollow-layer estimate controls an annular or nested-set difference. The
R0.74I hypothesis controls a normalized moving core. The former does not
contain the latter by set inclusion, and no estimate in the inspected paper
bridges them.

**Verdict: PASS AS A FINITE SCOPE COMPARISON.** This is not a theorem that such
a bridge is impossible, and it is not evidence that no other paper contains
one.

### 4.5 Non-novelty consequence

The logarithmic singular-set gauge, hollow-layer pigeonholing, spatial regular
intervals, and regular time epochs are published mechanisms. R0.74J cannot
claim those mechanisms alone as new.

## 5. Wang--Wu--Zhou 2019

### 5.1 One-scale, pressure-free epsilon regularity

**Claim tested.** Wang--Wu--Zhou prove a one-scale epsilon-regularity theorem
for suitable weak solutions with a velocity-only smallness hypothesis; R0.74I
uses the exponent \(3\) case.

**Primary-source check.** Theorem 1.1 states that for every \(\delta>0\) there
is an epsilon threshold such that

\[
 \iint_{Q(1)}|u|^{5/2+\delta}\,dx\,dt\le\varepsilon
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 u\in L^\infty(Q(1/16)).
\]

There is no pressure-smallness norm in the hypothesis. Setting
\(\delta=1/2\) gives the exponent \(3\) used by R0.74I. The theorem shrinks the
conclusion from \(Q(1)\) to \(Q(1/16)\), and the epsilon threshold is selected
after \(\delta\) is fixed.

**Verdict: PASS.** This checks the cited source theorem, not every normalization
and containment step in the separate R0.74I application.

### 5.2 Metadata and boundary verification

The formal journal record is volume 267, issue 8, pages 4673--4704, October
2019, DOI 10.1016/j.jde.2019.05.003.

The bound boundary, report, and main theorem all identify this source as 2019
and include its velocity-only one-scale theorem. Consequently:

- **source theorem: PASS;**
- **formal publication metadata: PASS;**
- **four-source inclusion and the precise \(Q(1)\to Q(1/16)\) scope: PASS.**

### 5.3 Non-novelty consequence

The velocity-only, one-scale exponent-\(3\) epsilon criterion is not new to
R0.74I/J: it is the \(\delta=1/2\) instance of this 2019 theorem. The paper does
not define or estimate the R0.74J complete payment.

## 7. Why the checked sources do not prove R0.74J Theorem 3.2

R0.74J Theorem 3.2 concerns one frozen sequence of smooth, periodic, unforced
solutions and one internally defined nonnegative payment row. Its proof uses:

1. a profile-independent heat lower bound on a fixed box;
2. placement of that box in the fifth weighted annulus;
3. \(|u|^3\ge B_j^3|\theta_j|^3\);
4. direct integration over a time interval of length \(4R_j^2\) and a box of
   volume \(64R_j^3\).

Yang and Vasseur--Yang concern moving-cylinder geometry and derivative
estimates; Lei--Ren concerns suitable-weak singular-set size and regular
regions; Wang--Wu--Zhou gives a conditional local regularity criterion. None
has the R0.74J payment definitions, frozen family, fifth-shell weight, or
claimed lower bound. None is used as a premise of the internal integration
argument.

Therefore the literature evidence is consistent with Theorem 3.2 and does not
subsume it. This is a theorem-level noncollision within the finite corpus,
not a global literature novelty certificate.
