# R0.73I proof: inherited endpoint audit and a continuum upper action

**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Parent inputs:** R0.73F moving dichotomy and R0.73H harmonic energy  
**Evidence class:** exact operator inequalities; source stage  
**Public status:** not released

## 1. Claims proved here

For the periodic row \(\gamma=1/2\), let

\[
 W(d,x)=-\frac12e^{-d}\sin x+\frac14e^{-4d}\sin2x,
 \qquad L=-\partial_x^2+\frac14,
\]

and \(B_\varepsilon(d)=\widetilde A(d)-\varepsilon L\).  This note proves
two statements that do not require a canonical rightmost branch.

First, every endpoint selected by the R0.73F proof satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 D=d_0< D_{\rm ub}:=\frac{\sqrt{19/180}}{392}
 <\frac1{450}.}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The number \(D_{\rm ub}\) is an upper bound, not the chosen endpoint; the
R0.73F construction permits \(d_0\) to be shrunk.

Second, for every \(0\le D\le1/450\), the exact evolution generated by
\(B_\varepsilon(\varepsilon\theta)\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \|U_\varepsilon(D/\varepsilon,0)\|
 \le \exp\!\left(\frac{\Omega_H(D)}{\varepsilon}-\frac D4\right),}
 \tag{1.2}
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{
 \Omega_H(D)=\frac8{405}\left[
 \left(\frac{19}{20}+\frac{45D}{4}\right)^{3/2}
 -\left(\frac{19}{20}\right)^{3/2}
 \right].}
 \tag{1.3}
\]

This is a one-sided upper action.  It does not prove a matching lower
action, eigenvalue uniqueness, or an adiabatic theorem.

## 2. Reusing the exact \(H_d\) certificate

R0.73H proves the infinite-dimensional form inequality

\[
 H_0=-\partial_x^2+1-\frac94W_x(0)^2\ge\frac1{20}I.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

It also proves

\[
 \|W_x(d)^2-W_x(0)^2\|_\infty\le5d.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore, without rounding any constants,

\[
 H_d=-\partial_x^2+1-\frac94W_x(d)^2
 \ge h(d)I,
 \qquad h(d)=\frac1{20}-\frac{45}{4}d.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

On \(0\le d\le1/450\), \(h(d)\ge1/40>0\).

## 3. Transfer from \(\gamma=1\) to \(\gamma=1/2\)

For a periodic profile \(v\), write

\[
 E_\gamma(v)=\|v'\|_2^2+\gamma^2\|v\|_2^2.
\]

The exact inviscid numerical form is

\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle A_\gamma(d)v,v\rangle_{E_\gamma}
 =\gamma\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb T}W_x(d)v'\bar v.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

At \(\gamma=1/2\), the inequality

\[
 \left|\frac12\operatorname{Im}\int W_xv'\bar v\right|
 \le c E_{1/2}(v)
 \tag{3.2}
\]

follows if both signs of

\[
 E_{1/2}(v)
 \mathbin{\pm}\frac1{2c}\operatorname{Im}\int W_xv'\bar v
\]

are nonnegative.  Completing the square and applying the periodic unitary
gauge gives the Schrödinger operator

\[
 H_{c,d}=-\partial_x^2+\frac14-\frac1{16c^2}W_x(d)^2.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Put \(\vartheta=(36c^2)^{-1}\).  Direct coefficient comparison yields the
identity

\[
 H_{c,d}
 =\vartheta H_d+(1-\vartheta)(-\partial_x^2)
 +\left(\frac14-\vartheta\right)I.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Using (2.3) and \(-\partial_x^2\ge0\),

\[
 H_{c,d}\ge
 \left[\frac14-\vartheta(1-h(d))\right]I.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Choose the largest allowed weight

\[
 \vartheta(d)=\frac1{4(1-h(d))}.
\]

Because \(h(d)\le1/20\), this weight lies in \((0,1)\).  Equations
(3.3)--(3.5) then prove

\[
 \boxed{
 \omega_{1/2}(d)\le c_H(d)
 :=\frac13\sqrt{1-h(d)}
 =\frac13\sqrt{\frac{19}{20}+\frac{45d}{4}}.}
 \tag{3.6}
\]

At \(d=0\), this gives

\[
 a:=\sup\operatorname{Re}\sigma(\widetilde A(0))
 \le\omega_{1/2}(0)
 \le\sqrt{\frac{19}{180}}.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

## 4. The inherited endpoint is strictly below \(1/450\)

R0.73F chooses

\[
 0<b<\alpha<c<a,
 \qquad
 \nu=\min\{\alpha-b,c-\alpha\},
 \qquad K\ge1,
\]

and requires

\[
 C_A d_0<\frac{\nu}{16K^2},
 \qquad C_A=\frac{49}{4}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The two positive gaps defining \(\nu\) have sum \(c-b\), so

\[
 2\nu\le c-b<a,
 \qquad \nu<\frac a2.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Combining (3.7), (4.1), (4.2), and \(K\ge1\) gives

\[
 d_0
 <\frac{a/2}{16(49/4)}
 \le\frac{\sqrt{19/180}}{392}
 =D_{\rm ub}.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Finally,

\[
 D_{\rm ub}<\frac1{450}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{19}{180}<\left(\frac{392}{450}\right)^2.
\]

After clearing positive denominators, this is the integer inequality

\[
 19\cdot450^2<180\cdot392^2,
\]

whose two sides are \(3{,}847{,}500\) and \(27{,}659{,}520\).  Thus
R0.73H's \(D=\min\{d_0,1/450\}\) equals \(d_0\) strictly.

## 5. Evolution bound and integration

In the kinetic representation, \(L\ge I/4\).  For a classical solution of

\[
 \partial_\theta v
 =B_\varepsilon(\varepsilon\theta)v,
\]

equation (3.6) gives

\[
 \frac12\frac{d}{d\theta}\|v(\theta)\|_2^2
 \le \left[c_H(\varepsilon\theta)-\frac\varepsilon4\right]
 \|v(\theta)\|_2^2.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Density and the evolution-family construction extend the estimate to every
initial vector.  Gronwall, followed by \(s=\varepsilon\theta\), yields

\[
 \log\frac{\|v(D/\varepsilon)\|_2}{\|v(0)\|_2}
 \le\frac1\varepsilon\int_0^D c_H(s)\,ds-\frac D4.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

Since

\[
 \int\frac13\sqrt{\frac{19}{20}+\frac{45s}{4}}\,ds
 =\frac8{405}
 \left(\frac{19}{20}+\frac{45s}{4}\right)^{3/2},
\]

equations (5.2) and (1.3) prove (1.2).
