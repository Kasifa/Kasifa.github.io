# R0.72T literature audit: time-dependent enhanced dissipation at an \(A_2\) collision

**Search date:** 2026-08-28

**Status:** primary-source audit.  The sources below delimit what can be
imported and what remains unproved for the R0.72T model.

## 1. Primary sources checked

### 1.1 Mixing-to-dissipation framework

M. Coti Zelati, M. G. Delgadino, and T. M. Elgindi,
“On the Relation between Enhanced Dissipation Timescales and Mixing Rates,”
*Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics* 73 (2020), 1205--1244.

- Primary preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.03258>
- Publisher DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.21831>
- Exact point used here: Section 2.3 explicitly allows nonautonomous
  antisymmetric operators if the energy/proximity estimates hold uniformly,
  and Theorem 2.1 requires the mixing bound for every initial time.  Its
  exponent is \(q=2/(2+p)\).
- Boundary for R0.72T: inserting the proved cubic-phase mixing power
  \(p=1/3\) gives \(q=6/7\).  This does not produce the \(3/5\) collision
  scale.  The theorem's compact Hilbert-scale setup also does not directly
  cover the global line model.

### 1.2 Quantitative Hörmander method for stationary shear

D. Albritton, R. Beekie, and M. Novack,
“Enhanced Dissipation and Hörmander's Hypoellipticity,”
*Journal of Functional Analysis* 283 (2022), 109522.

- Primary preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12308>
- Publisher DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2022.109522>
- Exact point used here: the paper derives quantitative bracket and
  interpolation inequalities for autonomous finite-type shear profiles.  Its
  Theorem 1.1 gives the usual \(\nu^{(N+1)/(N+3)}\) decay rate.
- Boundary for R0.72T: Remark 1.4 says variation on the enhancement
  timescale is a possible generalization, but the proved theorem assumes a
  fixed shear profile and exploits time-translation symmetry.  It does not
  prove a birth/death collision estimate for \(X^3+6SX\).

### 1.3 Slowly varying time-dependent critical points

D. Coble and S. He, “A Note on Enhanced Dissipation of Time-dependent
Shear Flows,” *Communications in Mathematical Sciences* 22 (2024), no. 6.

- Primary preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15738>
- Publisher DOI: <https://doi.org/10.4310/CMS.2024.v22.n6.a10>
- Exact point used here: Theorem 1.2 assumes a fixed finite number of
  nondegenerate critical points, shared by a reference shear and the actual
  shear, with disjoint neighborhoods and slow critical-point motion.
- Boundary for R0.72T: the R0.72S path changes the number of distinct critical
  points at the collision.  The hypotheses of Theorem 1.2 therefore fail at
  precisely the event studied here.

### 1.4 A genuinely moving time-dependent shear

J. Benthaus, G. M. Coclite, and C. Nobili,
“Mixing and Enhanced Dissipation in a Time-translating Shear Flow,” preprint
(2026).

- Primary preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14624>
- Exact point used here: the paper treats \(\sin(y-ct)\), proves a
  time-averaged \(H^{-1}\) estimate on a bounded pre-unmixing interval, and
  constructs a nonautonomous hypocoercive functional for moderate translation
  speeds.
- Boundary for R0.72T: translation preserves the type and number of critical
  points.  The mechanism is not a fold collision, and the result does not
  supply a global estimate for \(X^3+6SX\).

### 1.5 Two further nonautonomous near neighbors

J. Benthaus and C. Nobili, “Enhanced Dissipation via Time-modulated Velocity
Fields,” *Evolution Equations and Control Theory* (2025).

- Primary preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16905>
- Publisher DOI: <https://doi.org/10.3934/eect.2025051>
- Exact point used here: the shear has separable time dependence
  \(\xi(t)w(y)\), and the hypocoercive weights depend on the modulation.
- Boundary for R0.72T: the spatial critical set of \(w\) is fixed.  The paper
  does not treat a local birth/death collision.

S. He, “Localized Enhanced Dissipation: A Hypocoercivity Approach,” preprint
(2026).

- Primary preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14657>
- Exact point used here: the paper develops streamline-wise decay estimates
  for stationary shear flows with critical points.
- Boundary for R0.72T: localization near a fixed critical type does not by
  itself provide all-start observability through \(X^3+6SX\).

### 1.6 Quadratic-model calibration

J. Viola, “The Norm of the Non-self-adjoint Harmonic Oscillator Semigroup,”
*Integral Equations and Operator Theory* 85 (2016), 513--538.

- Primary preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02558>
- Publisher DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00020-016-2303-4>
- Exact point used here: the paper treats semigroups generated by complex
  elliptic quadratic operators.  Dilation of
  \(\nu\partial_x^2-ikbx^2\) gives the time scale
  \((\nu|kb|)^{-1/2}\).
- Boundary for R0.72T: this supports only the deliberately incorrect
  quadratic-potential calibration.  It does not analyze the combined
  nonautonomous cubic model.

### 1.7 Qualitative and quantitative bracket background

L. Hörmander, “Hypoelliptic Second Order Differential Equations,”
*Acta Mathematica* 119 (1967), 147--171.

- Publisher DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02392081>
- Exact point used here: bracket generation is the qualitative source of
  hypoellipticity.  R0.72T computes the missing direction at parabolic
  weighted bracket length five.
- Boundary for R0.72T: qualitative hypoellipticity alone is not a quantitative
  semigroup contraction uniform in the collision start time.

J. Bedrossian and K. Liss, “Quantitative Spectral Gaps for Hypoelliptic
Stochastic Differential Equations with Small Noise,” *Probability and
Mathematical Physics* 2 (2021), 477--531.

- Primary preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13297>
- Publisher DOI: <https://doi.org/10.2140/pmp.2021.2.477>
- Exact point used here: the paper is relevant to the quantitative role of
  time in parabolic Hörmander arguments and small-noise spectral gaps.
- Boundary for R0.72T: its Markov-semigroup hypotheses and coercive structure
  are not an off-the-shelf theorem for the nonconfining complex potential in
  (0.7) of the report source.

## 2. What the audit supports

The primary sources support four limited statements.

1. Nonautonomous mixing-to-dissipation arguments require estimates uniform in
   the initial time; a single block centered at the collision is insufficient.
2. The proved \(H^1\to H^{-1}\) cubic-phase rate \(T^{-1/3}\) is compatible
   with established oscillatory mixing theory, but the CDZE exponent map gives
   only \(q=6/7\).
3. A weighted Hörmander calculation is a credible route to a fifth-root
   estimate, but the quantitative Poincaré/observability step must be proved
   for this nonautonomous polynomial model.
4. Existing time-dependent shear theorems checked here preserve the number
   and type of critical points, or study rigid translation.  They do not
   directly include the R0.72S birth/death event.

## 3. Bounded-search absence statement

I searched the named papers, their primary reference chains, arXiv title and
abstract records, and DOI metadata for combinations of time-dependent shear,
critical-point collision, birth/death fold, enhanced dissipation, cubic
spacetime normal form, and quantitative Hörmander estimates.  Within that
bounded search, I found no theorem that proves a uniform viscous block
contraction through a change in critical-point count for the exact model
\(X^3+6SX\).

This is a search boundary, not a proof of novelty or an exhaustive literature
claim.  In particular, related microlocal, complex-potential, or stochastic
control results may contain a transferable estimate under different language.

## 4. Claims the literature does not authorize

The audit does not authorize any of the following statements:

- that the full model (0.7) has a proved uniform contraction;
- that bounded-chart convergence of the polynomial jets yields a periodic
  semigroup theorem;
- that the collision improves, worsens, or preserves the global nonlinear
  Navier--Stokes stability threshold;
- that the R0.72T calculation resolves any part of the Clay regularity
  criterion.
