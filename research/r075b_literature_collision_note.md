## 0. Scope and verdict

This is a **bounded framing search**, not a systematic review and not a
novelty or priority determination.  It asks only whether the two mechanisms
used in R0.75B -- localized Caccioppoli estimates and temporal distribution of
local dissipation -- are already represented in nearby primary literature.

**Verdict.**  Local-energy/Caccioppoli methods are established techniques.
The screened sources do not state the frozen R0.75B weighted annular ledger,

## 1. Screened primary sources

1. Tongkeun Chang and Kyungkeun Kang, *On Caccioppoli's inequalities of
   Stokes equations and Navier--Stokes equations near boundary*,
   arXiv:1806.02516.
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02516>

   The paper studies nonstationary Stokes/Navier--Stokes Caccioppoli
   inequalities near a boundary and emphasizes that boundary behavior can
   fail in contrast with the interior case.  It is relevant as a scope
   warning: R0.75B is periodic and uses an interior shell cutoff, so it must
   not be advertised as a boundary Caccioppoli theorem.  The abstract does
   not state R0.75B's weighted dyadic payment or temporal-packing criterion.

2. Thierry Gallay and Sinisa Slijepcevic, *Energy bounds for the
   two-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations in an infinite cylinder*,
   arXiv:1308.1544.
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.1544>

   The paper obtains long-time conclusions using detailed local energy
   dissipation estimates in a two-dimensional infinite cylinder.  It confirms
   that organizing a proof through localized dissipation is standard nearby
   methodology.  Its setting and conclusion differ from the three-dimensional
   periodic, exact common-shear, weighted finite-family gate in R0.75B.

3. Gengsheng Wang, Ming Wang, Can Zhang, and Yubiao Zhang, *Observable set,
   observability, interpolation inequality and spectral inequality for the
   heat equation in \(\mathbb R^n\)*, arXiv:1711.04279.
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04279>

   This source studies heat-equation observability, interpolation, and
   spectral inequalities.  It is closer to the R0.75A heat/spectral framing
   than to the R0.75B Caccioppoli ledger.  It does not, from the screened
   statement, supply the outer-collar accumulated-dissipation packing estimate
   needed in (B.44)--(B.45).
