## 2. Source-by-source verification

### 2.1 Taylor

G. I. Taylor, [*LXXV. On the decay of vortices in a viscous
fluid*](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786442308634295), *The London, Edinburgh,
and Dublin Philosophical Magazine and Journal of Science* 46(274), 671--674
(1923).

The author, title, DOI, year, volume, issue, and pages agree with the publisher
record.  Describing the displayed exact field as the classical bi-periodic
decaying vortex is consistent with this source.  The citation supplies only
historical provenance.  Closed-streamline recurrence, the Version-M
trajectory, physical-shell deletion, and payment comparison are derived in
the main note by direct substitution and are not attributed to Taylor.

### 2.2 Yang

Jincheng Yang, [*Construction of Maximal Functions associated with Skewed
Cylinders Generated by Incompressible Flows and
Applications*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05588), submitted 12 August 2020,
revised 11 September 2021.

The title, author, and link are correct.  Theorem 3 concerns the maximal
operator subordinate to admissible skewed cylinders generated by a spatially
mollified divergence-free flow.  Under its stated integrability hypothesis on
the spatial maximal function of the velocity gradient, it proves weak type
\((1,1)\) and strong type \((q,q)\) for every \(q>1\).

This is a close geometric match, but it does not estimate localized
energy-pressure flux, its strong \(L^1\)-in-time variation, or a tail after one
fixed finite shell deletion.  Weak \((1,1)\) is a superlevel-set estimate and
cannot itself be upgraded to the strong endpoint required by (S.444).  The
locked main note incorporates the audit's precision refinement by naming
admissible cylinders and the relevant generator hypothesis.

### 2.3 Dascaliuc--Grujić

Radu Dascaliuc and Zoran Grujić, [*Energy cascades and flux locality in
physical scales of the 3D Navier--Stokes
equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.2193), submitted 11 January 2011,
revised 4 March 2011.

The title, authors, and link are correct.  The paper studies signed total
kinetic-energy-plus-pressure flux through fixed physical-space cutoffs for
suitable weak solutions.  Its cascade and locality conclusions use temporal
and ensemble averaging and an inertial-range condition expressed through the
Taylor microscale and integral-scale domain.

Those conclusions do not bound absolute temporal variation along one moving
centre.  A signed time-space average may exploit cancellation, whereas
(S.444) charges the absolute derivative before integration and uses one
shell-deletion set fixed across time.  The locked main note precisely assigns
the inertial-range hypotheses to the cascade/locality bounds, rather than to
every preliminary flux identity.

### 2.4 Wolf

Jörg Wolf, [*On the local pressure of the Navier--Stokes equations and related
systems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01482), submitted 4 November 2016.

The title, author, and link are correct.  The paper constructs a local pressure
representation of distributions annihilating solenoidal test fields, with a
decomposition of the form \(\partial_t\nabla p_h+\nabla p_0\).  This is not a
temporal bounded-variation theorem.  It supplies neither strong \(L^1\)
control of moving-shell flux nor simultaneous shell packing and fixed
deletion.  The main note's non-implication statement is accurate.

### 2.5 Duchon--Robert

Jean Duchon and Raoul Robert, [*Dissipation d'énergie pour des solutions
faibles des équations d'Euler et Navier--Stokes
incompressibles*](https://www.numdam.org/item/SEDP_1999-2000____A13_0/),
*Séminaire Goulaouic-Schwartz* (1999--2000), talk 13.

The authors, title, Numdam link, venue, and date are correct.  The work gives a
distributional local energy balance and identifies an energy defect produced
by possible lack of regularity.  For dissipative solutions the defect has the
appropriate nonnegative sign; it should not be described without
qualification as a sign-changing defect.

The locked note incorporates the audit's refinement by placing the defect
distribution inside a signed local balance.  That balance does not control
the absolute repeated backtracking charged in (S.468), nor does it yield the
fixed-deletion temporal-tail estimate (S.444).

## 3. Proof independence

The analytic refutation does not invoke a theorem from any of the five sources
above.  Its operative ingredients are direct substitution of the explicit
smooth solution, calculation of its mollified trajectory and recurrence
period, elementary variation counting, physical-shell geometry, and the
stated payment asymptotics.  Taylor supplies historical origin; the other
four sources locate nearby architectures only.

Removing Section 8 would remove provenance and collision context but no
logical premise of the counterexample proof.

## 4. Collision and claim boundary

The checked sources offer, respectively, exact-field provenance,
skewed-cylinder covering geometry, signed fixed-cutoff flux/cascade estimates,
local pressure representation, and a signed local energy balance with defect.
None of their stated theorems supplies the conjunction required by (S.444):

1. strong \(L^1\)-in-time control of absolute moving-shell flux variation;
2. a centre transported by the mollified solution;
3. one finite shell-deletion set selected before the time norm;
4. the universal suitable/Leray-solution quantifiers of the proposal; and
5. the precise sublinear payment \(C(P_R^M)^{2/3}\).

This is a bounded source-by-source non-implication conclusion.  It does not
assert that no related theorem exists elsewhere, that the counterexample or
surviving target is novel, or that any author or group lacks priority.  The
falsity of (S.444) rests on the explicit analytic family, not on a literature
non-hit.
