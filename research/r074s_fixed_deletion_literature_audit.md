# R0.74S Step 18 — primary-source audit for fixed-deletion simultaneous height

## 0. Question, search boundary, and verdict

The audited open statement is the following deterministic suitable-weak
solution estimate.  For each fixed solution, scale, centre, and terminal
domain, one set of exceptional physical shells is selected and retained
for every common good terminal time.  That set may depend on the fixed data;
only its cardinality budget and the estimate constant are universal.  The
remaining forward stopped-flux increments are summed over infinitely many
shells, and the simultaneous height is bounded at the scale

\[
 (P_R^M)^{2/3}.
\]

Equivalently, after the already-proved payments in Step 18, the same question
may be asked for the completed-clock simultaneous height

\[
 \inf_{\#S\le N}\sup_{t\in\mathcal T_R}
       \sum_{k\notin S}K_{k,R}(t).
\]

The search was intentionally restricted to primary papers and official
article pages.  It used two passes:

1. classical local-energy, partial-regularity, critical-space, flux, and
   flow-adapted maximal-function neighbours; and
2. recent finite-chain, moving-window, pressure-flux, and filtered
   scale-packing neighbours.

**Verdict.**  No inspected source proves the full Step 18 quantifier package.
Several sources contain ingredients that could support a future proof, but
each misses at least one indispensable feature: common fixed deletion,
all-terminal simultaneous height, infinite-shell summability, the forward
stopped increment, or the specific quadratic payment.  This is a bounded
non-collision result, not a novelty, priority, or exhaustive-literature
claim.

## 1. Exact target signature

The collision test used the seven-coordinate signature

| Coordinate | Step 18 requirement |
|---|---|
| solution class | deterministic three-dimensional suitable weak solution |
| spatial organization | the inherited physical-shell family around one moving Version-M centre |
| exceptional set | for each fixed solution, scale, centre, and terminal domain, one instance-dependent set of shell indices with universally bounded cardinality, fixed across time |
| time quantifier | supremum over \(\mathcal T_R\cap\mathcal G_R\) for stopped flux; equivalently, after paid reduction and \(\ell^1\)-continuity, all \(t\in\mathcal T_R\) for completed clocks |
| measured object | nonnegative forward stopped-flux increments with hybrid starts \(\sigma_k^{\rm hyb}(\tau)\) still depending on \(\tau\), or the equivalent completed clock after known payments |
| shell quantifier | an infinite \(\ell^1\) tail |
| payment | \(C(P_R^M)^{2/3}\), uniform in solution, admissible scale, and centre |

A paper was treated as a collision only if its theorem supplied all seven
coordinates, not merely related terminology such as Carleson packing,
maximal function, energy cascade, bad-scale counting, or local regularity.

## 2. Classical local-energy and regularity neighbours

### 2.1 Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg

L. Caffarelli, R. Kohn, and L. Nirenberg,
[*Partial regularity of suitable weak solutions of the Navier–Stokes
equations*](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160350604),
Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 35 (1982), 771–831.

The paper supplies the suitable-weak local-energy framework and the
epsilon-regularity endpoint underlying this programme.  Its theorem controls
the size of the singular set through local scale-critical quantities.  It
does not formulate a moving-centre stopped-flux clock, a common finite
shell-deletion set, or an all-terminal infinite-shell height.

The exact inspected anchors are Section 1, Theorem B and Proposition 2 with
the small-\(\limsup\)
\(r^{-1}\int_{Q_r}|\nabla u|^2\) regularity criterion (1.20); the
generalized local energy inequality in Section 2, (2.5); and the parabolic
Vitali covering lemma used in the singular-set measure argument, Section 6,
Lemma 6.1.

**Classification:** foundational endpoint; no collision.

### 2.2 Duchon–Robert

J. Duchon and R. Robert,
[*Inertial energy dissipation for weak solutions of incompressible Euler and
Navier–Stokes equations*](https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/13/1/312),
Nonlinearity 13 (2000), 249–255.  An
[open seminar account](https://www.numdam.org/item/SEDP_1999-2000____A13_0/)
records the same local-energy-defect framework.

The paper makes the local energy-defect term explicit for weak Euler and
Navier–Stokes solutions and supplies a signed local energy-transfer
perspective.  That signed defect is structurally adjacent to the present
completed-clock bookkeeping.  The paper does not prove fixed best-\(N\)
deletion, a simultaneous temporal maximum, or a \(2/3\)-power shell-tail
bound.

The exact inspected anchors are Section 2, Propositions 1--3,
pages 250--251, where the local energy balance and defect distribution are
defined and their elementary properties are recorded.

**Classification:** signed-defect language and local-energy ingredient; no
collision.

### 2.3 Koch–Tataru

H. Koch and D. Tataru,
[*Well-posedness for the Navier–Stokes
equations*](https://math.berkeley.edu/~tataru/papers/nas.pdf),
Adv. Math. 157 (2001), 22–35.

Their \(BMO^{-1}\) norm is expressed through the caloric extension and a
space-time Carleson quantity.  Their main theorem gives a unique global
solution for sufficiently small divergence-free \(BMO^{-1}\) initial data in
the mild-solution class \(X\).  This is a small-data critical-space theorem.
It is not an a posteriori estimate for an arbitrary suitable weak solution
and does not select or delete physical shells along a moving trajectory.

The exact inspected anchors are Section 1, Definition 1.1, displays
(2)--(4), and Theorems 2--3.  The Carleson quantity belongs to the
initial-data and mild-solution construction, not to a posterior suitable-weak
terminal ledger.

**Classification:** important Carleson architecture in a different solution
class; no collision.

### 2.4 Lei–Ren

Z. Lei and X. Ren,
[*Quantitative partial regularity of the Navier–Stokes equations and
applications*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2024.109654),
Adv. Math. 445 (2024), 109654; an
[open arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01783) is available.

The paper obtains a logarithmic improvement of CKN partial regularity and
regular intervals using dissipation pigeonholing.  The mechanism extracts
favourable scales or intervals from a summable dissipation ledger.  It does
not provide one fixed exceptional shell set valid at every common good
terminal time, nor does it estimate the Step 18 stopped-flux or
completed-clock functional.

The exact inspected anchors are Section 1.1.1, Theorem A and (1.13), the
disjoint dissipation ledger (1.14)--(1.15), Theorem B and (1.16), Remark 8,
Proposition 9, and the axisymmetric application beginning with (5.1).

**Classification:** highly relevant pigeonhole mechanism; no collision.

## 3. Flux and flow-adapted maximal-function neighbours

### 3.1 Dascaliuc–Grujić

R. Dascaliuc and Z. Grujić,
[*Energy cascades and flux locality in physical scales of the 3D
Navier–Stokes equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.2193),
Comm. Math. Phys. 305 (2011), 199–220.

The paper proves rigorous bounds on kinetic-energy-plus-pressure flux in
physical space, and obtains cascade and locality statements under an
inertial-range condition.  The central quantities are signed time and
ensemble averages over physical covers.  They are not terminal-time maxima
and do not impose one common best-\(N\) shell deletion across all times.

The exact inspected anchors are Section 4, Theorem 4.1 and
(4.13)--(4.16), followed by Section 5, Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.1.
Those statements concern ensemble-averaged flux positivity and locality,
not the Step 18 time-shell minimax.

**Classification:** physical-space flux ingredient; no collision.

### 3.2 Yang

J. Yang,
[*Construction of maximal functions associated with skewed cylinders
generated by incompressible flows and
applications*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05588),
Ann. Inst. H. Poincaré C Anal. Non Linéaire 39 (2022), 793–818.

Yang constructs a maximal function for admissible skewed cylinders following
mollified trajectories and proves weak type \((1,1)\) and strong type
\((p,p)\) bounds using a covering lemma.  This directly supports the
legitimacy of flow-adapted geometric selection.  Its input is an average of
a scalar function over admissible cylinders.  It neither controls a common
fixed shell deletion nor converts an absolute-flux \(\ell^1\) ledger into
the Step 18 simultaneous \(2/3\)-power bound.

The exact published-version anchors are Definitions 1--2, Theorem 1, and
the covering statement in Section 2, Proposition 9.  The arXiv HTML
conversion labels the main maximal theorem as Theorem 3; this is a numbering
difference, not a second result.

**Classification:** flow-adapted covering tool; no collision.

## 4. Finite-chain and moving-window neighbours

The four papers in this section are 2026 arXiv preprints.  They are treated
as recent primary sources, not as peer-reviewed established literature.
Their theorem-status labels are therefore recorded exactly.

### 4.1 Yu: finite-chain CKN-bad-scale counting

R. Yu,
[*Finite-Chain CKN-Bad Scale Counting for Navier–Stokes: Standard PDE Closure
and Canonical Detector Realization*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21783),
arXiv:2606.21783v1.

Theorem 6.3 bounds the weighted number of bad scales on one preassigned
finite geometric chain by nonnegative channel costs.  Corollary 7.1 says
that sufficiently small total finite-chain cost yields at least one
CKN-small scale.  The closing mechanism imports compactness and a
zero-vertical-component regularity endpoint.

The index range is \(0,\ldots,K\), the conclusion is existence of one good
scale, and the detector is not a stopped-flux height uniform over the
common good terminal set.  The paper also states that it does not claim a
scale-uniform regularity theorem.

**Classification:** proved finite-chain counting, not infinite-shell
fixed-deletion control; no collision.

### 4.2 Yu: coarse-grained pressure–flux work depletion

R. Yu,
[*Coarse-Grained Resolution and Pressure–Flux Work Depletion for
Navier–Stokes CKN Badness*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25322),
arXiv:2606.25322v1.

Theorem 4.1 proves an exact weighted depletion inequality for a fixed finite
chain of coarse-grained pressure-flux work.  Negative work or backscatter is
retained on the right side.  Remark 4.2 explicitly states that the theorem
does not show the negative index set is small, does not make its extraction
constant uniform in a moving-window limit, and does not make the
localization leakage summable as the chain length tends to infinity.

Those three limitations are precisely where a direct transplant into Step
18 would fail.  A finite signed-work telescope is not the same as one
fixed-deletion bound over all common good terminal times and infinitely
many shells, or its all-terminal completed-clock equivalent.

**Classification:** exact finite-chain signed-work ledger with explicit
large-chain limitations; no collision.

### 4.3 Yu: invisible defect cascades

R. Yu,
[*Invisible Defect Cascades for Navier–Stokes
Regularity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12756),
arXiv:2606.12756v1.

Lemma 8.6 is a valid finite-overlap Tonelli argument after the moving
packages have been pulled back to one physical cylinder and dominated by
one common finite pressure-flux measure.  Thus it identifies a potentially
useful sufficient mechanism: common-measure domination plus weighted finite
overlap.

It does not derive those assumptions for the present shells.  More
importantly, moving-window growth is stated as Target Theorem 15.4, not as a
proved theorem.  The paper's Theorem 12.9 is an alternative conditional on
dyadic defect extraction, observable depletion, and the finite-window
hierarchy; its failure branches retain non-effective window constants or an
NS-realizable invisible cascade.

**Classification:** proved conditional finite-overlap lemma and conditional
alternative; the uniform moving-window closure remains a target; no
collision.

### 4.4 Yu: filtered vortex stretching

R. Yu,
[*Filtered Vortex Stretching and Subgrid Defects for the Three-Dimensional
Navier–Stokes Equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27560),
arXiv:2606.27560v1.

Theorem 8.7 obtains a truncation-independent reassigned-annulus bound under
the explicit sequence assumptions
\(\mathfrak A\in\ell^p\) and \(\mathcal Q\in\ell^q\),
\(1/p+1/q=1\).  Theorem 10.3 obtains an infinite unweighted closure only
after separately assuming summability of the full far field, the increment
defect, and all remaining shell budgets.

These results demonstrate a concrete way that annular reassignment and
sequence convolution can close an infinite sum.  They do not prove the
needed hypotheses from the Version-M payment, do not use the Step 18
terminal-clock functional, and do not select one instance-dependent shell
set of universally bounded cardinality that remains fixed across terminal
time.

**Classification:** conditional infinite-shell closure under stronger
summability hypotheses; no collision.

## 6. What may actually transfer

Four mechanisms survive the collision audit as technically plausible
inputs for the next step.

1. **Common-measure finite overlap.**  If the positive stopped increments
   can be dominated by restrictions of one finite physical measure and the
   selected time-shell incidence family has bounded overlap, Tonelli gives
   a genuine infinite-shell charge.
2. **Dissipation pigeonholing with persistence.**  Lei–Ren shows that a
   summable dissipation ledger can select good scales or intervals.  Step 18
   would need the stronger conclusion that high simultaneous clock height
   persists long enough to consume a cubic payment.
3. **Annular reassignment.**  The filtered-stretching convolution shows how
   far-field pieces can be charged to disjoint or decaying annuli.  A usable
   version must derive the required sequence norms from the present
   Version-M ledger rather than assume them.
4. **Signed-work depletion.**  The coarse-work identity keeps negative work
   visible.  A successful extension must either pay the negative branch or
   prove that the hybrid first-passage construction prevents repeated
   unpaid backscatter.

Each item is a research route, not an inherited theorem.

## 7. Exact remaining gap

The literature audit sharpens the next question to a single implication:

\[
 \text{Version-M local energy and cubic payment}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{uniform time-shell incidence packing after one finite deletion}.
\]

The missing statement is not a standard maximal-function estimate.  It must
simultaneously:

- preserve the forward sign selected by the hybrid first passage;
- charge all surviving shells at one terminal time without summing their
  separate temporal maxima;
- keep one exceptional set independent of terminal time; and
- produce \(A_R=(P_R^M)^{2/3}\), not the inherited linear payment.

The triangular clocks in the Step 18 main note show that none of these
features follows from abstract nonnegativity plus the linear variation
ledger.  Therefore the next proof attempt must use a genuinely
Navier–Stokes-specific persistence, overlap, pressure-work, or
vortex-stretching mechanism.
