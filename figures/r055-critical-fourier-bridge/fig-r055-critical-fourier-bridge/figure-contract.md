# Figure contract — R0.55 critical Fourier bridge

## Question

Which part of the full Fourier--Leray equation admits a scale-critical scalar
upper bound, which frequency class saturates it, and can the existing
nontrivial scalar charge be extended to arbitrary data without selecting a
frame?

## Supported claim

The figure supports three exact statements:

1. \(\mathcal X^{-1}\) is scale critical and the heat-Duhamel bilinear map has
   a finite \(1/\nu\) bound in \(\mathcal E_\nu\);
2. the displayed high--high-to-low family has normalized critical symbol
   ratio exactly one for every positive integer \(N\), with unbounded
   input/output scale separation;
3. every convolution-additive, rotation-invariant scalar charge is zero.

## Visual encodings

- Panel (a) uses a solid blue line for the exact scaling exponent and a gold
  diamond for the critical point.
- Panel (b) uses blue, gold, and black arrows for \(p_N\), \(q_N\), and their
  output \(k\).
- Panel (c) uses a solid blue line for the exact symbol ratio and a dashed
  gold line for scale separation. Line style preserves the distinction in
  grayscale.

## Scope boundary

The figure does not identify a large-data mechanism, compare its normalized
Catalan radius numerically with R0.54, or rule out vector-valued, directional,
or multi-frame bridges. The displayed finite rows are not proofs of the
all-index identities.
