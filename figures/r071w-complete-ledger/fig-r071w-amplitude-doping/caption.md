**Figure R0.71W-1. Amplitude-doped complete-ledger test.** The amplitude law
is \(A_q=q^{3/2}\), with shear and active-scalar amplitudes of order \(A_q\)
and a decoupled heat background \(B_q=A_q q\). **A**, the high-precision
certificate gives a leading second-root atom divided by the complete-ledger
proxy that has asymptotic order \(q^{+1}\); the independent reconstruction
agrees with the fitted power. The horizontal line marks ratio one. **B**, a
separate nonlinear retained Fourier-coset calculation for
\(q=256,512,1024,2048,4096\) gives atom-proxy order \(q^{+1}\) and full
retained \(\dot H^{-1}\) rotational-charge order \(q^{-1}\). The rotational
quantity includes all retained convolution modes and the reported analytic
upper bound for the omitted time tail. **C**, the normalized second-root slope
remains near \(0.163\); the inset shows relative slope differences for
\(R=15,30,60\) against the \(R=40\) reference at \(q=1024\). These
calculations provide computation corroboration only for the amplitude-doped
power ledger. They are not DNS,
do not prove spectral-truncation convergence or the uniform continuum
implicit-function theorem, and do not establish a Navier--Stokes regularity
result. The initial energy and enstrophy grow with \(q\), so the construction
tests a data-uniform complete ledger rather than an estimate with unrestricted
initial-data dependence.
