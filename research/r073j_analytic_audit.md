## 2. Audit of J0--J3

### 2.1 Spaces, compactness, and essential spectrum

**Decision: PASS.**

The proof defines

\[
 L=-\partial_x^2+\frac14,
 \qquad
 X=H^{-1}_{\rm per}
 \tag{2.1}
\]

with kinetic norm

\[
 \|q\|_X^2=4\langle L^{-1}q,q\rangle_{L^2}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The map \(U=2L^{-1/2}:X\to L^2\) is unitary.  Its conjugation of the kinetic
operator is

\[
 UA_XU^{-1}=-\frac i2M_{W_d}+K_d,
 \tag{2.3}
\]

where

\[
 K_d=-\frac i2\left(
 L^{-1/2}[M_{W_d},L^{1/2}]
 +L^{-1/2}M_{W_d''}L^{-1/2}
 \right).
 \tag{2.4}
\]

I checked the order of every factor in (2.4).  For the finite Fourier
profile, the commutator is bounded on \(L^2\), and the leading
\(L^{-1/2}\) is compact.  The second term is compact for the same reason.
Thus the Fredholm essential spectrum is

\[
 \sigma_{\rm ess}(A_X(d))
 =-\frac i2\operatorname{Ran}W_d\subset i\mathbb R.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

The complement of this compact segment is connected and contains the large
resolvent.  Analytic Fredholm theory then makes every right-half-plane
spectral point an isolated eigenvalue of finite algebraic multiplicity.

Potential failures checked:

- treating \(A_X\) and the ordinary \(L^2\) realization as similar;
- dropping the commutator generated by kinetic conjugation;
- asserting that an Evans count excludes continuous spectrum without first
  locating the essential spectrum;
- applying analytic Fredholm theory on a component with no known resolvent
  point.

The proof avoids all four failures.

### 2.2 Generalized-root and Jordan-chain bootstrap

**Decision: PASS.**

For

\[
 D_\lambda=M_{\lambda+iW_d/2},
 \tag{2.6}
\]

the right-half-plane estimate

\[
 |\lambda+iW_d/2|\ge\operatorname{Re}\lambda
 \tag{2.7}
\]

makes \(D_\lambda^{-1}\) a smooth multiplier on every periodic Sobolev
space.  If

\[
 r=(\lambda-A_X)q,
 \tag{2.8}
\]

then

\[
 D_\lambda q+\frac i2W_d''L^{-1}q=r.
 \tag{2.9}
\]

For an eigenvector, equation (2.9) has \(r=0\) and raises the regularity from
\(H^{-1}\) to \(H^1\).  For a higher Jordan vector, induction makes the
inhomogeneity \(r\) smooth before the same bootstrap is applied.  Repeated use
of \(L^{-1}\) then raises regularity by two derivatives at each step.  Thus
every vector in \(\ker(\lambda-A_X)^m\) is smooth.

The converse inclusion is also valid because the ordinary \(L^2\)
realization and \(A_X\) agree on the smooth generalized vectors.  Hence their
stabilized generalized eigenspaces and algebraic multiplicities agree.

Potential failures checked:

- proving regularity only for eigenvectors and silently extending it to
  Jordan vectors;
- using \(L\) as a bounded similarity on \(X\);
- comparing ordinary kernels but not stabilized generalized-root spaces;
- losing two derivatives in the inhomogeneous induction step.

The proof explicitly handles the inhomogeneous vector \(r\), so the full
Jordan-chain conclusion passes.

### 2.3 Operator-pencil factorization

**Decision: PASS.**

For the ordinary realization

\[
 A_2=-\frac i2(M_{W_d}+M_{W_d''}L^{-1}),
 \tag{2.10}
\]

the Rayleigh pencil is

\[
 T(d,\lambda)
 =L+\frac i2D_\lambda^{-1}M_{W_d''}
 =-\partial_x^2+\frac14+
 \frac{W_d''}{W_d-2i\lambda}.
 \tag{2.11}
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
 \boxed{(\lambda-A_2)L=D_\lambda T(d,\lambda).}
 \tag{2.12}
\]

I checked both the sign and the order of the factors.  Each side equals

\[
 \left(\lambda+\frac i2W_d\right)L+\frac i2W_d''.
 \tag{2.13}
\]

The identity also fixes the phase-speed convention

\[
 c=2i\lambda,
 \qquad
 \lambda=-\frac i2c.
 \tag{2.14}
\]

Thus \(c=i\eta\), \(\eta>0\), corresponds to
\(\lambda=\eta/2>0\).  No sign reversal remains in this bridge.

### 2.4 Periodic BVP, monodromy, and partial multiplicity

**Decision: PASS.**

This decision uses the standard analytic-equivalence facts listed in the
proof.

The augmented boundary-value operator

\[
 \mathcal F(\lambda)=(T^\sharp(\lambda),\mathcal B)
 \tag{2.15}
\]

is compared with the periodic-domain pencil by a fixed right inverse of the
boundary mismatch.  The resulting upper-triangular block is analytically
reduced to

\[
 \operatorname{diag}(T_{\rm per}(\lambda),I).
 \tag{2.16}
\]

The initial-value operator

\[
 \mathcal G(\lambda)=(T^\sharp(\lambda),\mathcal C)
 \tag{2.17}
\]

is an analytic isomorphism.  Variation of constants gives the second block
reduction

\[
 \mathcal F\mathcal G^{-1}
 \sim\operatorname{diag}(I,M(\lambda)-I).
 \tag{2.18}
\]

I multiplied both triangular block matrices explicitly.  The off-diagonal
blocks cancel with the signs written in the proof.  Stabilization by identity
blocks and analytic invertible left/right factors preserve partial
multiplicities.  Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{\lambda_0}\det(M-I)
 =\operatorname{algmult}_{A_X}(\lambda_0).
 \tag{2.19}
\]

Potential failures checked:

- inferring operator algebraic multiplicity directly from a finite
  determinant;
- comparing kernels but not partial multiplicities;
- omitting the fixed-domain stabilization between \(H^2_{\rm per}\) and
  unrestricted \(H^2\);
- reversing the BVP/IVP triangular multiplier;
- forgetting that the factor \(D_\lambda\) depends analytically on
  \(\lambda\).

The explicit factorization and two block eliminations close these analytic
gaps.  The audit adopts the standard Gohberg--Sigal analytic-equivalence
principle and the standard equality between characteristic multiplicity of
\(\lambda-A\) and Riesz algebraic multiplicity.

### 2.5 Evans sign and analyticity

**Decision: PASS.**

The first-order coefficient matrix has zero trace, so

\[
 \det M(d,\lambda)=1,
 \qquad
 E(d,\lambda)=\det(M-I)=2-\operatorname{tr}M.
 \tag{2.20}
\]

R0.73C used \(F=\operatorname{tr}M-2\), so the present convention is
\(E=-F\).  This constant factor changes real endpoint signs but not zeros or
winding numbers.  The denominator estimate

\[
 |W_d-2i\lambda|\ge2\operatorname{Re}\lambda
 \tag{2.21}
\]

gives holomorphy in the open right half-plane.  Complex-\(d\) ellipse sizes
and their numerical denominator margins are not established by this analytic
statement; those belong to the interval certificate.

### 2.6 Reflection--conjugation symmetry

**Decision: PASS.**

Plain conjugation is not enough because it changes the sign of the imaginary
spectral term.  The proof correctly also uses

\[
 W_d(2\pi-x)=-W_d(x),
 \qquad
 W_d''(2\pi-x)=-W_d''(x).
 \tag{2.22}
\]

With \(S=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), reflection and conjugation give

\[
 M(d,\bar\lambda)
 =S\,\overline{M(d,\lambda)^{-1}}\,S.
 \tag{2.23}
\]

Using \(\det M=1\) then yields

\[
 E(d,\bar\lambda)=\overline{E(d,\lambda)}.
 \tag{2.24}
\]

The kinetic operator calculation with the antiunitary reflection--conjugation
map gives the same spectral symmetry and preserves Jordan-chain lengths.

Potential failures checked:

- writing (2.24) as a consequence of conjugation alone;
- forgetting inversion of the monodromy under reversal of the spatial
  direction;
- using \(\operatorname{tr}M^{-1}=\operatorname{tr}M\) without
  \(\det M=1\);
- proving symmetry only for eigenvalues but not their algebraic data.

### 2.7 Howard enclosure

**Decision: PASS.**

For \(c=2i\lambda\) with \(\operatorname{Im}c>0\), the substitution

\[
 \phi=(W_d-c)F
 \tag{2.25}
\]

is nonsingular and periodic.  Integration by parts gives

\[
 \int_{\mathbb T}(W_d-c)^2
 \left(|F'|^2+\frac14|F|^2\right)dx=0.
 \tag{2.26}
\]

The associated positive weighted average implies

\[
 |c|^2=\langle W_d^2\rangle_G
 \le\|W_d\|_\infty^2.
 \tag{2.27}
\]

Writing \(a=e^{-d}\), \(b=e^{-4d}\), and \(t=\cos x\), the two sign regions
for \(t\) give

\[
 \|W_d\|_\infty\le\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}.
 \tag{2.28}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{|\lambda|\le\frac{3\sqrt3}{16}<\frac{13}{40}.}
 \tag{2.29}
\]

The strict comparison is exact because the difference of the squared bounds
is \(1/6400\).

Potential failures checked:

- a nonperiodic boundary term after the Howard substitution;
- using the identity when \(W_d-c\) can vanish;
- losing the factor two between \(c\) and \(\lambda\);
- replacing the profile maximum by an unsupported decimal;
- applying the eigenvalue bound to continuous spectrum without Section 2.1.

All are handled in the proof.

### 2.8 Branch conclusion

**Decision: CONDITIONAL.**

If a conjugation-invariant right-half-plane contour is uniformly zero-free
and has winding one at one parameter value, the argument principle gives one
Evans zero counted with order **inside that contour**.  Sections 2.4 and 2.6
then make that zero real and the corresponding kinetic eigenvalue
algebraically simple.  The holomorphic implicit-function theorem gives local
analytic dependence, and uniqueness patches the local branches across the
real \(d\)-interval.

This implication is correct.  The hypotheses are numerical certificate
claims and are outside this analytic audit.  I therefore do not mark the
unique branch, explicit gap, or J5--J9 as independently verified here.
Moreover, a count on one contour does not by itself exclude zeros in the rest
of the right half-plane.  A global or rightmost conclusion additionally needs
the certified contour geometry, the Howard enclosure, and the certified
exclusion of every relevant complementary region.  Likewise, a local-circle
count must be combined with the global count and a verified nesting relation;
neither numerical geometric assertion is reviewed here.

## 3. Audit of the overlap analytic proof

### 3.1 Kinetic adjoint potential

**Decision: PASS.**

At a real eigenvalue, let \(h=L\phi\).  The right factorization is

\[
 \lambda-A_2=\mathcal D_+T_+L^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_+=\frac i2(W_d-2i\lambda).
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Its adjoint has

\[
 T_+^*=L+\frac{W_d''}{W_d+2i\lambda},
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_+^*=-\frac i2(W_d+2i\lambda).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Conjugating the right Rayleigh equation gives
\(T_+^*\bar\phi=0\).  Hence

\[
 \boxed{p=\frac{\bar\phi}{W_d+2i\lambda}}
 \tag{3.3}
\]

satisfies \((\lambda-A_2)^*p=0\).  The kinetic left vector is

\[
 \ell=Lp.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The identity

\[
 \langle Lp,q\rangle_X=4\langle p,q\rangle_{L^2}
 \tag{3.5}
\]

then proves \(A_X^*\ell=\lambda\ell\).

This resolves the possible failure of identifying an ordinary \(L^2\)
adjoint vector directly with a kinetic-space vector.  The potential is
\(p\); the kinetic adjoint vector is \(Lp\).

### 3.2 Numerator and energy formulas

**Decision: PASS.**

The eigenvector interpretation applies on the Evans zero set; off that set
the same formulas define auxiliary interpolation quantities only.

At real parameters,

\[
 \bar p=\frac{\phi}{W_d-2i\lambda},
 \qquad
 L\phi=-\frac{W_d''}{W_d-2i\lambda}\phi.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

Thus

\[
 N:=\langle p,L\phi\rangle_{L^2}
 =-\int_{\mathbb T}
 \frac{W_d''\phi^2}{(W_d-2i\lambda)^2}\,dx.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

When \(\phi\) and \(p\) are periodic,

\[
 E_r=\int_{\mathbb T}
 \left(|\phi'|^2+\frac14|\phi|^2\right)dx,
 \qquad
 E_p=\int_{\mathbb T}
 \left(|p'|^2+\frac14|p|^2\right)dx.
 \tag{3.8}
\]

The normalized kinetic pairing is exactly

\[
 \frac{|\langle\ell,h\rangle_X|}
 {\|\ell\|_X\|h\|_X}
 =\frac{|N|}{\sqrt{E_rE_p}}.
 \tag{3.9}
\]

The factors of four cancel.  Away from a true Evans zero, these integrals are
interpolation auxiliaries rather than eigenvector norms.  The proof states
this distinction explicitly.

Potential failures checked:

- a missing conjugate in \(\langle p,L\phi\rangle\);
- a sign error in the Rayleigh identity for \(L\phi\);
- a missing kinetic factor four;
- integrating by parts before periodicity is known;
- calling an off-root selected solution an eigenvector.

### 3.3 Plus/minus holomorphic substitutes

**Decision: PASS.**

For complex parameters the proof defines

\[
 D_\pm=W_d\mp2i\lambda,
 \qquad
 Q_\pm=\frac14+\frac{W_d''}{D_\pm},
 \tag{3.10}
\]

with monodromies \(M_\pm\) and selected solutions \(\phi_\pm\).  At real
parameters,

\[
 M_-=\overline{M_+},
 \qquad
 \phi_-=\overline{\phi_+}.
 \tag{3.11}
\]

The analytic potential pair is

\[
 p_+=\frac{\phi_-}{D_-},
 \qquad
 p_-=\frac{\phi_+}{D_+}.
 \tag{3.12}
\]

The four interpolated functions are

\[
 \mathcal A=M_{+,12},
 \tag{3.13}
\]

\[
 \mathcal N=-\int_{\mathbb T}
 \frac{W_d''\phi_+^2}{D_+^2}\,dx,
 \tag{3.14}
\]

\[
 \mathcal E_r=\int_{\mathbb T}
 \left(\phi_+'\phi_-'+\frac14\phi_+\phi_-\right)dx,
 \tag{3.15}
\]

\[
 \mathcal E_p=\int_{\mathbb T}
 \left(p_+'p_-'+\frac14p_+p_-\right)dx.
 \tag{3.16}
\]

They are holomorphic wherever \(D_+D_-\ne0\), and their real-rectangle
restrictions are exactly the conjugate-based node quantities.  The proof does
not attempt to declare conjugation holomorphic.

Potential failures checked:

- complexifying \(\bar\phi(d,\lambda)\) as
  \(\overline{\phi(d,\lambda)}\);
- changing \(d\) to \(\bar d\) or \(\lambda\) to \(\bar\lambda\) inside a
  Chebyshev ellipse;
- choosing a minus solution with an initial vector unrelated to the
  conjugate plus vector on the real rectangle;
- forgetting the denominator derivative in \(p_\pm'\).

The plus/minus construction avoids these failures.

### 3.4 Complex-parameter denominator and output majorants

**Analytic decision: PASS.**

**Numerical positivity decision: OUT-OF-SCOPE.**

For the complex-\(d\) ellipse and real \(\lambda\), the proof bounds
\(\operatorname{Im}W_d\) and obtains

\[
 \delta_d
 =2\lambda_{\min}-B_{\operatorname{Im}W}.
 \tag{3.17}
\]

For real \(d\) and the complex-\(\lambda\) ellipse,

\[
 \delta_\lambda=2\min\operatorname{Re}\lambda.
 \tag{3.18}
\]

Both bounds apply to \(D_+\) and \(D_-\).  Whether the configured Arb balls
prove \(\delta_d>0\) and \(\delta_\lambda>0\) is a numerical question not
recomputed here.

Given any positive \(\delta\), the proof sets

\[
 Q_*=\frac14+\frac{B_{xx}}{\delta},
 \qquad
 s=\sqrt{Q_*},
 \qquad
 F=e^{2\pi s}.
 \tag{3.19}
\]

With the scaled maximum norm for

\[
 z=(s\phi,\phi'),
 \tag{3.20}
\]

the first-order coefficient has induced norm at most \(s\).  Consequently

\[
 |M_{\pm,11}|\le F,
 \qquad
 |M_{\pm,12}|\le F/s.
 \tag{3.21}
\]

For the selected vector \((M_{12},1-M_{11})\), the full solution bound is

\[
 S=F(1+F),
 \qquad
 |\phi_\pm|\le S/s,
 \qquad
 |\phi_\pm'|\le S.
 \tag{3.22}
\]

I checked that no Euclidean-norm factor is missing: the proof consistently
uses the scaled maximum norm.  The resulting anchor, numerator, right-energy,
potential-derivative, and left-energy majorants match the algebraic bounds in
the proof.

Potential failures checked:

- bounding only \(D_+\) but not \(D_-\);
- using \(2\operatorname{Re}\lambda\) when \(d\) is complex without
  subtracting \(|\operatorname{Im}W_d|\);
- omitting the dependence of the selected initial vector on the monodromy;
- using \(F\) rather than \(F(1+F)\) for the selected solution;
- dropping the \(W_d'\phi/D_\pm^2\) term from the potential derivative;
- silently switching between Euclidean and maximum norms.

### 3.5 Tensor Chebyshev error

**Decision: PASS.**

Let \(I_d\) and \(I_\lambda\) be the two one-variable interpolation
operators.  The proof uses

\[
 f-I_\lambda I_df
 =(f-I_\lambda f)+I_\lambda(f-I_df),
 \tag{3.23}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \|f-I_\lambda I_df\|_\infty
 \le\epsilon_\lambda+\Lambda_\lambda\epsilon_d.}
 \tag{3.24}
\]

This is correct.  A joint complex \((d,\lambda)\) majorant is unnecessary:
the first error uses complex \(\lambda\) with real \(d\), and the second uses
complex \(d\) with real \(\lambda\), followed by the real
\(\lambda\)-interpolation operator norm.

The degree convention is also consistent: degree 28 uses 29 first-kind
roots, and the standard error

\[
 \frac{4M\rho^{-28}}{\rho-1}
 \tag{3.25}
\]

has no node-count shift.  The Taylor order-12 statement is separate and uses
an order-11 polynomial plus an order-12 remainder.

Potential failures checked:

- omitting the Lebesgue factor on the propagated \(d\)-error;
- applying a Lebesgue factor to the wrong summand;
- requiring an unavailable joint ellipse;
- confusing polynomial degree with node count;
- reusing the Chebyshev degree as the ODE Taylor order.

The analytic formula passes.  I did not recompute any interpolant coefficient,
Bernstein hull, cell margin, or interval remainder value.

### 3.6 Fixed kinetic anchor

**Functional decision: PASS.**

**Uniform-nonvanishing decision: CONDITIONAL.**

The proposed anchor is

\[
 \mathfrak a(h)=(L^{-1}h)(0).
 \tag{3.26}
\]

It is a fixed bounded functional on \(X=H^{-1}_{\rm per}\), because

\[
 L^{-1}:H^{-1}_{\rm per}\longrightarrow H^1_{\rm per}
 \tag{3.27}
\]

is bounded and one-dimensional \(H^1_{\rm per}\) embeds continuously in
\(C^0\).  For \(h=L\phi\),

\[
 \mathfrak a(h)=\phi(0)=M_{+,12}.
 \tag{3.28}
\]

Thus a validated lower bound \(|M_{+,12}|>0\) on the root branch supplies a
fixed phase convention.  The functional argument passes; the uniform
nonvanishing lower bound is a numerical certificate claim and is not
independently verified here.

### 3.7 Extraction of J10 and J11

\[
 \frac{|\mathcal N|}

and \(|\mathcal A|>0\) throughout the rectangle, J10 and J11 follow.

The implication is correct.  Neither premise is independently certified by
this analytic audit, so J10 and J11 are not marked closed here.

or require final integration.

   final programs and configurations, both primary certificates, the

   publication package.

   symmetry bridge before `uniqueAlgebraicallySimpleRightRoot` is closed.

   its auxiliary functions are interpreted as kinetic eigenvector quantities.

   than reporting a standalone eigenvector theorem from a rectangle
   calculation.
6. Any source change after the hashes in Section 1 invalidates the matching
   audit entry until the diff is reviewed and the final manifest is updated.

The correct present conclusion is narrow: the two analytic proof files pass
this audit at the recorded hashes, while the interval assertions and the
combined R0.73J theorem remain outside this audit and fail closed until their
separate gates pass.
