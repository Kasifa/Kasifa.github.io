## 3. Exact shear and pathwise transport

N. K. Singh and S. Sridhar,
[*Plane shearing waves of arbitrary form: exact solutions of the
Navier--Stokes equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.5507),
arXiv:1101.5507 (2011; revised 2017),
[DOI 10.1140/epjp/i2017-11659-5](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjp/i2017-11659-5).

This is an exact incompressible Navier--Stokes shear precedent with
superposable parallel Kelvin modes.  It does not contain the R0.74Y
physical-shell observable, quadratic-versus-cubic exponent screen, or
fixed-deletion quantifier.

V. Gardner, K. L. Liss, and J. C. Mattingly,
[*A pathwise approach to the enhanced dissipation of passive scalars advected
by shear flows*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05657),
arXiv:2410.05657 (2024).

The paper develops stochastic-trajectory and Girsanov-control methods for
passive scalars in shear flows and obtains local-in-space decay information.
That is relevant to any future proof of changed-geometry bridge survival.
The screened record does not compare a remote endpoint with a
Navier--Stokes exterior cubic payment and does not construct the proposed
corrector cell.

## 4. Heat observability, control cost, and propagation of smallness

C. Laurent and M. Léautaud,
[*Observability of the heat equation, geometric constants in control theory,
and a conjecture of Luc Miller*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00969),
arXiv:1806.00969 (2018).

This work studies short-time heat observability constants of exponential
form and their dependence on the geometry of the observation set.  It also
derives bounds for tunneling and approximate-control costs.  It is directly
relevant to the warning that local exponential cancellation may require a
large global corrector.  It does not evaluate the R0.74Y scale-dependent
packet, inversion, winding, or cubic-payment ledger.

N. Burq and I. Moyano,
[*Propagation of smallness and control for heat
equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07402),
arXiv:1912.07402 (2019).

The source extends propagation-of-smallness and spectral-projector estimates
to heat equations and applies them to null controllability from small
observation sets.  It supplies an appropriate neighboring toolbox for a
future quantitative no-go or construction.  R0.74Y has not yet derived from
it the exponent needed to compare corrector cost with \(P_R^M\).

C. Zhang,
[*Quantitative unique continuation for the heat equation with Coulomb
potentials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.07744),
arXiv:1707.07744 (2017).

The paper proves a Hölder-type quantitative unique-continuation estimate in
bounded domains.  Its operator, potential, norms, and observation geometry
are not the R0.74Y common-shear problem.  The source is therefore an
ingredient-level precedent only.
