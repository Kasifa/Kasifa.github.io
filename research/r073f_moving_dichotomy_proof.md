# R0.73F proof: moving-profile dichotomy and fixed-window exponential gain

**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Scope:** the exact periodic row \(\gamma=1/2\), \(\beta=\xi=0\), and
the singular limit \(\varepsilon=|\Lambda|^{-1}\downarrow0\)  
**Evidence class:** exact operator theorem, conditional on the certified
R0.73C eigenvalue and the R0.73E uniform frozen dichotomy

## 1. Statement of the result

Let

\[
 W(d,x)=-\frac12e^{-d}\sin x+\frac14e^{-4d}\sin2x,
 \qquad
 L=-\partial_x^2+\frac14,
 \tag{1.1}
\]

and let

\[
 \widetilde B_\varepsilon(d)
 =\widetilde A(d)-\varepsilon L
 \quad\hbox{on}\quad
 H=L^2(\mathbb T_{2\pi}),
 \qquad
 D(\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d))=H^2_{\rm per}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Here \(\widetilde A(d)\) is the bounded kinetic-space conjugate of the
inviscid row.  R0.73E proved

\[
 \|\widetilde A(d)-\widetilde A(0)\|
 \le C_A d,
 \qquad C_A=\frac{49}{4}.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

For \(s=+1\), let \(U_\varepsilon(t,r)\) be the exact fast-time evolution
family generated by

\[
 \widetilde B_\varepsilon(\varepsilon t)
 =\widetilde B_\varepsilon(0)
  +\widetilde A(\varepsilon t)-\widetilde A(0).
 \tag{1.4}
\]

There are constants

\[
 d_0>0,
 \qquad \varepsilon_0>0,
 \qquad K_1<\infty,
 \qquad \eta>0,
 \qquad \alpha>0.17035,
 \tag{1.5}
\]

such that, for every \(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0\), the evolution on
\(0\le t\le d_0/\varepsilon\) has a finite-dimensional moving unstable
bundle of fixed positive dimension and a complementary stable bundle.  If
\(\widehat P_\varepsilon(t)\) and
\(\widehat Q_\varepsilon(t)=I-\widehat P_\varepsilon(t)\) denote the
corresponding projections, then

\[
 \|U_\varepsilon(t,r)\widehat Q_\varepsilon(r)\|
 \le K_1e^{(\alpha-\eta)(t-r)},
 \qquad 0\le r\le t\le d_0/\varepsilon,
 \tag{1.6}
\]

and the restriction from the moving unstable fiber at \(r\) to that at
\(t\) is invertible, with

\[
 \left\|
 \left(U_\varepsilon(t,r)|_{\widehat P_\varepsilon(r)H}\right)^{-1}
 \widehat P_\varepsilon(t)
 \right\|
 \le K_1e^{-(\alpha+\eta)(t-r)}.
 \tag{1.7}
\]

The word stable in (1.6) is relative to the shift \(\alpha\); the unshifted
complement is not asserted to decay.

For every fixed physical observation window \(D>0\), put

\[
 d_D=\min\{D,d_0\}.
 \tag{1.8}
\]

Then, for both signs of \(\Lambda\),

\[
 \boxed{
 G_{1/2}(\Lambda;D)
 \ge K_1^{-1}
 \exp\left((\alpha+\eta)d_D|\Lambda|\right)
 }
 \tag{1.9}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(|\Lambda|\).  Together with the R0.73B
all-row kinetic upper bound, this gives

\[
 \boxed{
 (\alpha+\eta)d_D
 \le
 \liminf_{|\Lambda|\to\infty}
 \frac{\log G_{1/2}(\Lambda;D)}{|\Lambda|}
 \le
 \limsup_{|\Lambda|\to\infty}
 \frac{\log G_{1/2}(\Lambda;D)}{|\Lambda|}
 \le\frac5{16}.}
 \tag{1.10}
\]

Thus the fixed-window logarithmic gain is \(\Theta(|\Lambda|)\).  Equation
(1.10) does not assert that the normalized logarithm has a limit or identify
its sharp value.

 a=\max\{\operatorname{Re}z:z\in\sigma(\widetilde A(0))\}.
 \tag{2.1}

\[
 a\ge\sigma_*>0.17035.
 \tag{2.2}

\[
 \max\{\beta,0.17035\}<\alpha<a,
 \qquad
 \max\{\beta,0\}<b<\alpha<c<a.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

R0.73E supplies, for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), a
finite-rank top projection \(P_\varepsilon\), with
\(Q_\varepsilon=I-P_\varepsilon\), and constants \(C_b,C_c\) independent

\[
 \|e^{t\widetilde B_\varepsilon(0)}Q_\varepsilon\|
 \le C_be^{bt},
 \qquad t\ge0,
 \tag{2.4}

and

\[
 \|e^{-t\widetilde B_\varepsilon(0)}P_\varepsilon\|
 \le C_ce^{-ct},
 \qquad t\ge0.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

The second expression is only the inverse group on the finite-dimensional top

\[
 C_\varepsilon=\widetilde B_\varepsilon(0)-\alpha I,
 \qquad
 \delta_s=\alpha-b,
 \qquad
 \delta_u=c-\alpha,

 K=\max\{1,C_b,C_c\}.

 \|e^{tC_\varepsilon}Q_\varepsilon\|

 \tag{2.8}

At \(t=0\), (2.8) also gives
\(\|P_\varepsilon\|,\|Q_\varepsilon\|\le K\).

## 3. A roughness lemma that permits a noninvertible stable semigroup

The following lemma is stated with conservative constants.  Sharp roughness
radii are not needed.

**Lemma 3.1.**  Let \(C\) generate an exponentially bounded semigroup on a
complex Banach space \(X\).  Suppose a bounded projection \(P\), with
\(Q=I-P\), commutes with the semigroup, the part on \(PX\) extends to a
group, and

\[
 \|e^{tC}Q\|\le K e^{-\nu t},
 \qquad
 \|e^{-tC}P\|\le K e^{-\nu t},
 \qquad t\ge0,
 \tag{3.1}
\]

where \(K\ge1\) and \(\nu>0\).  Let
\(V:\mathbb R\to\mathcal B(X)\) be norm-continuous and bounded, and put

\[
 \rho=\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}\|V(t)\|.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

If

\[
 \rho<\frac{\nu}{16K^2},
 \tag{3.3}
\]

then the mild evolution generated by \(C+V(t)\) has an exponential
dichotomy on \(\mathbb R\).  Its stable and unstable fibers are graphs over
\(QX\) and \(PX\), respectively.  With

\[
 \eta=\frac\nu2,
 \qquad
 q=K\rho\left(\frac1{\nu-\eta}+\frac1{\nu+\eta}\right)
 =\frac{8K\rho}{3\nu}<1,
 \tag{3.4}
\]

one may choose a dichotomy prefactor depending only on \(K,\nu,\rho\), and
the forward stable and backward unstable rates are both \(\eta\).

### Proof of Lemma 3.1: stable graph

Fix \(s\in\mathbb R\) and \(\xi\in QX\).  On the Banach space of continuous
functions \(x:[s,\infty)\to X\) with norm

\[
 \|x\|_{s,+}
 =\sup_{t\ge s}e^{\eta(t-s)}\|x(t)\|,
 \tag{3.5}
\]

consider

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 x(t)={}&e^{(t-s)C}\xi
 +\int_s^t e^{(t-r)C}QV(r)x(r)\,dr\\
 &-\int_t^\infty e^{(t-r)C}PV(r)x(r)\,dr.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.6}
\]

The last integral uses only negative time on \(PX\).  No inverse on \(QX\)
is used.  Equation (3.1) gives

\[
 \|\mathcal T x-\mathcal T y\|_{s,+}
 \le q\|x-y\|_{s,+}.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

Indeed, the forward \(Q\) integral contributes
\(K\rho/(\nu-\eta)\), while the backward \(P\) integral contributes
\(K\rho/(\nu+\eta)\).  Hence (3.6) has a unique fixed point and

\[
 \|x\|_{s,+}\le\frac{K}{1-q}\|\xi\|.
 \tag{3.8}
\]

At \(t=s\),

\[
 x(s)=\xi+\Phi_s\xi,
 \qquad
 \Phi_s\xi
 =-\int_s^\infty e^{(s-r)C}PV(r)x(r)\,dr\in PX,
 \tag{3.9}
\]

and

\[
 \|\Phi_s\|
 \le\frac{K^2\rho}{(\nu+\eta)(1-q)}.
 \tag{3.10}
\]

Define

\[
 E^s(s)=\{\xi+\Phi_s\xi:\xi\in QX\}.
 \tag{3.11}
\]

### Proof of Lemma 3.1: unstable graph

For \(\zeta\in PX\), solve on \(( -\infty,s]\), with norm

\[
 \|x\|_{s,-}
 =\sup_{t\le s}e^{\eta(s-t)}\|x(t)\|,
 \tag{3.12}
\]

the equation

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 x(t)={}&e^{(t-s)C}\zeta
 +\int_{-\infty}^t e^{(t-r)C}QV(r)x(r)\,dr\\
 &-\int_t^s e^{(t-r)C}PV(r)x(r)\,dr.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.13}
\]

The same contraction constant \(q\) applies.  The unique solution satisfies

\[
 \|x\|_{s,-}\le\frac{K}{1-q}\|\zeta\|,
 \tag{3.14}
\]

and

\[
 x(s)=\Psi_s\zeta+\zeta,
 \qquad
 \Psi_s\zeta
 =\int_{-\infty}^s e^{(s-r)C}QV(r)x(r)\,dr\in QX,
 \tag{3.15}
\]

with

\[
 \|\Psi_s\|
 \le\frac{K^2\rho}{(\nu+\eta)(1-q)}.
 \tag{3.16}
\]

Define

\[
 E^u(s)=\{\Psi_s\zeta+\zeta:\zeta\in PX\}.
 \tag{3.17}
\]

### Proof of Lemma 3.1: complementarity and invariance

Under (3.3), (3.4) gives \(q<1/6\), while (3.10) and (3.16) give

\[
 \sup_s\|\Phi_s\|\,\sup_s\|\Psi_s\|<1.
 \tag{3.18}
\]

The elementary graph equations then show

\[
 X=E^s(s)\oplus E^u(s)
 \tag{3.19}
\]

with uniformly bounded projections.  To see this explicitly, if
\(x=q_0+p_0\) in \(QX\oplus PX\), solve

\[
 (I-\Phi_s\Psi_s)\zeta=p_0-\Phi_sq_0,
 \qquad
 \xi=q_0-\Psi_s\zeta.
 \tag{3.20}
\]

The Neumann series is valid by (3.18), and
\(x=(\xi+\Phi_s\xi)+(\Psi_s\zeta+\zeta)\).

Fixed-point uniqueness shows that the mild evolution carries
\(E^s(s)\) into \(E^s(t)\).  The backward construction does the same for
\(E^u\), and it shows that the evolution from \(E^u(s)\) onto \(E^u(t)\)
is bijective.  More explicitly, splitting either fixed-point equation at an
intermediate time gives the usual variation-of-constants formula for
\(C+V(t)\).  Restricting a stable fixed point to \([t_0,\infty)\), or an
unstable fixed point to \(( -\infty,t_0]\), gives the corresponding graph
equation based at \(t_0\); uniqueness gives invariance.  Conversely, the
backward fixed point through any vector in \(E^u(t_0)\) supplies its unique
preimage at every earlier time.  The uniform contraction and norm continuity
of \(V\), together with dominated convergence in (3.6) and (3.13), show that
\(s\mapsto\Phi_s\) and \(s\mapsto\Psi_s\) are norm-continuous.  Thus the
graph projections are norm-continuous.  If \(\widehat Q(s)\) and
\(\widehat P(s)\) are the
projections onto these two graphs, (3.8), (3.14), and
\(\|P\|,\|Q\|\le K\) give a common constant \(K_1\) such that

\[
 \|V_C(t,s)\widehat Q(s)\|
 \le K_1e^{-\eta(t-s)},
 \tag{3.21}
\]

and

\[
 \left\|
 \left(V_C(t,s)|_{E^u(s)}\right)^{-1}\widehat P(t)
 \right\|
 \le K_1e^{-\eta(t-s)},
 \qquad t\ge s.
 \tag{3.22}
\]

If \(PX\) has finite rank \(m\), (3.17) shows directly that every unstable
fiber has dimension \(m\).  This proves the lemma.  Notice that neither the
proof nor the statement requires a group on the stable space. \(\square\)

## 4. Uniform instantaneous gap and the exact heat profile

Choose \(d_0>0\) so small that

\[
 C_A d_0<\frac{\nu}{16K^2}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

It is harmless to shrink \(d_0\) further so that
\((\alpha+\eta)d_0<5/16\).

### 4.1 A common instantaneous spectral strip and contour

For each fixed \(0\le d\le d_0\), apply Lemma 3.1 to the constant bounded
perturbation

\[
 V_d(t)=\widetilde A(d)-\widetilde A(0).
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The resulting invariant graphs are independent of \(t\).  They therefore
give the autonomous generator
\(\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d)-\alpha I\) a dichotomy with stable and
unstable rates \(\eta\), uniformly in \(d\) and small \(\varepsilon\).  The
unstable space has rank \(m\).  This dichotomy identifies its own spectral
split.  On the stable part, the positive-time Laplace integral gives the
resolvent for every shifted \(z\) with \(\operatorname{Re}z>-\eta\).  On
the finite-dimensional unstable part, the negative-time integral gives the
resolvent for every \(\operatorname{Re}z<\eta\).  Hence the complete
generator has the uniform resolvent strip

\[
 \boxed{
 \{z:|\operatorname{Re}z-\alpha|<\eta\}
 \subset\rho(\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d)).}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

The stable spectrum lies in
\(\{\operatorname{Re}z\le\alpha-\eta\}\), while the \(m\)-dimensional
unstable spectrum lies in
\(\{\operatorname{Re}z\ge\alpha+\eta\}\).  The invariant graph projection
is therefore the unique Riesz projection for the latter separated spectral
part.  Denote it by \(P_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\), and put
\(Q_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)=I-P_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\).  This
argument does not rely on spectral mapping as an unstated extra hypothesis.

This separation can be represented by one fixed contour.  Put

\[
 M_A=\sup_{0\le d\le d_0}\|\widetilde A(d)\|<\infty.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Choose \(R>2M_A\) and \(J>2M_A\), and let \(\Gamma\) be the positively
oriented rectangle with vertical sides \(\operatorname{Re}z=\alpha\) and
\(\operatorname{Re}z=R\), and horizontal sides
\(\operatorname{Im}z=\pm J\).  The constant-perturbation dichotomy gives a
uniform resolvent bound on the left side.  If
\(C_{\varepsilon,d}=\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d)-\alpha I\), then

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (i\tau-C_{\varepsilon,d})^{-1}
 ={}&\int_0^\infty e^{-i\tau t}
 e^{tC_{\varepsilon,d}}Q_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\,dt\\
 &-\int_{-\infty}^0 e^{-i\tau t}
 e^{tC_{\varepsilon,d}}P_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\,dt,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4.5}
\]

where the second integral again uses only the finite-dimensional unstable
group.  Therefore

\[
 \sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}
 \|(\alpha+i\tau-\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d))^{-1}\|
 \le\frac{2K_1}{\eta}.
 \tag{4.6}
\]

On the other three sides, factor

\[
 z-\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d)
 =(z+\varepsilon L)
 \left[I-(z+\varepsilon L)^{-1}\widetilde A(d)\right].
 \tag{4.7}
\]

The right side uses
\(\|(z+\varepsilon L)^{-1}\|\le(\operatorname{Re}z)^{-1}\), and the
horizontal sides use
\(\|(z+\varepsilon L)^{-1}\|\le|\operatorname{Im}z|^{-1}\).  Neumann
series therefore give one contour-resolvent bound for all
\(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0\) and \(0\le d\le d_0\).  Every spectral
point to the right of \(\alpha\) lies inside \(\Gamma\): compactness of the
viscous resolvent makes that part discrete, while an eigenvector identity
gives
\(|\operatorname{Im}z|\le M_A\) and
\(\operatorname{Re}z\le M_A\).

Consequently, the same projection is given by the fixed-contour formula

\[
 P_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)
 =\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma
 (z-\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d))^{-1}\,dz
 \tag{4.8}
\]

It has rank \(m\) and a uniform norm bound.  The exact finite Fourier profile
makes \(d\mapsto\widetilde A(d)\) norm-\(C^1\).  Differentiating the fixed
contour formula gives

\[
 \partial_dP_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)
 =\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma
 R_{\varepsilon,d}(z)\widetilde A'(d)
 R_{\varepsilon,d}(z)\,dz,
 \tag{4.9}
\]

where

\[
 R_{\varepsilon,d}(z)
 =(z-\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d))^{-1}.
\]

Hence

\[
 \sup_{\substack{0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0\\0\le d\le d_0}}
 \left(
 \|P_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\|
 +\|\partial_dP_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\|
 \right)<\infty.
 \tag{4.10}
\]

This closes the uniform instantaneous contour in \(\mathcal B(H)\).  It
does not give an unscaled \(H^2\) graph-norm transport theorem, and the
instantaneous projection need not equal the dynamical projection constructed
below.

### 4.2 The clamped exact moving profile

For a physical length
\(0<d\le d_0\), define the clamped slow time

\[
 \chi_{\varepsilon,d}(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&t\le0,\\
 \varepsilon t,&0\le t\le d/\varepsilon,\\
 d,&t\ge d/\varepsilon,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4.11}
\]

and the norm-continuous bounded perturbation

\[
 D_{\varepsilon,d}(t)
 =\widetilde A(\chi_{\varepsilon,d}(t))-
  \widetilde A(0).
 \tag{4.12}
\]

By (1.3),

\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathbb R}\|D_{\varepsilon,d}(t)\|
 \le C_A d\le C_A d_0<\frac{\nu}{16K^2}.
 \tag{4.13}
\]

For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), the generator
\(C_\varepsilon\) has domain \(H^2_{\rm per}\), while
\(D_{\varepsilon,d}(t)\) is bounded on \(H\).  The bounded
nonautonomous perturbation theorem, equivalently the convergent Volterra
series, gives a unique exponentially bounded mild evolution family.  The
unbounded term \(-\varepsilon L\) never leaves the fixed generator.  Lemma
3.1 therefore applies with constants independent of \(\varepsilon\) and of
the fast interval length \(d/\varepsilon\).

The clamped extension is only a proof device.  On
\(0\le t\le d/\varepsilon\), it is exactly the physical profile in (1.4).
No assertion is made about the artificially constant coefficient after that
time.

There is one useful exact initial-time identity.  In the unstable graph
formula (3.15) at \(s=0\), the integrand contains
\(D_{\varepsilon,d}(r)=0\) for all \(r\le0\).  Hence

\[
 \Psi_0=0,
 \qquad
 E^u_{\varepsilon,d}(0)=P_\varepsilon H.
 \tag{4.14}
\]

Thus the moving unstable bundle starts exactly from the frozen top space; it
is not merely close to it.

Undoing the shift gives

\[
 U_\varepsilon(t,r)
 =e^{\alpha(t-r)}V_{C,\varepsilon,d}(t,r).
 \tag{4.15}
\]

Equations (3.21)--(3.22) become (1.6)--(1.7).

## 5. Endpoint lower bound

Let \(0<d\le d_0\), put \(T=d/\varepsilon\), and choose any unit vector

\[
 v_\varepsilon\in P_\varepsilon H.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

By (4.14), this is a unit vector in the moving unstable fiber at time zero.
Set

\[
 y=V_{C,\varepsilon,d}(T,0)v_\varepsilon
 \in E^u_{\varepsilon,d}(T).
 \tag{5.2}
\]

The backward estimate (3.22) gives

\[
 1=\|v_\varepsilon\|
 \le K_1e^{-\eta T}\|y\|,
 \tag{5.3}
\]

so

\[
 \|U_\varepsilon(T,0)v_\varepsilon\|
 =e^{\alpha T}\|y\|
 \ge K_1^{-1}e^{(\alpha+\eta)T}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

The initial vector may depend on \(\varepsilon\).  This is exactly what an
operator-norm lower bound permits.  In fact, (5.4) holds for every unit vector
in the frozen top space, not only for a selected eigenline.

## 6. Return to physical time and the complete row

For a prescribed observation window \(D>0\), apply Section 5 with

\[
 d=d_D=\min\{D,d_0\}.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Since \(\varepsilon=|\Lambda|^{-1}\), (5.4) becomes

\[
 \|U_\varepsilon(d_D/\varepsilon,0)\|
 \ge K_1^{-1}
 e^{(\alpha+\eta)d_D|\Lambda|}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

The physical gain takes the supremum over \(0\le d\le D\), so the time
\(d_D\) is admissible even when \(D>d_0\).  This is why the theorem gives a
fixed-observation-window lower law for every \(D>0\), although the endpoint
statement at a prescribed \(D>d_0\) remains open.

On the row \(\beta=\xi=0\), \(\gamma=1/2\), the Squire forcing coefficient
\(i\xi\Lambda\) vanishes.  Zero initial Squire vorticity remains zero, and
the R0.73E unitary kinetic identity transfers (6.2) isometrically to the
complete OS--Squire row.  Complex conjugation maps \(s=+1\) to \(s=-1\)
because \(W\) and \(L\) are real.  This proves (1.9) for both signs.

R0.73B proved the complete-row estimate

\[
 \|U(d,0)\|\le e^{5|\Lambda|/16},
 \tag{6.3}
\]

uniformly for every \(d\ge0\).  Restricting to the present row and taking
the supremum over \(0\le d\le D\) therefore gives the same upper bound for
\(G_{1/2}(\Lambda;D)\), so (1.10) follows after taking logarithms and
dividing by \(|\Lambda|\).

## 7. Why frozen spectra alone would not prove the theorem

The proof uses the uniform semigroup constants in (2.4)--(2.5), not merely
the locations of the frozen spectra.  Two exact finite-dimensional examples
show why both shortcuts are invalid.

### 7.1 A spectral gap without a uniform dichotomy prefactor

Let \(N=\varepsilon^{-1}\) and

\[
 C_N=\operatorname{diag}(a,D_N),
 \qquad
 D_N=\begin{pmatrix}-N&N^2\\0&-N\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad a>0.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

The family is constant in time, smooth, and has the fixed spectrum
\(\{a,-N\}\) with a growing gap.  Its top projection is constant and
orthogonal.  Nevertheless,

\[
 e^{tD_N}=e^{-Nt}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&N^2t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \tag{7.2}
\]

so at \(t=N^{-1}\),

\[
 \|e^{tD_N}\|\ge \frac{N}{e}.
 \tag{7.3}
\]

No \(\varepsilon\)-uniform dichotomy prefactor follows from the spectral gap.
R0.73E excludes this failure for the present operator through its uniform
reduced-resolvent and Bromwich estimates.

### 7.2 Positive instantaneous spectral abscissa without net growth

Fix \(D>0\) and define on \(\mathbb C^4\)

\[
 \lambda_j(d)
 =\cos\left(\frac{2\pi d}{D}-\frac{2\pi j}{3}\right)-\frac14,
 \qquad j=0,1,2,
 \tag{7.4}
\]

and

\[
 B(d)=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_0(d),\lambda_1(d),
 \lambda_2(d),-2).
 \tag{7.5}
\]

This family is normal, smooth, and has a fixed three-dimensional cluster
separated from \(-2\).  At every \(d\), one of the three phases lies within
\(\pi/3\) of zero, so

\[
 \max_j\lambda_j(d)\ge\frac14.
 \tag{7.6}
\]

But each cosine integrates to zero over the full interval and the first three
components of the slow evolution all equal

\[
 \exp\left(-\frac{D}{4\varepsilon}\right)
 \tag{7.7}
\]

at the endpoint.  The instantaneous positive direction rotates among the
cluster components.  Pointwise positive spectral abscissa therefore does not
imply a fixed-window growing direction.  The backward estimate for the entire
top block in (2.5) is the stronger input used in R0.73F.

## 8. Exact boundary of the result

The proof closes the instantaneous contour and norm-\(C^1\) Riesz projection
on the local interval \(0\le d\le d_0\).  It also closes a moving
**dynamical** unstable bundle and the gain law for every fixed observation
window.  It does not prove that the two bundles are equal.  The following
remain open:

- a uniform instantaneous contour beyond \([0,d_0]\);
- equality of \(P_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\) and the dynamical projection;
- a Kato connection preserving the unscaled \(H^2\) graph norm;
- a sharp exponent or existence of the limit in (1.10);
- an endpoint law at an arbitrarily prescribed \(D>d_0\).

The theorem still concerns one linear invariant row along a family of
background shears whose amplitude tends to infinity.  It supplies no
complete all-row OS--Squire \(A_2\) direct-sum closure or matching fixed-window
lower bound across all rows, no nonlinear mode-convolution closure, no
single-solution singularity, and no conclusion about the Clay problem.
