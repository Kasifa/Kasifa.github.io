## 0. Direct decision

R0.73F closes the local moving-profile and fixed-observation-window gates left
open by R0.73E for one exact linear Fourier row.  Let

\[
 W(d,x)=-\frac12e^{-d}\sin x+\frac14e^{-4d}\sin2x,
 \qquad L=-\partial_x^2+\frac14,
 \tag{0.1}
\]

and let

\[
 \widetilde B_\varepsilon(d)
 =\widetilde A(d)-\varepsilon L
 \quad\hbox{on}\quad H=L^2(\mathbb T_{2\pi}).
 \tag{0.2}
\]

The frozen top cluster at \(d=0\) has the R0.73E uniform relative
dichotomy.  After a shift by a number \(\alpha>0.17035\), both the stable
forward semigroup and the finite-dimensional unstable backward group decay
with one rate \(\nu>0\) and one prefactor \(K\), uniformly in small
\(\varepsilon\).  The exact heat-profile drift satisfies

\[
 \|\widetilde A(d)-\widetilde A(0)\|
 \le \frac{49}{4}d.
 \tag{0.3}
\]

A self-contained Lyapunov--Perron graph argument shows that a bounded
nonautonomous perturbation of size

\[
 \rho<\frac{\nu}{16K^2}
 \tag{0.4}
\]

preserves the dichotomy even though the parabolic stable part is not
invertible.  Hence there is an existential \(d_0>0\), independent of
\(\varepsilon\), on which the exact moving profile has a finite-dimensional
dynamical unstable bundle.  If

\[
 d_D=\min\{D,d_0\},
 \tag{0.5}
\]

then every fixed observation window \(D>0\) obeys, for both signs of
\(\Lambda\),

\[
 \boxed{
 G_{1/2}(\Lambda;D)
 \ge K_1^{-1}
 \exp\!\left((\alpha+\eta)d_D|\Lambda|\right),
 \qquad \eta=\frac\nu2.}
 \tag{0.6}
\]

The R0.73B complete-row kinetic upper bound gives

\[
 \boxed{
 (\alpha+\eta)d_D
 \le\liminf_{|\Lambda|\to\infty}
 \frac{\log G_{1/2}(\Lambda;D)}{|\Lambda|}
 \le\limsup_{|\Lambda|\to\infty}
 \frac{\log G_{1/2}(\Lambda;D)}{|\Lambda|}
 \le\frac5{16}.}
 \tag{0.7}
\]

Thus the fixed-window logarithmic gain is
\(\Theta(|\Lambda|)\) on this row.  The result does not identify a sharp
exponent, give an endpoint law at every prescribed \(D>d_0\), close the
all-row OS--Squire \(A_2\) direct sum, control nonlinear mode convolution,
or resolve any part of the Clay regularity alternative.

## 1. Exact row and inherited frozen split

The physical kinetic vorticity norm is unitarily represented on \(H\) by

\[
 U=2L^{-1/2}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

In fast time \(t=|\Lambda|d\), the positive-sign evolution is generated by

\[
 \widetilde B_\varepsilon(\varepsilon t)
 =\widetilde B_\varepsilon(0)
 +\widetilde A(\varepsilon t)-\widetilde A(0).
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Let

\[
 a=\max\{\operatorname{Re}z:z\in\sigma(\widetilde A(0))\}.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The certified inviscid eigenvalue from R0.73C implies
\(a\ge\sigma_*>0.17035\), without proving that the certified eigenvalue is
itself simple or rightmost.  If \(\beta<a\) is the spectral abscissa after
removing the complete top cluster, choose

\[
 \max\{\beta,0.17035\}<\alpha<a,
 \qquad \max\{\beta,0\}<b<\alpha<c<a.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

R0.73E supplies projections \(P_\varepsilon\) and
\(Q_\varepsilon=I-P_\varepsilon\), and constants independent of small
\(\varepsilon\), such that

\[
 \|e^{t\widetilde B_\varepsilon(0)}Q_\varepsilon\|
 \le C_be^{bt},
 \qquad
 \|e^{-t\widetilde B_\varepsilon(0)}P_\varepsilon\|
 \le C_ce^{-ct}.
 \tag{1.5}
\]

The negative-time expression occurs only on the finite-dimensional top
block.  It does not assert that the stable parabolic semigroup is invertible.
With

\[
 C_\varepsilon=\widetilde B_\varepsilon(0)-\alpha I,
 \quad
 \nu=\min\{\alpha-b,c-\alpha\},
 \quad
 K=\max\{1,C_b,C_c\},
 \tag{1.6}
\]

both shifted estimates have decay rate \(\nu\).

## 2. Bounded-perturbation roughness with a noninvertible stable part

For a norm-continuous bounded perturbation
\(V:\mathbb R\to\mathcal B(H)\), put

\[
 \rho=\sup_t\|V(t)\|,
 \qquad \eta=\frac\nu2.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

The stable graph over \(QH\) is the unique fixed point of

\[
\begin{aligned}
 x(t)={}&e^{(t-s)C}\xi
 +\int_s^t e^{(t-r)C}QV(r)x(r)\,dr\\
 &-\int_t^\infty e^{(t-r)C}PV(r)x(r)\,dr,
 \qquad t\ge s,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

and the unstable graph over \(PH\) is the unique fixed point of

\[
\begin{aligned}
 x(t)={}&e^{(t-s)C}\zeta
 +\int_{-\infty}^t e^{(t-r)C}QV(r)x(r)\,dr\\
 &-\int_t^s e^{(t-r)C}PV(r)x(r)\,dr,
 \qquad t\le s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The contraction constant is

\[
 q=K\rho\left(\frac1{\nu-\eta}+\frac1{\nu+\eta}\right)
 =\frac{8K\rho}{3\nu}
 <\frac1{6K}\le\frac16.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

The graph norms are below \(1/20\), so the stable and unstable graphs are
uniformly complementary.  Splitting either fixed-point formula at an
intermediate time proves invariance.  The backward fixed point supplies the
unique preimage on the unstable bundle; no inverse is introduced on the
stable bundle.  Norm continuity follows from the uniform contraction and
dominated convergence.  The unstable rank equals
\(\operatorname{rank}P_\varepsilon\).

This argument is the analytic core of R0.73F.  General roughness theorems are
literature precedent, not substituted for its model-specific hypotheses.

## 3. Local instantaneous strip and norm-C1 Riesz projection

Choose \(d_0>0\) so that

\[
 \frac{49}{4}d_0<\frac{\nu}{16K^2}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Applying the roughness lemma to each constant perturbation
\(\widetilde A(d)-\widetilde A(0)\), \(0\le d\le d_0\), gives a uniform
strip

\[
 \{z:|\operatorname{Re}z-\alpha|<\eta\}
 \subset\rho(\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d)).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The stable positive-time and unstable negative-time Laplace formulas give the
left-line resolvent bound directly.  On the other sides of a fixed rectangle,
the factorization

\[
 z-\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d)
 =(z+\varepsilon L)
 \left[I-(z+\varepsilon L)^{-1}\widetilde A(d)\right]
 \tag{3.3}
\]

gives uniform Neumann bounds.  One contour \(\Gamma\) therefore defines the
complete instantaneous unstable projection,

\[
 P_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)
 =\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma
 (z-\widetilde B_\varepsilon(d))^{-1}\,dz,
 \tag{3.4}
\]

and fixed-contour differentiation yields

\[
 \sup_{\varepsilon,d}
 \left(\|P_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\|
 +\|\partial_dP_\varepsilon^{\rm inst}(d)\|\right)<\infty.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

This is a theorem in \(\mathcal B(H)\).  It neither gives a uniform map into
unscaled \(H^2\) nor identifies the instantaneous Riesz projection with the
dynamical projection below.

## 4. The exact moving dynamical bundle

For \(0<d\le d_0\), clamp the slow profile on the full fast-time line by

\[
 \chi_{\varepsilon,d}(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&t\le0,\\
 \varepsilon t,&0\le t\le d/\varepsilon,\\
 d,&t\ge d/\varepsilon.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
 D_{\varepsilon,d}(t)
 =\widetilde A(\chi_{\varepsilon,d}(t))-\widetilde A(0)
 \tag{4.2}
\]

is a bounded norm-continuous perturbation satisfying the roughness radius on
all of \(\mathbb R\).  The unbounded operator
\(-\varepsilon L\) remains inside the fixed generator, so the bounded
nonautonomous perturbation theorem gives the exact mild evolution.  On
\(0\le t\le d/\varepsilon\), the clamped coefficient is exactly the heat
profile; its constant continuation after that interval is only a proof
device.

At time zero the unstable graph correction is exactly zero because the
perturbation vanishes for every negative fast time:

\[
 E^u_{\varepsilon,d}(0)=P_\varepsilon H.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

If \(\widehat P_\varepsilon(t)\) denotes the moving unstable projection,
then the unshifted evolution satisfies

\[
 \left\|
 \left(U_\varepsilon(t,r)|_{\widehat P_\varepsilon(r)H}\right)^{-1}
 \widehat P_\varepsilon(t)
 \right\|
 \le K_1e^{-(\alpha+\eta)(t-r)}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

## 5. Fixed-window lower law and matching exponential order

Take any unit vector \(v_\varepsilon\in P_\varepsilon H\).  Equation
(4.3) puts it in the moving unstable fiber at time zero.  At
\(T=d/\varepsilon\), (4.4) gives

\[
 \|U_\varepsilon(T,0)v_\varepsilon\|
 \ge K_1^{-1}e^{(\alpha+\eta)T}.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

The initial vector may depend on \(\varepsilon\); that is permitted in an
operator-norm lower bound.  For \(D>d_0\), the certified lower bound occurs at
the admissible interior time \(d_0\), not necessarily at the endpoint \(D\).

On the row \(\xi=0\), the Squire forcing coefficient vanishes.  Zero initial
Squire vorticity remains zero, the kinetic transform is exactly unitary, and
complex conjugation transfers the positive-sign result to negative
\(\Lambda\).  This proves (0.6).  R0.73B supplies the all-time upper sentinel

\[
 \|U(d,0)\|\le e^{5|\Lambda|/16},
 \tag{5.2}
\]

which gives (0.7) after restricting to this row and taking the observation
supremum.

## 6. Two invalid shortcuts

### 6.1 Spectral separation without a uniform prefactor

Let \(N=\varepsilon^{-1}\) and

\[
 D_N=\begin{pmatrix}-N&N^2\\0&-N\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Despite a growing spectral gap and zero coefficient drift,

\[
 \|e^{D_N/N}\|\ge\frac{N}{e}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

A frozen spectral gap and a common domain do not give an
\(\varepsilon\)-uniform dichotomy prefactor.  R0.73E supplies the stronger
uniform semigroup input actually used here.

### 6.2 Positive instantaneous abscissa without net growth

For \(j=0,1,2\), define

\[
 \lambda_j(d)=
 \cos\left(\frac{2\pi d}{D}-\frac{2\pi j}{3}\right)-\frac14.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

The diagonal family with these three entries and a fourth entry \(-2\) has
pointwise spectral abscissa at least \(1/4\).  Nevertheless, each leading
component equals

\[
 \exp\left(-\frac{D}{4\varepsilon}\right)
 \tag{6.4}
\]

at the endpoint.  A positive instantaneous eigenvalue can rotate among
directions; the complete-block backward estimate is the decisive stronger
input.

## 7. Literature boundary

Latushkin--Schnaubelt give the Banach-space convention for an exponential
dichotomy with a noninvertible stable part and a backward-invertible unstable
part.  Coppel gives the classical finite-dimensional matrix roughness
precedent.  Schnaubelt's analytic-semigroup theorem requires its full
conditions (P1), (ED), and \(L\|\phi\|_1<1\); it is not a theorem that small
\(\mathcal L(X)\) oscillation alone suffices.

Schmid's Proposition 2.17 assumes the comparison evolution and proves its
intertwining; his Theorem 3.1 uses Condition 2.9 with \(\omega=0\),
\(P\in W_*^{2,1}\), and a uniform gap.  Joye additionally assumes a common
dense domain, complex-neighborhood analyticity, finite separated spectral
values of constant algebraic multiplicity, and a complementary
contraction-semigroup shift.  None of these adiabatic results is invoked as a
black box for the present singular graph-domain family.

No originality or priority claim is made for the abstract roughness lemma.
The model-specific statement is the verified splice from the R0.73E frozen
dichotomy and the exact \(49/4\) drift bound to the heat-profile
fixed-window lower law.  A broader hydrodynamic-instability literature search
and expert review remain necessary before journal submission.

## 8. Finite diagnostic and formal figure boundary

The archived Fourier calculations sample the exact finite matrix family at a
physical diagnostic length \(d_{\rm diag}=10^{-2}\), several viscosities, and
multiple cutoffs.  They compare the full propagator norm with the conorm on a
finite leading block, check step halving and independent matrix assembly, and
render the two exact counterexamples from Section 6.

The diagnostic length is not the existential certified \(d_0\).  Finite
cutoff agreement is not tail control, the finite leading block is not the
continuum top cluster, sampled propagation is not a continuous-time theorem,
and no measured slope is substituted for \(\alpha+\eta\).  The analytic
proof, not the figure, establishes Sections 2--5.

## 9. Research value and exact limit

R0.73E had shown growth beyond every fixed polynomial on an interval whose
physical length shrank to zero.  R0.73F upgrades that statement to an
exponential-in-\(|\Lambda|\) lower law inside every fixed positive
observation window.  It also closes a local instantaneous spectral strip and
a genuinely nonautonomous dynamical bundle without assuming invertibility of
the stable parabolic evolution.

This is a meaningful linear instability theorem for a highly structured
family of exact background shears.  Its direct value for the Millennium
problem remains limited: the theorem varies the background amplitude with
\(|\Lambda|\), permits the maximizing initial direction to vary, and contains
no nonlinear perturbation theorem, frequency-cascade mechanism, a priori
regularity improvement, or singularity construction for one solution.

The next logically distinct gate is a nonlinear one: determine whether this
family-level linear amplification can be embedded in a quantitatively closed
nonlinear instability statement with an explicit topology, lifespan, and
remainder bound.  Complete all-row OS--Squire control remains an alternative
linear gate and must not be silently assumed.
