# R0.72D literature audit

**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Question:** Does an existing theorem construct or control temporal roots of
one Fourier coordinate, their crossing slopes, and the complete normalized
root ledger for a heat-decaying, phase-cancelled shear profile?

## Direct conclusion

The bounded search found no primary source that supplies the R0.72D root
construction or its normalized full-charge lower bound.  Current mixing and
enhanced-dissipation results control spatial norms for fixed or specially
modulated shear profiles.  They do not count time zeros of the scalar target
coordinate \(F_0(t)\), give a lower bound for the crossing slope, or pay the
launch-inclusive root ledger.

The literature is used here for two limited purposes:

1. Rudin--Shapiro sources justify the static phase-flat polynomial input and
   arbitrary-prefix control.
2. Shear-flow sources delimit why a terminal or positive-time mixing theorem
   cannot replace the direct short-time root proof.

## Search boundary

The search emphasized 2023--2026 primary papers on time-dependent shear
mixing, enhanced dissipation, critical layers, phase cancellation, and
Rudin--Shapiro polynomials.  Focused searches combining enhanced dissipation
with temporal zeros, zero crossings, target modes, and Fourier-coordinate
slopes returned no directly matching theorem.  This is a bounded search
conclusion, not an assertion that no related result exists anywhere.

## Remaining literature gaps after R0.72D

1. No cited theorem produces the interior root in Section 3; the construction
   is direct.
2. No cited theorem relates a temporal target slope lower bound to the full
   projected rotational charge.
3. No cited enhanced-dissipation theorem gives constants uniform in the
   changing translated RS profile.
4. No cited result transfers the order-one triangular root ledger to a
   general three-dimensional continuation criterion.
5. The current literature does not decide whether an order-one ceiling holds
   for all triangular solutions or whether a supercritical family exists.
