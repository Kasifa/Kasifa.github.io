# R0.73L literature audit: nonselfadjoint adiabatic tracking

**Status:** bounded primary-source audit complete; claims are limited to the
sources explicitly listed below

**Search date:** 2026-08-31 (Asia/Shanghai)

## 1. Direct literature decision

Adiabatic theorems for nonselfadjoint and unbounded generators are established
in the literature.  R0.73L is therefore not a first general nonselfadjoint
adiabatic theorem.

The issue specific to this project is a two-parameter uniformity problem.
The slow parameter is \(d\), while the same \(\varepsilon\) that sets the
adiabatic time scale also multiplies the unbounded elliptic operator in
\(B_\varepsilon(d)=\widetilde A(d)-\varepsilon L\).  A theorem applied for
each fixed \(\varepsilon\) does not automatically give a comparison constant
that remains bounded as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

## 2. Schmid: general operators with a common domain

Schmid considers densely defined closed operators \(A(t):D\subset X\to X\)
with time-independent domain.  His uniform-gap theorem assumes Kato stability,
\(t\mapsto A(t)\) regular as a map from the graph space to \(X\), and an
associated projection \(P\in W_*^{2,1}\).  It concludes

\[
 \sup_t\|U_\varepsilon(t)-V_\varepsilon(t)\|=O(\varepsilon),
\]

where \(V_\varepsilon\) is generated by
\(A/\varepsilon+[P',P]\) and exactly intertwines the spectral subspaces.

- J. Schmid, [*Adiabatic theorems for general linear operators with
  time-independent domains*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.11213),
  Asymptotic Analysis 113 (2019), 1--64,
  DOI: [10.1142/S0129055X19500144](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129055X19500144).

This is the closest abstract theorem.  It is used as a structural check, not
as a black-box closure: R0.73K proves a uniform \(P_\varepsilon'\) bound but
does not supply a uniform \(P_\varepsilon''\) or a uniform graph-space
constant as \(\varepsilon\to0\).

## 3. Abou Salem--Fröhlich: a nonnormal common-domain theorem

Abou Salem and Fröhlich state a common-domain adiabatic theorem for a simple,
uniformly isolated eigenvalue of a nonnormal generator.  With twice
differentiable Riesz projection and uniformly bounded exact evolution, the
Kato-corrected evolution intertwines the spectral subspaces and differs from
the exact evolution by \(O(1/\tau)\).  Their explicit remark is essential:
if the evolution is only quasi-bounded by \(Me^{\gamma t}\), the comparison
becomes \(Ce^{\tau\gamma}/\tau\), which is unusable on the present growing
selected branch before a relative shift is justified.

- W. K. Abou Salem and J. Fröhlich,
  [*Adiabatic theorems for quantum resonances*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0607054),
  Communications in Mathematical Physics 273 (2007), 651--675.

## 4. Joye: nilpotent obstruction and superadiabatic projectors

Joye treats analytic families with a spectral gap, finitely many isolated
finite-multiplicity spectral blocks, and a contractive complementary
evolution after a scalar shift.  The instantaneous projectors need not be
followed when a selected block contains a nonzero nilpotent; superadiabatic
projectors are constructed instead, and the block evolution can carry a
subexponential factor \(e^{c/\varepsilon^\beta}\).  The factor disappears on
a diagonalizable rank-one block.

- A. Joye,
  [*General adiabatic evolution with a gap condition*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0608059),
  Communications in Mathematical Physics 275 (2007), 139--162.

This explains why the R0.73I Jordan counterexample is genuine and why the
R0.73K algebraic simplicity result is necessary.  It does not itself prove
the required \(\varepsilon\)-uniform complementary evolution for this
singular viscous family.

## 5. Avron--Fraas--Graf--Grech: contracting evolutions

Avron, Fraas, Graf, and Grech work with generators of contraction semigroups
on a common dense domain.  In the gap case they require a direct-sum
decomposition by kernel and range and a sufficiently smooth projection; their
slow-manifold expansion gives an \(O(\delta)\) correction for a separate
adiabatic parameter \(\delta\).  Without a gap the general conclusion is
qualitative rather than a universal rate.

- J. E. Avron, M. Fraas, G. M. Graf, and P. Grech,
  [*Adiabatic theorems for generators of contracting evolutions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.4661),
  Communications in Mathematical Physics 314 (2012), 163--191.

The normalized family \(B_\varepsilon-\lambda_\varepsilon\) has not been
proved contractive in one common norm.  Shifting by a coarse full-space growth
bound would insert an exponential much larger than the selected action and
therefore does not close R0.73L.

## 6. Kato and finite-dimensional nonselfadjoint warnings

Kato's original theorem is the source of the parallel-transport commutator,
but its classical formulation concerns the unitary/self-adjoint quantum
setting.  The idea, not the original theorem's hypotheses, is used here.

- T. Kato,
  [*On the adiabatic theorem of quantum mechanics*](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpsj1946/5/6/5_6_435/_article),
  Journal of the Physical Society of Japan 5 (1950), 435--439.

Nenciu and Rasche show in a two-level nonselfadjoint model that a naive
adiabatic statement can fail, while the least dissipative branch has a more
favorable expansion.  This finite-dimensional paper is not a PDE theorem,
but it is a relevant warning against treating a nonnormal spectral gap as a
complete dynamical estimate.

- G. Nenciu and G. Rasche,
  [*On the adiabatic theorem for nonself-adjoint Hamiltonians*](https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/25/21/027),
  Journal of Physics A 25 (1992), 5741--5751.

## 7. Exact gap left by the literature

The primary sources establish the following boundary.

1. A frozen estimate
   \(\|e^{tB_\varepsilon(d)}Q_\varepsilon(d)\|
   \le C e^{0.12t}\) does not by itself imply a moving-complement estimate;
   arbitrary products of nonnormal frozen semigroups can grow.
2. Schmid's direct \(O(\delta)\) comparison requires stability of the full
   ordered family and second-order projection regularity.  R0.73K supplies
   neither with constants already verified uniformly in viscosity.
3. Joye's stronger analytic theorem requires a uniform complex-time analytic
   framework and a contractive complementary block after a scalar shift.
4. No source checked states the exact two-parameter conclusion needed here,
   where the viscosity and the inverse slow time are the same parameter.

Within the sealed model, R0.73L supplies the required estimate directly.  Its
fixed-fast-time block lemma proves the moving-complement estimate from the
model-specific bounded drift, the frozen relative decay, and exact Kato fiber
intertwining.  It therefore needs only a uniform \(P_\varepsilon'\) bound and
does not claim a general replacement for the cited abstract theorems.

## 8. Evidence-supported wording

The strongest currently admissible description is:

> In the sealed periodic shear model, R0.73L proves a profile-specific,
> parameter-uniform adiabatic estimate for a singularly viscous rank-one
> branch, using a fixed-fast-time block argument to avoid unproved uniform
> graph-norm and second-projection-derivative assumptions.

The following wording is not supported:

- “the first nonselfadjoint adiabatic theorem”;
- “a direct corollary of Kato's theorem”;
- “a superadiabatic expansion”;
- “an explicit prefactor limit”;
- “a nonlinear Navier--Stokes instability theorem”;
- “a solution of the Clay problem.”
