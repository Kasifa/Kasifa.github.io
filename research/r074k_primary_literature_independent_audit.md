## 3. Theorem-scope audit

### S1 — Bedrossian--Coti Zelati: PASS

The paper fixes a shear profile (u=u(y)) and studies the linear semigroups
generated by full- and transverse-diffusion drift--diffusion equations on
\(\mathbb T^2\) and on \(\mathbb T\times[0,1]\).  Theorem 1.1 gives
mode-by-mode \(L^2\) semigroup decay for nonzero streamwise Fourier modes;
Remark 1.2 gives Gevrey/hypoelliptic regularization; and the proof explicitly
uses localized spectral-gap inequalities and a hypocoercive augmented
energy.  No theorem conclusion is a signed radial collar integral.

### S2 — Albritton--Beekie--Novack: PASS

The main equations have a fixed profile (b=b(y)).  Theorems 1.1 and 1.2
give enhanced \(L^2\) decay and Gevrey-in-(x) smoothing for periodic,
Dirichlet, or Neumann transverse conditions, using bracket/subelliptic
estimates.  Remark 1.4 says the approach can be generalized to shears that
vary on the enhanced-dissipation timescale.  That remark is not a stated
time-dependent theorem, and it supplies neither the frozen
\(R_j\)-dependent family nor a signed finite-window annular observable.
Thus the audited source's deliberately narrower phrase “not in screened
theorem statements” is accurate.

### S3 — Villringer: PASS

Equation (1.1) fixes a smooth periodic shear (u=u(y)) with finitely many
finite-order critical points.  Theorem 1.1 recovers the nonzero-(x)-mode
enhanced-dissipation estimate, and Remark 1.1 records the associated
hypoelliptic/Gevrey regularization.  The proof uses stochastic
characteristics, the Malliavin matrix, and Malliavin integration by parts.
The audited statement that this mechanism is *relevant* to retaining
shear--Brownian dependence is a methodological inference, not an attributed
theorem.  The theorem contains no conditioned periodic-bridge collar bound.

### S4 — Gardner--Liss--Mattingly: PASS

The paper explicitly treats autonomous shear flows.  Its principal torus
argument uses Girsanov's theorem to reduce total-variation contraction to a
quantitative path-control problem.  Theorem 1 covers profiles allowed to
have infinite-order critical points under Assumption 1; Theorem 2 gives
streamline-dependent local decay for the smoother finite-order class; and
Theorem 3 treats radial shears on \(\mathbb R^2\) or the disk.  Radial
geometry and signed measures do occur in the paper, but no theorem estimates
a signed smooth radial-collar flux or preserves the R0.74K calibrated
observable.  Calling this the closest method in the five-paper screen is
reasonable because it is both pathwise and spatially local; it is not a
global literature-ranking claim.

### S5 — Liss--Luan: PASS

The paper fixes a parallel shear (b=b(y)) on \(\mathbb T^2\).  Theorem 1.1
proves the sharp uniform-in-diffusivity decay in
\(\ell_k^2(W_y^{-1,\infty})\) from
\(\ell_k^2(W_y^{1,1})\); Remark 1.1 identifies the latter regularity as the
weakest required for the zero-diffusion integration-by-parts rate.  Theorem
1.2 gives a second mixing topology, and the proofs start from a stochastic
representation.  These are mixing-norm estimates, not a signed calibrated
collar-flux estimate.

## 5. Audit of the bounded non-hit

**Verdict:** `PASS`.

The target estimate simultaneously requires a time-dependent and
\(R_j\)-dependent shear family, a finite observation window, conditioning or
correlation at the periodic inward bridge, a signed radial-collar
observable, and the exact \(\Gamma_jL_jR_j^5\) scale.  Each screened theorem
instead concludes a global semigroup/mixing norm, a regularization estimate,
a total-variation contraction, or streamline-local decay for a fixed
autonomous shear.  Consequently none of the quoted theorems can be imported
verbatim to yield the target estimate.  At minimum, a new localization and
correlation argument would be required.

This validates exactly the source's operational meaning of **“no direct
implication found.”**  It does not prove that an adaptation of one of these
methods cannot close the estimate, that no other paper contains a suitable
lemma, or that the proposed lemma is new.

## 6. Limitations

   sources and their stated theorems, remarks, and proof mechanisms.  It is
   not an exhaustive citation, synonym, author, or forward-citation search.

2. The audit does not examine later versions, journal supplements, or other
   papers cited by these sources.  The SHA-256 values above prevent silent
   transfer of the verdict to different PDF bytes.

3. Absence is asserted only at the level needed for the bounded conclusion:
   no screened theorem directly supplies the full target.  It is not an
   impossibility theorem and is not evidence of novelty or priority.

4. This audit does not prove the R0.74K collar estimate, the bridge/shear-lag
   lemma, any Navier--Stokes endpoint theorem, regularity or singularity, or
