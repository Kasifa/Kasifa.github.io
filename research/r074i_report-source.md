# R0.74I primary-source report

## Research questions

This report checks the literature boundary for three claims considered in
R0.74I.

1. Can a terminally anchored trajectory of a spatially mollified suitable
   weak velocity be used in the local energy inequality?
2. Does small scale-invariant energy in the resulting moving tube imply a
   standard fixed-cylinder velocity-only epsilon criterion?
3. Is there an established Navier--Stokes estimate with the same observable
   form as

   \[
    Y_R\lesssim P_R^{2/3}\sqrt{1+\log_+P_R},
    \qquad Y_R\in\{X_R,\mathfrak C_R\}?
   \]

This is a bounded primary-source comparison, not an exhaustive novelty or
priority search.  Source review was completed on 2026-09-02.

## Direct answer

The suitable-weak local energy inequality, fixed-cylinder interpolation, and
velocity-only one-scale epsilon regularity are established tools.  Mollified
flow trajectories and their skewed cylinders also have direct precedents.
Yang anchors a mollified trajectory at a reference time, and Vasseur--Yang
write the path as \(X(t)=x\) and use a one-sided backward skewed cylinder.
Thus reference-time, or terminal, anchoring and backward skewness are already
established ingredients.  Those results support components of the R0.74I
moving-tube argument, but they do not state its exact moving local-energy test,
frozen Version-M observables, positive collar-flux estimate, or
small-payment-to-epsilon implication.

Several logarithmic Navier--Stokes criteria were found, including a genuine
square-root logarithm in a scaling-invariant Besov estimate.  The logarithms
occur in different global norms, uniqueness hypotheses, partial-regularity
gauges, or quantitative blow-up bounds.  No screened primary source states the
same local scalar-payment inequality displayed above.  This is only a finite
non-hit.  It is not evidence of novelty.

## Exact relation of the logarithmic precedents to the R0.74I family

For the frozen R0.74F--H family,

\[
 R_j=e^{-\rho L_j^2},\qquad B_jR_j^2\longrightarrow\frac1{128},
 \qquad Y_j\gtrsim B_j^2L_jR_j^2,
\]

and

\[
 B_j^2L_jR_j^2\lesssim P_j\lesssim B_j^3R_j^3.
\]

Thus \(\log P_j\asymp L_j^2\).  The candidate square-root logarithm
reproduces the missing factor \(L_j\), but only if the payment also reaches
the currently unproved scale \(P_j\gtrsim B_j^3R_j^3\).  None of the sources
above supplies that lower bound, identifies the R0.74H payment with its own
logarithmic functional, or controls the positive moving-collar flux.

## Collision screen and stopping rule

The bounded search covered the following distinct mechanisms:

- suitable weak local energy and fixed-cylinder epsilon regularity;
- pressure-free one-scale criteria at exponents \(3\), \(20/7\), and
  \(5/2+\delta\);
- reference-time-anchored trajectories of mollified incompressible flows and,
  in Vasseur--Yang, one-sided backward skewed cylinders;
- logarithmically improved Serrin conditions;
- critical Besov/Orlicz interpolation with square-root logarithms;
- quantitative critical \(L^3\) estimates; and
- logarithmic improvements of CKN singular-set gauges.

The search stopped after these mechanisms repeated without producing the
same observable-level theorem.  A direct-title 2025 preprint,
arXiv:2503.19944, was screened but not used as a validated precedent because
its proof was not independently established in this audit.  No claim in
R0.74I depends on that preprint.

## What the literature does not certify

The sources do not certify:

1. admissibility of the exact R0.74I solution-dependent moving test;
2. the finite-shell passage, pressure gauge, signs, or constants in the weak
   Version-M theorem;
3. the moving-to-fixed tube inclusion or the constants in its epsilon gate;
4. the exact R0.74F--H logarithmic lower frontier;
5. an endpoint upper estimate at logarithmic exponent \(1/2\);
6. smallness of the R0.74I payment at a possible singular point;
7. novelty or publication priority; or
8. global regularity or a solution of the Millennium problem.

Those claims stand or fall on the separate analytic derivation and independent
audits.  **NOT CLAY.**
