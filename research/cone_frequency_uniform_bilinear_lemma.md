# R0.21 cone-frequency cancellation lemma

## Status and scope

This note proves the exact frequency geometry and a shell-uniform, modewise
Leray-bilinear estimate for the sparse cone family used in R0.20.  It closes
the geometric part of Gate A.  It does **not** yet close the bilinear map in
an infinite analytic sequence space, estimate the Taylor remainder, or prove
a Navier--Stokes regularity or singularity statement.

Throughout, let

\[
 d=(1,1,1),\qquad
 K_\delta(c,\beta)=\delta^{-1}c d+\beta,\qquad
 \beta\cdot d=0,\qquad \delta=4^{-n}.
\]

All charges and offsets below belong to the additive lattice generated by
the eight signed initial modes.

## Lemma 1: the support is a transformed lattice cube

Use the rational coordinates

\[
 G(c,\beta)=(6c,3\beta_1,3\beta_2).
\]

The four positive input labels are

\[
 (1,1,1),\quad (1,1,-2),\quad (2,-1,2),\quad (2,-1,-1),
\]

and the four negative inputs are their negatives.  Define

\[
 a=(3/2,0,0),\qquad b=(0,0,3/2),\qquad
 c=(-1/2,1,-1/2).
\]

The eight signed labels are exactly

\[
 \{\,\varepsilon_1a+\varepsilon_2b+\varepsilon_3c:
       \varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}\,\}.
\]

The determinant of the three basis columns is \(-9/4\), hence the
representation is injective.  A sum of exactly \(L\) input labels has, along
each of the three basis directions, one of

\[
 -L,-L+2,\ldots,L-2,L.
\]

Consequently the exact \(L\)-leaf support has \((L+1)^3\) labels.  The zero
label occurs if and only if \(L\) is even, and then occurs at only one support
point.  After the zero Fourier mode is removed, the number of modes is

\[
 \#S_L=\begin{cases}
 (L+1)^3,&L\ \text{odd},\\
 (L+1)^3-1,&L\ \text{even}.
 \end{cases}
\]

This is a support statement, not a claim that all tree coefficients at all
these labels are nonzero.  It nevertheless replaces an a priori exponential
support count by an exact cubic count.

## Lemma 2: removal of the large derivative

Let \(u_1\in\mathbb C^3\) be divergence-free at
\(K_1=K_\delta(c_1,\beta_1)\), and put

\[
 L_\delta(u)=\delta^{-1}|d\cdot u|.
\]

If \(c_1\ne0\), incompressibility gives

\[
 \delta^{-1}d\cdot u_1=-\frac{\beta_1\cdot u_1}{c_1}.
\]

Therefore, for \(K_2=K_\delta(c_2,\beta_2)\),

\[
 K_2\cdot u_1
 =\left(\beta_2-\frac{c_2}{c_1}\beta_1\right)\cdot u_1.
\]

This identity removes the apparent \(\delta^{-1}\) factor exactly.  Without
dividing by the charge it also yields the bound, valid for every \(c_1\),

\[
 |K_2\cdot u_1|
 \le |\beta_2|\,|u_1|+|c_2|L_\delta(u_1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

## Lemma 3: the longitudinal component closes modewise

For a nonzero output frequency \(K=K_1+K_2\), write

\[
 Q=P_K\big[(K_2\cdot u_1)u_2\big],
\]

where \(P_K\) is the orthogonal Leray projection.  Orthogonality and (2.1)
give

\[
 |Q|\le
 \big(|\beta_2|\,|u_1|+|c_2|L_\delta(u_1)\big)|u_2|.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If the output charge \(c=c_1+c_2\) is nonzero, \(K\cdot Q=0\) implies

\[
 L_\delta(Q)\le \frac{|\beta_1+\beta_2|}{|c_1+c_2|}|Q|.
\tag{3.2}
\]

If \(c=0\), the output frequency is \(\beta=\beta_1+\beta_2\), with
\(\beta\cdot d=0\).  Hence \(d\cdot P_\beta v=d\cdot v\), and

\[
 L_\delta(Q)\le
 \big(|\beta_2|\,|u_1|+|c_2|L_\delta(u_1)\big)L_\delta(u_2).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Equations (3.1)--(3.3) have no negative power of \(\delta\).  In particular,
the charge-zero sector does not by itself reintroduce the shell loss feared
in the Gate A failure condition.

## Heat operator

The transverse decomposition gives the exact identity

\[
 \delta^2|K_\delta(c,\beta)|^2
 =3c^2+\delta^2|\beta|^2,
\]

because \(d\cdot\beta=0\).  Thus the heat multiplier in dimensionless time
\(\tau=\delta^{-2}t\) is also free of negative powers of \(\delta\).

## Remaining Gate A problem

The modewise obstruction is gone, but the factors
\(|\beta_j|\), \(|c_j|\), and
\(|\beta_1+\beta_2|/|c_1+c_2|\) still grow with the leaf label.  The next
step is to choose an analytic weight with a controlled radius loss and prove
a convolution inequality for the two-component quantity

\[
 |u(c,\beta)|+L_\delta(u(c,\beta)).
\]

Only after that infinite-dimensional estimate closes will the order-12
approximate solution and its residual supply a rigorous Taylor-tail bound.
