# R0.72I bounded primary-source non-collision audit

**Search cutoff:** 2026-08-27

**Audit target:** literature collision with the R0.72I odd-carrier
parity-refined complete-root theorem, and with a generic arbitrary-carrier
absorption or replacement of the remaining shear coefficient

\[
 B_A^2
 =K_z^2\sum_l |w_l|^2e^{-2\kappa r_l^2A}
 (\lambda_{q,r_l}+\lambda_{q,-r_l}).
 \tag{0.1}
\]

Here “generic absorption” means a bound with no extra one-sign helicity,
small-perturbation, monotone-shear, slow-variation, or fixed-carrier
assumption, and with no constant growing with the carrier count or carrier
locations.

## 0. Direct decision

I found primary results for fixed observation operators, Laplace--Carleson
embeddings, critical and logarithmic Besov spaces, helicity decompositions,
time analyticity, Couette enhanced dissipation, helical invariant subspaces,
and slowly varying large solutions. I did not find a theorem in those sources
that directly gives either of the two R0.72I conclusions under audit:

1. the all-odd, no-separation sum of slopes over the solution-dependent
   complete target root set, with the project’s real-gauge/parity refinement;
   or
2. generic absorption or replacement of the explicit coefficient (0.1) in
   the arbitrary-carrier case, using only the declared
   energy/helicity/critical-log physical ledger with a carrier-uniform
   constant.

The closest theories control different objects. Fixed-observation theorems
retain the observation norm. Besov theorems assume or propagate a spatial
critical norm. Helicity is coercive only after a sign or sector restriction.
Time analyticity isolates roots but does not sum their slopes. Couette and
slow-variation theorems impose a particular background geometry and
regularity regime.

This is a bounded non-collision conclusion. It is not a proof that no
equivalent theorem exists, and it is not a priority or originality
certificate.

The termwise \(B_AQ_*\) obstruction and the all-odd parity repair proved in
R0.72I are therefore internal results. The sources below provide context and
comparison, not either conclusion.

## 2. Four distinctions that affect the R0.72I claim

### 2.1 Admissibility estimates an observation; it does not erase it

For a fixed operator \(C\), admissibility has the schematic form

\[
 \int_0^T\lVert CT(t)x\rVert^2\,dt
 \le K_C^2\lVert x\rVert^2.
\]

The value and uniformity of \(K_C\) are part of the theorem. In R0.72I, the
row producing \(B_A\) is itself built from the shear coefficients. Treating
that row as an abstract observation merely moves \(B_A\) into an observation
norm; it does not prove that the physical ledger pays it.

### 2.2 A stronger spatial norm can contain \(B_A\) without absorbing it

On a fixed finite carrier set, standard Fourier norm comparisons can dominate
(0.1) by a sufficiently strong Sobolev or Besov norm. The comparison either
contains the same frequency-weighted sum or pays the largest carrier and the
number of carriers. R0.72I instead asks for payment by the declared physical
normalization with no such loss. None of the checked Besov sources proves that
conversion.

### 2.3 Helicity and parity are different structures

Helical sign selects an eigenspace of the curl operator. The parity refinement
in the finite triangular lattice selects an arithmetic carrier sector and a
compatible real target gauge. One property does not imply the other. The
coercive helicity theorems therefore cannot be imported unless an additional
helical sign theorem is proved for the exact data class.

### 2.4 Analyticity and enhanced dissipation do not pack complete roots

Time analyticity is a local regularity property. Enhanced dissipation is a
decay mechanism tied to a specified shear background. Neither statement
controls the squared derivative mass at every endogenous target zero without
root separation. The parity-refined Rolle/BV mechanism remains an internal
part of the project, not a corollary of either literature.

## 3. Query range

I checked primary journal pages, DOI records, author manuscripts, and arXiv
records through 2026-08-27. Query and citation chaining covered:

- weighted observation admissibility, exact observability, square functions,
  Laplace--Carleson embeddings, and non-autonomous evolution families;
- vector and operator-valued Carleson embedding with finite-dimensional
  constants;
- \(BMO^{-1}\), negative Besov self-improvement, logarithmic Besov spaces,
  heat-semigroup characterizations, and scale-invariant interpolation;
- helicity identities, spin/helical decomposition, helical decimation, curl,
  and projected Lamb-vector geometry;
- time analyticity, temporal Fourier-coordinate zeros, complex caloric zeros,
  zero isolation, root counts, and derivative sampling;
- Couette stability thresholds, enhanced dissipation, inviscid damping, and
  2.5D streaks;
- helical invariant subspaces, almost-two-dimensional data, and solutions
  slowly varying in one direction;
- combinations of “finite Fourier carriers”, “triangular lattice”,
  “complete roots”, “root-slope mass”, “parity”, “shear moment”, and
  “Navier--Stokes”.

Returned surveys, duplicate records, unrelated inverse-problem Carleman
estimates, and sources that only mentioned Navier--Stokes as an example were
not used in the ledger. The search was stopped after the targeted query
families returned the same primary sources or weaker analogues.

## 4. Allowed literature statement

The following wording is supported by this audit:

> In the bounded primary-source search through 2026-08-27, I found no theorem
> that directly turns fixed-observation or Carleson admissibility, critical or
> logarithmic Besov control, helicity, time analyticity, Couette enhanced
> dissipation, or known helical/slow-variation special classes into the
> parity-refined complete-root estimate and generic carrier-uniform
> \(B_A\)-absorption required here.

The stronger statement “no such theorem exists” is not supported. The audit
does not exhaust the Navier--Stokes literature, prove priority, or establish
novelty. Any positive R0.72I result must be stated as a theorem for the exact
finite triangular class and with its parity, phase, amplitude, and carrier
uniformity assumptions explicit. Any negative result must identify the
normalized family and the precise absorption inequality it disproves.
