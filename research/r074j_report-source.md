## Direct answer

The strongest tractable R0.74J result is not a new trajectory-containment
theorem.  Published work already gives quantitative comparison and
containment for admissible skewed cylinders, and it already uses that geometry
for suitable weak Navier--Stokes solutions.  Finite dissipation gives
eventual admissibility only almost everywhere, which does not settle a
preassigned possible singular point.

The selected theorem instead closes an exact-family gap left in R0.74I.  A
fixed box in the fifth payment shell forces

\[
 \mathcal G_u\ge8e^{-8}B_j^3R_j^3.
\]

Together with the frozen R0.74G upper bound, this gives

\[
 P_j:=P_{R_j}^M=P_{R_j}^F,
 \qquad P_j\asymp B_j^3R_j^3,
 \qquad
 \frac{\log P_j}{L_j^2}\to\frac3{320}.
\]

This result is internal to one explicit smooth family.  It does not prove the
universal square-root-log endpoint or global regularity.  **NOT CLAY.**

## Evidence synthesis

### 1. Cross-scale trajectory geometry

Yang defines an \(\eta\)-admissible skewed cylinder by the scale-invariant
average

\[
 \varepsilon^2\fint_{Q_\varepsilon}\mathcal M(|\nabla u|)<\eta.
\]

Under admissibility, the paper quantitatively compares trajectories generated
by different mollification scales.  For intersecting comparable cylinders,
final Lemma 6 (arXiv v2 Lemma 9) controls the centre-trajectory separation and
final Proposition 7 (arXiv v2 Proposition 10) gives a \(9Q\) containment only
during the common lifespan.  In the same-centre neighboring scale band, the
proof of final Proposition 11 (arXiv v2 Proposition 14) gives the stronger
\(Q_{\varepsilon_\beta}\subset(3/4)Q_{\varepsilon_\alpha}\) conclusion when
the large cylinder has the stronger admissibility threshold.

Source:
[Jincheng Yang, *Construction of maximal functions associated with skewed
cylinders generated by incompressible flows and applications*](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.05588v2),
published in *Annales de l'Institut Henri Poincare C, Analyse non lineaire*
39 (2022), 793--818; official
[EMS record](https://ems.press/journals/aihpc/articles/4938796), DOI
[10.4171/AIHPC/20](https://doi.org/10.4171/AIHPC/20).

Consequence for R0.74J: a theorem whose only new-looking conclusion is
\(|X_R-X_S|\lesssim R\) or neighboring-scale tube containment would collide
directly with published work.  A future noncolliding result would need a new
source of admissibility, such as a payment-to-admissibility estimate.

### 2. Almost-everywhere versus prescribed-point admissibility

Yang's final Proposition 11 (arXiv v2 Proposition 14) states that for almost
every spacetime point, all
sufficiently small skewed cylinders are admissible and their diameters tend
to zero.  For energy-class Navier--Stokes solutions,
\(\nabla u\in L^2_{t,x}\) is compatible with the paper's maximal-function
assumption.

The qualifier “almost every” is decisive.  A null exceptional set can still
contain the entire singular set, so this theorem does not produce
admissibility at one preassigned possible singular point.

Source: the same
[Yang full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.05588v2), arXiv v2 Proposition
14; final Proposition 11.

### 3. Suitable-weak use of skewed cylinders

Vasseur--Yang use mollified-flow recentering and a local theorem with zero
mollified mean velocity.  Their Theorem 1.3 turns mixed velocity-gradient and
vorticity smallness into higher-vorticity regularity, without a local
pressure-smallness hypothesis.  The local-to-global argument uses the
skewed-cylinder maximal function and yields improved Lorentz integrability of
second derivatives for suitable weak solutions.

Source:
[Alexis Vasseur and Jincheng Yang, *Second derivatives estimate of suitable
solutions to the 3D Navier--Stokes equations*](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.14291v1),
*Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis* 241 (2021), 683--727,
DOI [10.1007/s00205-021-01661-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00205-021-01661-4).

Consequence for R0.74J: mollified trajectories and skewed cylinders are not a
new suitable-weak framework by themselves.

### 4. Hollow-shell quantitative partial regularity

Lei--Ren prove the gauge improvement

\[
 \mathcal P^f(\mathcal S)=0,\qquad f(r)=r|\log r|,
\]

and obtain quantitative regular regions.  Their central pigeonhole input uses
nonoverlapping hollow dissipation layers whose total mass is finite.  Theorem B
produces a quantitative spatial interval, while Proposition 9 separately
produces a regular time epoch.  Theorem D gives an axisymmetric one-point
criterion under an additional logarithmically weighted dissipation
hypothesis.

Source:
[Zhen Lei and Xiao Ren, *Quantitative partial regularity of the
Navier--Stokes equations and applications*](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.01783v1),
*Advances in Mathematics* 445 (2024), 109654,
DOI [10.1016/j.aim.2024.109654](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2024.109654).

Consequence for R0.74J: the literature supplies small hollow layers and
singular-set control, but not the missing implication

\[
 \text{small moving collar}\Longrightarrow
 \text{small normalized moving core at a prescribed point}.
\]

That implication remains a route gap, not a conclusion of the cited paper.

### 5. One-scale pressure-free epsilon regularity

Wang--Wu--Zhou prove a one-scale epsilon-regularity criterion for suitable
weak solutions using a velocity norm and no pressure-smallness assumption.
For each \(\delta>0\), their Theorem 1.1 turns small
\(L^{5/2+\delta}(Q(1))\) norm into \(L^\infty(Q(1/16))\) regularity.  R0.74I
uses the specialization \(\delta=1/2\), hence exponent \(3\), after the
moving-to-fixed tube containment has been established.

Source:
[Y. Wang, G. Wu, and D. Zhou, *A regularity criterion at one scale without
pressure for suitable weak solutions to the Navier--Stokes
equations*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022039619302086),
*Journal of Differential Equations* 267 (2019), 4673--4704,
DOI [10.1016/j.jde.2019.05.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jde.2019.05.003).

Consequence for R0.74J: the remaining issue is not the absence of a one-scale
epsilon theorem.  It is the absence of a mechanism that forces its smallness
hypothesis at a preassigned possible singular point.

## Search record and stop reason

The first pass separated three route families:

1. mollified-trajectory comparison and skewed-cylinder containment;
2. prescribed-singular-point good-scale or bad-shell results;
3. the exact R0.74F--H family's complete-payment asymptotics.

The focused pass read the relevant theorem statements and assumptions in
Yang, Vasseur--Yang, Lei--Ren, and Wang--Wu--Zhou.  It found direct
collisions for the first route and a prescribed-point scope gap for the
second.  The third route reduced to a one-shell analytic lower bound.
Further broad searches began repeating the same three mechanisms, so the
search stopped.  This finite search does not establish novelty or priority.
