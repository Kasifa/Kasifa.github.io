**Question:** Which primary results bear directly on a heat-decaying
Fourier-lattice evolution with strong skew coupling, a shrinking observation
layer, and complete temporal-root slope mass?

## 1. Operator dictionary

Fourier transformation in the lattice index turns

\[
 F'=D_qF+\delta V_z(x)F
\]

into a diffusion equation on the circle with a real, heat-decaying imaginary
potential,

\[
 \partial_x f
 =\bigl(\kappa\partial_\theta^2+\text{lower diagonal terms}
 -i\delta b_M(x,\theta)\bigr)f,
 \tag{1.1}
\]

where

\[
 b_M(x,\theta)=2K_z\sum_{l=1}^M
 z_le^{-\kappa r_l^2x}\cos(r_l\theta).
 \tag{1.2}
\]

This places the frozen problem next to enhanced-dissipation and large
imaginary-potential theory. The observable in R0.72A is different: it is the
sum of squared derivatives at all zeros of one coordinate. No source below
states that estimate.

## 3. Research decision forced by the literature

For a fixed profile with maximal critical degeneracy \(n_M\), the adjacent
frozen theory suggests an enhanced-dissipation comparison rate of the form

\[
 \lambda_{{\rm ED},M}
 \asymp
 \kappa^{(n_M+1)/(n_M+3)}
 |\delta_M|^{2/(n_M+3)},
 \tag{3.1}
\]

up to profile constants and possible logarithmic losses. Equation (3.1) is a
comparison scale, not a theorem for (1.1).

Two regimes must be separated:

1. If \(L_M\lambda_{{\rm ED},M}\to\infty\) and profile constants remain
   uniform, existing frozen-profile theory predicts strong terminal
   \(L^2\) decay and motivates testing whether charge can survive; it does not
   itself exclude temporal root-slope mass.
2. If \(L_M\lambda_{{\rm ED},M}=O(1)\), or profile degeneracy/sublevel
   constants deteriorate, the observation can end before enhanced
   dissipation acts. This is the construction window tested in R0.72A.

The frozen approximation must also track

\[
 L_M\kappa r_{\max,M}^2,
 \tag{3.2}
\]

because heat decay changes the profile independently of \(|\delta_M|L_M\).

## 4. Negative search result, with exact boundary

No primary source in this bounded search proves the chain

\[
 \text{large skew coupling}
 \Longrightarrow \text{many exact coordinate zeros}
 \Longrightarrow \sum|g'(\tau_j)|^2\text{ lower bound}
 \Longrightarrow \text{nonlinear rotational charge}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The search therefore supports four bounded statements only:

1. Bessel identities and asymptotics used in the explicit family are
   standard and directly sourced.
2. The growing-window dissipative comparison and exact-root persistence are
   proved inside R0.72A, not imported from the literature.
3. Fixed-profile enhanced dissipation makes a scalar scan in \(\eta\) alone
   scientifically inadequate.
4. No originality, priority, or nonexistence claim follows from this bounded
   audit.
