# A correlated two-block weighted-column theorem

## R0.46 research note

### Abstract

R0.45 identified an exact obstruction in the unweighted strict-tail column of
the reduced canonical edge generating system: at radius (r=0.372), the
fixed input-charge (s=-1) column is (1.0010616516ldots>1).  The
obstruction is concentrated in the exceptional output-charge-zero channel.
This note separates that channel from the nonzero-charge output and introduces

\[
  \|f\|_{r,\kappa}
  :=\kappa\|P_0f\|_{B_r}+\|P_{\ne0}f\|_{B_r},
  \qquad \kappa=\frac34.
\]

The essential point is not a separately maximized (2\times2) block matrix.
For each input monomial, its zero- and nonzero-charge outputs are combined
with the weights above *before* the supremum over input columns is taken.
This retains the correlation between two outputs generated by the same input.

Five disjoint all-order sectors cover every strict-tail monomial:
(s=0), (s=-1), (s=1), (2\le s<241), and (s\ge241).  At
(r=0.376), their certified bounds are respectively

\[
  0.7667413989,\quad
  0.9806763654,\quad
  0.2029506567,\quad
  0.9952048059,\quad
  0.9977064757.
\]

Thus the full correlated tail bound is (0.99770647568583198433<1), and
the weighted Banach restart and inherited canonical-stretch construction both
close.  At the adjacent probe (r=0.377), the inherited large-charge
sufficient bound is (1.0030411177094620525>1), while the polynomial
stretch bound remains below one.  This is a failure of the present sufficient
bound only.  It is not a failure theorem for the exact operator, and it has no
direct implication for three-dimensional Navier--Stokes regularity or
singularity.

## 1. Setting

For a monomial (Z^mW^n), write

\[
  i=m+n,\qquad q=2n-m
\]

for its total degree and charge.  The exact center (p_{80}) is generated by
the pinned recurrence through degree 80, and its active support satisfies
(q\ge-1).  Let

\[
  c_{i,q}(r)=|a_{i,q}|r^i
\]

denote its nonnegative weighted coefficients.  No coefficient-sign
cancellation is used.

The ordinary degree-weighted Wiener norm is denoted by (\|\cdot\|_{B_r}).
Let (P_0) and (P_{\ne0}) be the projections onto output charge zero and
nonzero charge.  For (0<\kappa<1), define the equivalent two-block norm

\[
  \|f\|_{r,\kappa}
  =\kappa\|P_0f\|_{B_r}+\|P_{\ne0}f\|_{B_r}.
\]

It satisfies

\[
  \kappa\|f\|_{B_r}
  \le \|f\|_{r,\kappa}
  \le \|f\|_{B_r}.
\]

The fixed choice in this note is (\kappa=3/4).

## 2. The correlation principle

For an input monomial of degree (j) and charge (s), let
(C_0(j,s)) and (C_{\ne0}(j,s)) be the exact absolute column sums in
the two output blocks.  The induced weighted-column ratio is

\[
  \mathcal C_{r,\kappa}(j,s)
  =\frac{\kappa C_0(j,s)+C_{\ne0}(j,s)}{w(s)},
  \qquad
  w(s)=
  \begin{cases}
    \kappa,&s=0,\\
    1,&s\ne0.
  \end{cases}
\]

The numerator combines both outputs of the same input column.  The induced
operator bound is obtained by taking the supremum only afterward:

\[
  \|D\Phi(p_{80})\|_{r,\kappa}
  \le \sup_{j,s}\mathcal C_{r,\kappa}(j,s).
\]

This order of operations is indispensable.  If one first maximizes each
block entry over potentially different input columns, one obtains a coarse
matrix

\[
  M=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\ C&D\end{pmatrix}.
\]

At (r=0.376), this matrix has display Perron value
(1.0753987917062369).  Its failure is exact because

\[
  D+BC-1
  =0.081256698459034414403\ldots>0.
\]

The matrix therefore cannot certify the target.  This does not contradict the
correlated column theorem: its entries came from separate column suprema and
discarded the joint structure that the weighted norm preserves.

## 3. The five exhaustive sectors

The input support cone and the cutoff (N=80) divide all strict-tail
columns into five disjoint cases.

### 3.1 Zero input charge: (s=0)

The output charge equals the center charge (q).  When (q=0), the
determinant factor vanishes, so the zero-charge output is identically zero.
For (q\ne0), the exact column is

\[
  c_{i,q}(r)\frac{i+j}{i+j-1}\frac{|q|}{3},
\]

which decreases with (j).  The minimum admissible degree is (j=81),
and division by the zero-input weight gives the exact all-order endpoint

\[
  Z_0(r,\kappa)
  =\frac1\kappa
  \sum_{q\ne0}c_{i,q}(r)
  \frac{i+81}{i+80}\frac{|q|}{3}.
\]

At (r=0.376), (Z_0=0.76674139886755508747).

### 3.2 Fixed negative input charge: (s=-1)

Put (t=1/j\), with (0\le t\le1/82).  R0.45 proved that the
unweighted column can have negative derivative only through center charge
(q=1).  Those terms now land in the zero-output block and acquire the
factor (\kappa).  If (\widehat Q_r(1/82)) denotes the same exact
uniform negative-derivative bound as in R0.45, the weighted column
(G_r(t)) satisfies

\[
  G'_r(t)\ge3r-\kappa\widehat Q_r(1/82).
\]

At the target radius,

\[
  3r=1.128,
  \qquad
  \kappa\widehat Q_r(1/82)
  =0.13051418504647839798\ldots,
\]

so

\[
  G'_r(t)\ge0.99748581495352160202\ldots>0.
\]

Hence the maximum over every admissible tail degree is the true lattice
endpoint (j=82), not a sampled approximation.  Its two output parts are

\[
  C_0(82,-1)=0.14330206063429646699\ldots,
  \qquad
  C_{\ne0}(82,-1)=0.87319981990783293436\ldots,

and therefore

\[
  Z_{-1}(0.376,3/4)

### 3.3 Fixed positive input charge: (s=1)

Only center charge (q=-1) lands in the zero-output block.  Applying the
all-degree termwise bound at the minimum admissible degree (j=83) yields

\[
  Z_1(r,\kappa)
  \le \kappa Z_{1,0}(r)+Z_{1,\ne0}(r).

At the target,

\[
  Z_{1,0}=0.12801961114116615299\ldots,
  \quad
  Z_{1,\ne0}=0.10693594831053124914\ldots,

and (Z_1=0.20295065666640586388\ldots).

### 3.4 Finite positive charges: (2\le s<241)

These columns do not produce charge-zero output because the center support
has (q\ge-1).  The R0.41 fixed-charge theorem therefore applies without a
weight change.  Its complete core is convex in the shared slope (x=s/j),
and the degree prefactor is uniform for every (j\ge J_s).  At the target,
the maximum occurs at (s=162):

\[
  \max_{2\le s<241}Z_s(0.376)
  =0.99520480590228934501\ldots.
\]

### 3.5 Large positive charges: (s\ge241)

Again no zero-output channel is possible.  The R0.44 common-slope theorem
retains (x=s/j\) in the full positive sum.  Convexity on (0\le x\le2)
reduces the entire infinite sector to two endpoints.  At (r=0.376), the
(x=2) endpoint is active:

\[
  \sup_{s\ge241}Z_s(0.376)
  =0.99770647568583198433\ldots.

## 4. All-order theorem at the target radius

### Theorem 4.1

For the pinned degree-80 center, cutoff (S=241), radius
(r=376/1000), and zero-charge weight (\kappa=3/4), every strict-tail
input monomial belongs to exactly one of the five sectors above.  The induced
weighted-column bound is

\[
  \boxed{
  \|D\Phi(p_{80})\|_{r,\kappa}
  \le0.99770647568583198433<1.}
\]

The active sector is (s\ge241).

#### Proof

The sectors (s=0,-1,1\), (2\le s<241), and (s\ge241) are
disjoint and exhaustive.  Sections 3.1--3.5 give all-order bounds for each
sector.  Their exact maximum is the large-charge value
(0.99770647568583198433\ldots).  The zero- and nonzero-output terms are
combined inside each input column before this maximum is taken.  Therefore
the maximum is a valid induced (\ell^1) column bound in
(\|\cdot\|_{r,\kappa}).  □

The theorem uses neither a tail-degree grid nor coefficient-sign
cancellation.  The finite exact columns in the certificate are regression
tests only.

## 5. Weighted Banach restart

Norm equivalence transfers the quadratic Wiener-algebra estimate to

\[
  \|\Phi(h)\|_{r,\kappa}
  \le \frac{3}{\kappa^2}\|h\|_{r,\kappa}^2.
\]

For (\kappa=3/4), the quadratic constant is (16/3).  At
(r=0.376), the exact contraction margin is

\[
  \delta=1-0.99770647568583198433
  =0.0022935243141680156741\ldots.
\]

With ball radius (\rho=\delta/10^6), the certified quantities are

\[
\begin{aligned}
  \|R_{80}\|_{r,\kappa}
    &=9.8011648772190277515\times10^{-31},\\
  \text{mapping upper bound}
    &=2.2882640884430226324\times10^{-9}<\rho,\\
  \text{Lipschitz upper bound}
    &=0.99770650015009133545<1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the reduced fixed point closes in the two-block norm.  To invoke the
inherited canonical-stretch construction in the ordinary norm, use

\[
  \|h\|_{B_r}\le\kappa^{-1}\|h\|_{r,\kappa}.
\]

The resulting stretch-operator bound is

\[
  0.98637137111814374792<1.
\]

Consequently the regular canonical fields constructed in R0.42 remain
available at the common radius (0.376).  The obsolete direct-transport
diagnostic is (1.6238582372\ldots>1); it is not the construction gate after
the regular decomposition.

## 8. Research value and limits

R0.46 contributes a reusable operator-estimation principle: a block weight
can succeed when it is applied within each input column, even though the
matrix of separately maximized block suprema fails.  It also moves the exact
research boundary.  R0.45 proved that the unweighted (s=-1) column blocks
(r=0.372); R0.46 removes that obstruction through (r=0.376), after which
the large positive-charge sector becomes active.

This is a theorem for a reduced bivariate generating system.  No theorem in
the present chain transfers its radius to a scale-critical norm of the full
three-dimensional velocity field, controls every Fourier interaction of the
PDE, or addresses arbitrary smooth initial data.  It therefore does not solve
the Clay Navier--Stokes problem and should not be described as evidence of a
PDE singularity or global regularity.

## 9. Next falsifiable step

R0.47 should keep (r=0.377) as the first acceptance test and attack the
large-charge estimate that is now active.  The present common-slope theorem
uses a uniform degree floor and a separated degree prefactor before applying
convexity in (x=s/j).  The next test is whether retaining the exact
charge--degree lattice relation inside that prefactor lowers the large-sector
endpoint below one.

The acceptance conditions are:

1. cover every integer (s\ge241) and every admissible (j>80) without a
   finite charge or degree cutoff;
2. retain a common analytic variable or prove an equivalent monotonicity or
   convexity theorem;
3. certify the (r=0.377) large-sector bound by exact rational inequalities;
4. keep the (s=162) finite-positive sector and the weighted (s=-1)
   sector as explicit controls;
5. publish failure, including the exact worst sector and margin, if the new
   all-order estimate remains above one.

The adjacent radius should be chosen only after the (0.377) gate has been
settled; no extrapolation from the current four radii is mathematically
justified.
