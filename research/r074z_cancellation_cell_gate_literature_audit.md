records concerning:

- quantitative unique continuation and propagation of smallness for heat or
  parabolic equations;
- observability from measurable, thick, or shrinking spatial sets;
- small-time heat-control costs and their geometric constants;
- Remez or Turán--Nazarov inequalities for finite exponential sums; and
- combinations of common shear, Navier--Stokes, finite corrector, cubic
  payment, endpoint persistence, and fixed deletion.

opened primary record, its hypotheses were compared against R0.74Z, and the

## 3. Measurable-set observability and propagation of smallness

J. Apraiz, L. Escauriaza, G. Wang, and C. Zhang,
[*Observability Inequalities and Measurable Sets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.4876)
(submitted 2012, revised 2013; later JEMS), prove heat-equation
observability from subsets of positive spacetime measure and establish a
Lebeau--Robbiano spectral inequality for suitable bounded domains. This is a
genuine precedent for replacing open observations by measurable ones. It
does not by itself quantify a family of observation sets whose width,
location, and time duration depend exponentially on \(L^2\), nor does it
identify the observation norm with the nonnegative exterior cubic row of
\(P_R^M\).

N. Burq and I. Moyano,
[*Propagation of smallness and control for heat equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07402)
(2019, revised 2022), establish spectral-projector and
propagation-of-smallness results on sets of positive measure, including
Lipschitz-coefficient settings. This is the closest screened precedent to the
idea that qualitative analyticity should be replaced by a quantitative
inequality. Its stated framework does not automatically provide the
coefficient dependence required for the R0.74Z drift
\(\partial_t+b(t,x_3)\partial_2-\Delta_{23}\), the moving remote strip, or
the threshold \(\kappa_*\). Importing it would require a new rescaling and
constant audit, not a citation-only step.

G. Wang, M. Wang, C. Zhang, and Y. Zhang,
[*Observable set, observability, interpolation inequality and spectral
inequality for the heat equation in \(\mathbb R^n\)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04279)
(2017), characterize observable sets by thickness. Y. Duan, H. Yu, and
C. Zhang,
[*Quantitative unique continuation and observability on an equidistributed
set for the diffusion equation in \(\mathbb R^N\)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.04540)
(2021), prove a quantitative unique-continuation and observability inequality
from an equidistributed set. These results show that observation geometry is
not cosmetic. R0.74Z supplies one shrinking moving strip, not a scale-uniform
thick or equidistributed set; the published hypotheses therefore do not
directly close (Z.39).

## 4. Small-time cost and the critical temporal branch

L. Miller,
[*Geometric bounds on the growth rate of null-controllability cost for the
heat equation in small time*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307158)
(2003), proves geometric lower bounds for the exponential constant in the
small-time heat-control cost. The result supports the research heuristic that
forcing a heat profile to change on a much shorter time scale is not free.
It does not prove R0.74Z's needed alternative: its control is an external
source, whereas an admissible corrector here is an uncontrolled solution
generated by initial data and transported by the same \(b\); moreover the
control norm is not the central-energy or exterior component of \(P_R^M\).

C. Laurent and M. Léautaud,
[*Observability of the heat equation, geometric constants in control theory,
and a conjecture of Luc Miller*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00969)
(2018; *Analysis & PDE* 2021), study short-time observability constants,
small observation regions, and maximal vanishing rates of sums of
eigenfunctions. Their dependence on observation geometry and spectral
complexity makes this a high-value source for the next proposition. It still
does not supply the R0.74Z conclusion without proving that its spectral norm
and constants dominate every ill-conditioned common-shear corrector while
remaining visible in the complete Version-M payment ledger.

These sources support the direction of (Z.39), but none classifies the
critical layer
\(-L^{-2}\log\theta_L=\kappa_*+o(1)\). The strict subcritical conclusion in
R0.74Z remains an internal Hölder calculation; the critical and shorter-time
branches remain open.

## 5. Finite exponential sums and multi-center Gaussian cells

After division by the primary Gaussian, the equal-age multi-center model in
(Z.30) is an exponential polynomial in the normalized vertical coordinate.
O. Friedland,
[*A Disk-Growth Remez Principle and a Modular Proof of the Measurable
Turán--Nazarov Inequality*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24823)
(2026), gives a measurable Remez inequality for exponential polynomials and
makes the term-count/effective-degree mechanism explicit. This is a plausible
tool for turning “finite family” into a quantitative statement when the
number of centers, spectral diameter, coefficient conditioning, and observed
set are all controlled.

It does not close the project gate as quoted. R0.74Z permits \(M=M(R)\),
exponentially ill-conditioned coefficients, re-evolution under a
time-dependent common shear, inversion partners, and all periodic windings.
A fixed-time spatial Remez bound also does not yield the required temporal
persistence or show that its global norm is paid by \(P_R^M\). A useful next
lemma would have to track all of those parameters and preserve a strict sign
relative to the small target exponent \(\zeta=1/5000\).
