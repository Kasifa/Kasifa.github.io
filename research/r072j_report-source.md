# R0.72J -- residue graphs and the true mixed-parity cubic row

**Date:** 2026-08-27

**Status:** exact carrier-graph classification and a common-frequency-band
upper theorem for the true cubic contribution in the finite triangular 2.5D
Navier--Stokes class. A genuinely non-bipartite coherent block attains the
raw cubic scale allowed by that theorem, but its physically normalized cubic
ratio still tends to zero. The complete-root ledger in a complex target gauge
is not closed. This is not a theorem for general three-dimensional
Navier--Stokes solutions.

**Keywords:** Navier--Stokes regularity, triangular 2.5D flow, Cayley graph,
lattice parity, signed Schur triple, cubic interaction, critical-log action,
physical normalization

---

## 0. Direct decision

Let the nonzero shear carriers be \(r_1,\ldots,r_N\), put

\[
 g_0=\gcd(r_1,\ldots,r_N),
 \qquad a_l=r_l/g_0,
 \tag{0.1}
\]

and consider the Cayley graph of \(g_0\mathbb Z\) generated by
\(\{\pm r_l\}\). The exact two-colour criterion is

\[
 \boxed{\text{the graph is bipartite}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a_l\text{ is odd for every }l.}
 \tag{0.2}
\]

Thus the invariant statement in R0.72I was not that the unreduced carriers
are odd. It was that they all lie in the nontrivial coset of
\(2g_0\mathbb Z\) inside \(g_0\mathbb Z\).

Write

\[
 \Sigma=\{\pm r_l:1\le l\le N\},
 \qquad \mathcal R_1=\Sigma,
 \qquad \mathcal R_2=\Sigma+\Sigma.
 \tag{0.3}
\]

For \(h=P_0VF\) and \(b=P_0V^2F\), the row \(h\) reads the coordinates
indexed by \(-\mathcal R_1\), whereas the uncollected two-step path expansion
of \(b\) reads those indexed by \(-\mathcal R_2\). These path supports
overlap exactly when there is a signed Schur triple:

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal R_1\cap\mathcal R_2\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 s+t=u\text{ for some }s,t,u\in\Sigma.}
 \tag{0.4}
\]

Non-bipartiteness and (0.4) are different. The set
\(\{1,4\}\) is non-bipartite because \(1+1+1+1-4=0\) is an odd closed
walk, but it has no signed Schur triple. Its first obstruction is a
five-cycle, not a triangle.

The exact mixed-parity decomposition is

\[
 V=V_{\rm o}+V_{\rm e},
 \tag{0.5}
\]

where \(V_{\rm o}\) contains the odd reduced carriers and swaps the two
residue classes, while \(V_{\rm e}\) contains the even reduced carriers and
preserves them. Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
 h&=P_0V_{\rm e}F_{\rm e}+P_0V_{\rm o}F_{\rm o},\\
 b&=P_0(V_{\rm o}^2+V_{\rm e}^2)F_{\rm e}
   +P_0(V_{\rm o}V_{\rm e}+V_{\rm e}V_{\rm o})F_{\rm o}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{0.6}
\]

This is the precise point at which the all-odd repair stops being generic.

There is nevertheless a carrier-uniform estimate if all carriers occupy one
frequency band. Assume

\[
 R\le |r_l|\le C_0R,
 \qquad c_0a\le |w_l|\le C_0a,
 \qquad
 \|V(x)\|\le C_1aB e^{-c_1R^2x},
 \tag{0.7}
\]

with

\[
 \sqrt N\lesssim B\lesssim N,
 \qquad g=|\delta|a,
 \qquad \varepsilon=\frac{gB}{R^2}\le\gamma_0.
 \tag{0.8}
\]

For a row-aligned launch of energy \(N\), the one-dimensional product
estimate gives

\[
 \mathfrak q(x)\le C\rho(x)^2E(x),
 \tag{0.9}
\]

and hence, on a fixed interval beginning at zero,

\[
 Q_*\asymp
 a^2N^2R^{-4/3}(1+\log R),
 \qquad
 m_*\le
 Ca^2NR^{4/3}(1+\log R)^{-1}.
 \tag{0.10}
\]

The true cubic row has the joint bound

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_\times
 :=|\delta|\int|hP_0V^2F|\,dx
 \le C\frac{gB}{R^2}a^2N^2
 \le Ca^2N^2.}
 \tag{0.11}
\]

Under the same physical amplitude balance as R0.72I,

\[
 \Theta\asymp\frac{g^2}{a^2NR^2},
 \qquad D\asymp g^2NR^2,
 \qquad
 \mathscr A_*:=\Theta Q_*\asymp
 g^2NR^{-10/3}(1+\log R).
 \tag{0.12}
\]

It follows uniformly in \(N,B,g\) subject to (0.7)--(0.8) that

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{2\Theta\mathcal C_\times}
 {D^{1/3}(1+\mathscr A_*)}
 \le
 CR^{-4/9}(1+\log R)^{-2/3}\longrightarrow0.}
 \tag{0.13}
\]

This is a no-go theorem for producing a physical obstruction from the true
cubic row inside one common frequency band. It is not a complete-root
theorem.

The explicit block

\[
 S_R=\{R,R+1,\ldots,3R-1\},
 \qquad N=2R,
 \qquad w_r=1
 \tag{0.14}
\]

is genuinely non-bipartite and triangle-rich. It has \(R(R+1)/2\) ordered
positive solutions of \(r+s=t\), and \(3R(R+1)\) corresponding ordered
signed triples. For the coherent launch below,

\[
 |P_0V(0)^2G|=\frac{3R(R+1)}{\sqrt2}.
 \tag{0.15}
\]

With \(\delta=\gamma R\) and fixed sufficiently small \(\gamma>0\), its
true raw cubic mass is \(\asymp R^2\), yet its physical normalized ratio is
only \(\asymp R^{-2/3}\). The strongest mixed-parity coherence in this
single band therefore still does not supply a counterfamily.

## 1. Exact finite-carrier model

I use the exact triangular class and scaled variables of R0.72H--I. The
active Fourier lattice satisfies

\[
 \partial_xF=D_qF+\delta V_w(x)F,
 \qquad
 (D_qF)_r=-\lambda_{q,r}F_r,
 \tag{1.1}
\]

where

\[
 \lambda_{q,r}
 =\nu\left[(dr+K_y/q)^2+K_z^2/q^2\right]
 \tag{1.2}
\]

and

\[
 (V_w(x)F)_r=-iK_z\sum_{l=1}^N e^{-\kappa r_l^2x}
 \left(w_lF_{r-r_l}+\overline{w_l}F_{r+r_l}\right),
 \qquad \kappa=\nu d^2.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The conjugate pairing makes \(V_w^*=-V_w\). Therefore

\[
 \frac12\frac d{dx}\|F(x)\|_2^2
 =-\sum_r\lambda_{q,r}|F_r(x)|^2,
 \qquad E(x):=\|F(x)\|_2^2\le E(0).
 \tag{1.4}
\]

Set

\[
 z=V_wF,
 \qquad h=P_0z,
 \qquad b=P_0V_wz=P_0V_w^2F,
 \tag{1.5}
\]

\[
 \mathfrak q=\langle A_q^{-1}z,z\rangle,
 \qquad A_q=-D_q,
 \qquad
 \rho(x)=\|P_0V_w(x)\|_{\ell^2\to\mathbb C}.
 \tag{1.6}
\]

The target coordinate gives

\[
 |h|^2\le\lambda_0\mathfrak q.
 \tag{1.7}
\]

Differentiation gives the exact row equation

\[
 h'+\lambda_0h=QF+\delta b,
 \qquad
 Q=P_0[V_w'+V_w(D_q+\lambda_0)].
 \tag{1.8}
\]

On \(I=[0,X]\), with fixed \(X>0\), I retain the R0.72H definitions

\[
 Q_*^I=\int_Iw_*(x/X)\mathfrak q(x)\,dx,
 \qquad
 w_*(s)=s^{-1/3}[1+\log(1/s)],
 \tag{1.9}
\]

and

\[
 m_*(0,X)
 =\sup_{0<s\le1}w_*(s)^{-1}A_1(Xs)^2,
 \qquad
 A_1(x)^2=\sum_l r_l^2|w_l|^2e^{-2\kappa r_l^2x}.
 \tag{1.10}
\]

The endpoint convention is the one in R0.72H: the reciprocal weight tends
to zero at \(s=0\).

## 2. The residue graph and the two interaction spheres

Let \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Cay}(g_0\mathbb Z,\Sigma)\). It is connected by
the definition of \(g_0\).

### Theorem 2.1 -- exact bipartite classification

The graph \(\Gamma\) is bipartite if and only if every reduced carrier
\(a_l=r_l/g_0\) is odd.

#### Proof

If every \(a_l\) is odd, colour \(g_0n\) by \(n\bmod2\). Every generator
changes the colour, so this is a bipartition.

Conversely, assume \(\Gamma\) is bipartite. A connected bipartite graph has
a unique bipartition after the colour of zero is fixed. Translation by any
element of \(g_0\mathbb Z\) is a graph automorphism, so it either preserves
or swaps the two colours. The corresponding colour change defines a
homomorphism

\[
 \chi:g_0\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_2.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Every generator joins opposite colours, hence \(\chi(r_l)=1\). After
identifying \(g_0\mathbb Z\) with \(\mathbb Z\), the only nonzero
homomorphism to \(\mathbb Z_2\) is reduction modulo two. Therefore every
\(a_l\) is odd. \(\square\)

This is also the specialization to \(\mathbb Z\) of the standard Cayley
graph criterion recorded in Proposition 9.1 of Árnadóttir et al.

There is an elementary odd-walk check on the converse. If one reduced
carrier \(a\) is even and another \(c\) is odd, then

\[
 \underbrace{c+\cdots+c}_{a\text{ terms}}
 -\underbrace{a-\cdots-a}_{c\text{ terms}}=0
 \tag{2.2}
\]

has odd length \(a+c\). Since the reduced carriers have gcd one, the case
in which every carrier is even cannot occur.

### Proposition 2.2 -- exact one-step/two-step overlap

For each \(s\in\Sigma\), define

\[
 \alpha_s(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 -iK_zw_le^{-\kappa r_l^2x},&s=r_l,\\
 -iK_z\overline{w_l}e^{-\kappa r_l^2x},&s=-r_l.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Then

\[
 (V_wF)_r=\sum_{s\in\Sigma}\alpha_sF_{r-s},
 \tag{2.4}
\]

and exactly

\[
 h=\sum_{s\in\Sigma}\alpha_sF_{-s},
 \qquad
 b=\sum_{s,t\in\Sigma}\alpha_s\alpha_tF_{-(s+t)}.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

It follows immediately that the one-step support and the uncollected
two-step path support overlap if and only if \(u=s+t\) for some
\(s,t,u\in\Sigma\), proving (0.4). Coefficients belonging to the same
two-step endpoint may still cancel after phases are collected; (0.4) is the
exact path-support criterion, not a phase-independent lower bound.

The implication

\[
 \text{signed Schur triple}\Longrightarrow\text{non-bipartite}
 \tag{2.6}
\]

holds because the triple is a three-cycle. The converse fails. For
\(\Sigma=\{\pm1,\pm4\}\),

\[
 \Sigma+\Sigma=\{0,\pm2,\pm3,\pm5,\pm8\},
 \tag{2.7}
\]

which is disjoint from \(\Sigma\), even though the five-step relation in
(2.2) makes the graph non-bipartite.

## 3. Exact mixed-parity block form

Identify \(g_0\mathbb Z\) with \(\mathbb Z\), and let \(\Pi_{\rm e}\) and
\(\Pi_{\rm o}\) be the even and odd projections. Split

\[
 \Sigma=\Sigma_{\rm o}\sqcup\Sigma_{\rm e},
 \qquad
 V=V_{\rm o}+V_{\rm e},
 \tag{3.1}
\]

according to the parity of \(s/g_0\). Then

\[
 V_{\rm e}\Pi_\alpha=\Pi_\alpha V_{\rm e},
 \qquad
 V_{\rm o}\Pi_\alpha=\Pi_{\alpha+1}V_{\rm o},
 \qquad \alpha\in\mathbb Z_2.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Since the target index is even, (0.6) follows by expanding

\[
 V^2=V_{\rm o}^2+V_{\rm e}^2
     +V_{\rm o}V_{\rm e}+V_{\rm e}V_{\rm o}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

If \(V_{\rm e}=0\), the first line of (0.6) reads only \(F_{\rm o}\), and
the second reads only \(F_{\rm e}\). This recovers the R0.72I separation.
If both parts are present, \(h\) and \(b\) can read both residue sectors.
There is then no parity-only estimate forcing the input of \(b\) to be a
dynamically small component.

There is a second phase boundary. Suppose a target sector admits a fixed
real gauge in which \(V\) is a real skew matrix, and choose the strictly
row-aligned launch \(G\) proportional to \(Ve_0\). Then

\[
 P_0V^2G\ \text{ is proportional to }\
 \langle e_0,V^3e_0\rangle=0.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The last equality holds because an odd power of a real skew matrix is skew.
Thus a triangle-rich complex coherent launch cannot simply be imported into
the fixed-real-line Rolle argument. Either the direct cubic coherence or the
old real-gauge sampling mechanism may disappear under the required phase
choice. I do not treat them as simultaneously available.

## 4. A joint minimum for the cubic row

R0.72H gave the critical-action estimate

\[
 |b|^2\le B_A^2\mathfrak q,
 \qquad
 |h|^2\le\lambda_0\mathfrak q,
 \tag{4.1}
\]

and hence, since \(w_*\ge1\),

\[
 |\delta|\int_I|hb|\,dx
 \le |\delta|\sqrt{\lambda_0}B_AQ_*^I.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The alternative estimate keeps the two heat rows together. From the
definition of \(\rho\) and energy contraction,

\[
 |h|\le\rho E(0)^{1/2},
 \qquad
 |b|\le\rho\|V\|E(0)^{1/2}.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
 |\delta|\int_I|hb|\,dx
 \le |\delta|E(0)\int_I\rho(x)^2\|V(x)\|\,dx.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Combining the two valid estimates gives the exact hybrid decision

\[
 \boxed{
 |\delta|\int_I|hb|\,dx
 \le\min\left\{
 |\delta|\sqrt{\lambda_0}B_AQ_*^I,
 |\delta|E(0)\int_I\rho^2\|V\|\,dx
 \right\}.}
 \tag{4.5}
\]

The second term is the useful branch in a common band. It contains the
joint heat exposure and never forms the positive product \(B_AQ_*\).

## 5. Common-band theorem

I now fix the geometry, \(X>0\), and the constants in (0.7). All implicit
constants in this section may depend on those fixed quantities, but not on
\(R,N,B,a,g\).

Assume \(R\) is large, the carriers are distinct, (0.7)--(0.8) hold, and
the launch \(G\) satisfies

\[
 \|G\|_2^2=N,
 \qquad G_0=0,
 \qquad |P_0V(0)G|\ge c_2aN.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

I also assume the terms in the last row are phase-aligned, so the free
diagonal flow retains the lower bound

\[
 |P_0V(x)e^{xD_q}G|\ge caN
 \qquad(0\le x\le cR^{-2}).
 \tag{5.2}
\]

This assumption is explicit. It does not assert a compatible real target
gauge.

### Lemma 5.1 -- one-dimensional negative-norm product bound

For \(z=VF\),

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak q(x)\le C\rho(x)^2E(x).}
 \tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

After Fourier transform in the lattice index, \(V\) is multiplication by a
trigonometric polynomial \(b_x\), up to a fixed imaginary factor. For the
fixed nondegenerate geometry in this section, the quadratic form of \(A_q\)
is uniformly equivalent to the \(H^1(\mathbb T)\) norm, and hence

\[
 \|A_q^{-1/2}z\|_2\le C\|b_xf\|_{H^{-1}(\mathbb T)}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

For every \(\phi\in H^1(\mathbb T)\), one-dimensional Sobolev embedding
and Hölder give

\[
 |\langle b_xf,\phi\rangle|
 \le\|b_x\|_2\|f\|_2\|\phi\|_\infty
 \le C\|b_x\|_2\|f\|_2\|\phi\|_{H^1}.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

Thus \(\|b_xf\|_{H^{-1}}\le C\|b_x\|_2\|f\|_2\). Parseval identifies
\(\|b_x\|_2^2\) with \(\rho(x)^2\), up to a fixed normalization. Squaring
proves (5.3). \(\square\)

### Lemma 5.2 -- critical-log and reciprocal moments

For the coupled solution from \(G\), or from the exact-root correction in
Lemma 5.3,

\[
 Q_*^{[0,X]}\asymp
 a^2N^2R^{-4/3}(1+\log R),
 \tag{5.6}
\]

and

\[
 m_*(0,X)\le
 Ca^2NR^{4/3}(1+\log R)^{-1}.
 \tag{5.7}
\]

#### Proof

The target row norm satisfies

\[
 \rho(x)^2
 =2K_z^2\sum_l|w_l|^2e^{-2\kappa r_l^2x}
 \le Ca^2Ne^{-cR^2x}.
 \tag{5.8}
\]

Equations (5.3), (5.8), and \(E(x)\le CN\) give

\[
 \mathfrak q(x)\le Ca^2N^2e^{-cR^2x}.
 \tag{5.9}
\]

Duhamel's formula and (0.7)--(0.8) give

\[
 \|F(x)-e^{xD_q}F(0)\|_2
 \le C\varepsilon\sqrt N.
 \tag{5.10}
\]

Taking \(\gamma_0\) small, (5.2), (5.8), and (5.10) yield

\[
 |h(x)|\ge caN,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak q(x)\ge\lambda_0^{-1}|h(x)|^2
 \ge ca^2N^2
 \tag{5.11}
\]

for \(0\le x\le cR^{-2}\). The regularly varying endpoint weight obeys

\[
 \int_0^{cR^{-2}}w_*(x/X)\,dx
 \asymp R^{-4/3}(1+\log R).
 \tag{5.12}
\]

Equations (5.9)--(5.12) prove (5.6).

For (5.7), the exact profile formula from R0.72H gives

\[
 m_*(0,X)
 =\sum_l r_l^2|w_l|^2\Phi(2\kappa Xr_l^2),
 \tag{5.13}
\]

where

\[
 \Phi(y)\asymp y^{-1/3}[1+\log(2+y)]^{-1}
 \qquad(y\gg1).
 \tag{5.14}
\]

Using \(r_l\asymp R\) and \(|w_l|\asymp a\) proves (5.7).
\(\square\)

### Lemma 5.3 -- one exact interior root

Let \(U(x,s)\) be the finite-carrier evolution and set \(\tau=R^{-3}\),

\[
 A_R=P_0U(\tau,0)e_0,
 \qquad B_R=P_0U(\tau,0)G.
 \tag{5.15}
\]

For sufficiently small fixed \(\gamma_0\),

\[
 |A_R|\ge\frac12,
 \qquad
 |B_R|\le CgNR^{-3}.
 \tag{5.16}
\]

Define

\[
 \zeta_R=-B_R/A_R,
 \qquad
 \widetilde F(0)=G+\zeta_Re_0,
 \tag{5.17}
\]

and normalize \(\widetilde F(0)\) back to energy \(N\). Then

\[
 P_0F(\tau)=0,
 \qquad
 |\zeta_R|\le CgNR^{-3},
 \qquad
 |P_0V(\tau)F(\tau)|\ge caN.
 \tag{5.18}
\]

#### Proof

The uncoupled evolution keeps \(e_0\) in the target coordinate. Duhamel's
formula and

\[
 |\delta|\int_0^\infty\|V(x)\|\,dx
 \le CgBR^{-2}=C\varepsilon
 \tag{5.19}
\]

give the first estimate in (5.16) after decreasing \(\gamma_0\). The free
target coordinate of \(G\) vanishes. On \(0\le x\le\tau\), its first
coupled contribution is bounded by \(CgN\tau\), which proves the second
estimate. Equation (5.17) gives the root exactly by linearity. Finally,
(5.2), the short-time Duhamel bound, and

\[
 |\zeta_R|\rho(\tau)=o(aN)
 \tag{5.20}
\]

give the last estimate in (5.18). \(\square\)

The correction and \(A_R,B_R\) may be complex. Lemma 5.3 constructs one
exact zero and a non-collapsing row there. It does not put the whole target
trajectory on a fixed real line, so it does not authorize the real Rolle
sampling used in R0.72H.

### Theorem 5.4 -- common-band cubic no-go

Under (0.7)--(0.8) and (5.1)--(5.2),

\[
 \mathcal C_\times
 =|\delta|\int_0^X|hP_0V^2F|\,dx
 \le C\varepsilon a^2N^2.
 \tag{5.21}
\]

Under the exact physical amplitude balance used in R0.72I, its normalized
contribution obeys (0.13).

#### Proof

Equation (5.8), the heat-stable multiplier bound, and \(E(0)\le CN\) in
(4.4) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal C_\times
 &\le C|\delta|N
 \int_0^X(a^2Ne^{-cR^2x})(aBe^{-cR^2x})\,dx\\
 &\le C\frac{gB}{R^2}a^2N^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.22}
\]

For the physical conversion, the common-band carrier moments give

\[
 K_f\asymp NR^2,
 \qquad K_v\asymp a^2NR^2.
 \tag{5.23}
\]

The exact amplitude balance and enstrophy comparison from R0.72I therefore
give (0.12). In particular,

\[
 2\Theta\mathcal C_\times\le Cg^2NR^{-2}.
 \tag{5.24}
\]

Put

\[
 Z=g^2NR^{-10/3}(1+\log R).
 \tag{5.25}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{2\Theta\mathcal C_\times}
 {D^{1/3}(1+\mathscr A_*)}
 &\le C\frac{g^{4/3}N^{2/3}R^{-8/3}}{1+Z}\\
 &=CR^{-4/9}(1+\log R)^{-2/3}
 \frac{Z^{2/3}}{1+Z}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.26}
\]

The last scalar factor is uniformly bounded for \(Z\ge0\), proving
(0.13). \(\square\)

The theorem controls only the true cubic contribution in the differentiated
row. The mixed-row term \(\int|hQF|\), the one exact root in Lemma 5.3, and
the cubic contribution are all individually at their natural common-band
scales. Without a fixed real target gauge, I do not have a valid argument
that packs every complex target root into one complete ledger.

## 6. A genuinely non-bipartite coherent block

Set

\[
 \nu=d=K_z=q=1,
 \qquad K_y=0,
 \qquad
 S_R=\{R,R+1,\ldots,3R-1\},
 \tag{6.1}
\]

take \(w_r=1\), and define

\[
 (V_R(x)F)_k
 =-i\sum_{r\in S_R}e^{-r^2x}(F_{k-r}+F_{k+r}),
 \tag{6.2}
\]

\[
 G_R=\frac{i}{\sqrt2}\sum_{r\in S_R}(e_r+e_{-r}),
 \qquad \|G_R\|_2^2=2R.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

The set contains both parities and has gcd one, so Theorem 2.1 makes its
Cayley graph non-bipartite.

### Proposition 6.1 -- exact triangle count and initial cubic row

The number of ordered positive triples \(r+s=t\) in \(S_R\) is

\[
 P_R=\sum_{i=0}^{R-1}(R-i)=\frac{R(R+1)}2.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

Each ordered positive triple produces three placements of the negative term
and their global sign reversals. Hence the ordered signed count is

\[
 T_R=6P_R=3R(R+1).
 \tag{6.5}
\]

At \(x=0\), direct convolution gives

\[
 h_G(0)=P_0V_R(0)G_R=\sqrt2N=2\sqrt2R,
 \tag{6.6}
\]

\[
 P_0V_R(0)^2G_R=-\frac{iT_R}{\sqrt2},
 \qquad
 \boxed{|P_0V_R(0)^2G_R|=\frac{T_R}{\sqrt2}.}
 \tag{6.7}
\]

No numerical enumeration is needed for (6.4)--(6.7).

### Proposition 6.2 -- exact exposure and sharp raw cubic scale

The Fourier multiplier of \(V_R(x)\) has norm

\[
 \|V_R(x)\|=2\sum_{r=R}^{3R-1}e^{-r^2x}.
 \tag{6.8}
\]

Indeed, the triangle inequality gives the upper bound and equality holds at
Fourier angle zero. Therefore the total coupling exposure is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 |\delta|\int_0^\infty\|V_R(x)\|\,dx
 =2|\delta|\sum_{r=R}^{3R-1}r^{-2}.}
 \tag{6.9}
\]

Take \(\delta=\gamma R\) with a fixed sufficiently small \(\gamma>0\).
Then (6.9) is bounded above and below by fixed multiples of \(\gamma\).
On \(0\le x\le cR^{-2}\), all free heat factors are bounded away from
zero. Equations (6.6)--(6.7) therefore give

\[
 |h_{\rm free}(x)|\ge cR,
 \qquad
 |b_{\rm free}(x)|\ge cR^2.
 \tag{6.10}
\]

Duhamel's formula and (6.9) keep both rows within \(O(\gamma)\) of these
relative sizes. After reducing \(\gamma\),

\[
 |\delta|\int_0^{cR^{-2}}|h_Rb_R|\,dx
 \ge c\gamma R^2.
 \tag{6.11}
\]

Theorem 5.4 applies with \(N=2R\) and \(B\asymp N\), and gives the matching
upper bound. Thus

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal C_{\times,R}\asymp_\gamma R^2.}
 \tag{6.12}
\]

This is a true interaction lower bound, not a large separated
\(B_AQ_*\) upper term.

For this family,

\[
 \Theta\asymp R^{-1},
 \qquad D\asymp R^5,
 \qquad \mathscr A_*\asymp R^{-1/3}(1+\log R)=o(1).
 \tag{6.13}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{2\Theta\mathcal C_{\times,R}}
 {D^{1/3}(1+\mathscr A_*)}
 \asymp_\gamma R^{-2/3}\longrightarrow0.}
 \tag{6.14}
\]

The block saturates the raw common-band cubic scale and still loses after
physical normalization.

The launch (6.3) does not place the target trajectory on the fixed real line
required by the R0.72H Rolle corollary. Conversely, imposing a real skew
gauge and choosing the strictly row-aligned launch invokes (3.4) and removes
the initial cubic. I therefore use (6.12) only as a theorem about the true
cubic integral.

## 8. Literature boundary

## 9. What is and is not proved

The completed analytic content is:

1. the exact gcd-reduced bipartite criterion (0.2);
2. the exact distinction between a non-bipartite carrier graph and a signed
   Schur triangle;
3. the exact \(V_{\rm o}+V_{\rm e}\) block expansion (0.6);
4. the hybrid minimum (4.5), retaining either critical action or joint heat
   exposure;
5. the common-band negative-norm, critical-log, exact single-root, and true
   cubic estimates;
6. uniform decay (0.13) of the physically normalized true cubic
   contribution;
7. an explicit genuinely non-bipartite block with exact triangle count,
   exact exposure, sharp raw cubic mass, and the sharper decay (6.14).

This report does not prove:

1. a complete-root upper theorem when the target coordinate moves in the
   complex plane;
2. a simultaneous phase choice giving both the coherent direct cubic and
   the real-line Rolle mechanism;
3. a strong-coupling theorem beyond \(gB/R^2\le\gamma_0\);
4. a theorem for carriers spread over several separated frequency scales;
5. a generic physical critical-log inequality for every finite triangular
   flow;
6. any continuation criterion, singularity exclusion, or singularity
   construction for general three-dimensional Navier--Stokes solutions.

## 10. Exact conclusion and next gate

R0.72J shows that mixed parity is not by itself the obstruction. The exact
local obstruction is a signed additive relation, and even the densest
single-band example makes the true cubic row only as large as the joint heat
exposure permits. After the physical conversion, that contribution vanishes
uniformly.

The unresolved gate has two parts. First, the complete roots of a complex
target coordinate need a sampling argument that does not use one-dimensional
real Rolle theory. Second, the common-band proof must be tested against
multiscale carrier geometries, where different heat windows can accumulate
and \(gB/R^2\) is no longer one global parameter. Either part must be closed
before a broader claim is justified.
