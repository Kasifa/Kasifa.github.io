## Primary evidence

### Shear-enhanced dissipation is a different mechanism

Bedrossian, Vicol, and Wang prove stability near Couette flow and identify
mixing-enhanced dissipation at a viscosity-dependent time scale.  Their shear
has spatial variation and creates mixing.  In S, `B` is constant, can be
removed by translation, and the only decay is the ordinary heat factor.  The
time-phase estimate in S.34 is therefore not an enhanced-dissipation theorem.

- Jacob Bedrossian, Vlad Vicol, and Fei Wang, “The Sobolev stability threshold
  for 2D shear flows near Couette,” *Journal of Nonlinear Science* 28 (2018),
  2051--2075, arXiv:1604.01831,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01831.

Siming He studies passive scalars under nonconstant shear and obtains enhanced
dissipation and hypoelliptic estimates, including fractional diffusion.  This
supports the later nonconstant-shear branch but does not produce S's signed
physical-collar `L^3` conversion.

- Siming He, “Enhanced dissipation, hypoellipticity for passive scalar
  equations with fractional dissipation,” *Journal of Functional Analysis*
  282 (2022), 109319, arXiv:2103.07906,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07906.

### Spectral observation carries quantitative geometry

Egidi and Veselic prove scale-free unique-continuation and
Logvinenko--Sereda estimates on the torus for Fourier support in finitely many
parallelepipeds.  Their constants depend on the density and scale of the
observation set and on the spectral description.  S does not invoke this
theorem: its observation lower bound is calculated directly for one cosine in
one explicit radial subcollar.

- Michela Egidi and Ivan Veselic, “Scale-free unique continuation estimates
  and Logvinenko--Sereda Theorems on the torus,” *Annales Henri Poincare* 21
  (2020), 3757--3790, arXiv:1609.07020,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.07020.

Wang, Wang, Zhang, and Zhang characterize observable sets for the Euclidean
heat equation by quantitative thickness.  A single shrinking shell is not a
uniform thick set at the ambient scale.  S avoids that mismatch by retaining
the exact harmonic and proving its shell mass directly.

- Gengsheng Wang, Ming Wang, Can Zhang, and Yubiao Zhang, “Observable set,
  observability, interpolation inequality and spectral inequality for the
  heat equation in `R^n`,” *Journal de Mathematiques Pures et Appliquees* 126
  (2019), 144--194, arXiv:1711.04279,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04279.

### The global problem boundary remains unchanged

The official Clay problem asks for a global existence-and-smoothness or
breakdown conclusion for the three-dimensional incompressible equations.  S
proves an estimate only for an exact globally smooth shear subfamily and does
not address that quantifier.

- Charles L. Fefferman, “Existence and Smoothness of the Navier--Stokes
  Equation,” official Clay Mathematics Institute problem description,
  https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf.
