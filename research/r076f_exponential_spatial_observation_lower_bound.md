# R0.76F -- exponential lower bound for the inherited spatial observation

## 0. Result and exact boundary

R0.76E proved the upper observation row

\[
 \|g\|_{L^\infty(J)}
 +(\alpha+q)^{-1}\|g'\|_{L^\infty(J)}
 \le e^{Cq}\|g\|_{L^3(I)},
 \qquad I=[-1/2,1/2],\quad J=[-3/2,3/2],
 \tag{F.1}
\]

for the real spatial fibres generated by one dyadic packet.  The present
note tests whether the exponential dependence on the mode count can be
removed from this inherited row.

Fix an integer `q>=2` and a spacing

\[
 0<\delta\le\frac{2\pi}{3}.
 \tag{F.2}
\]

There is a real trigonometric polynomial with exactly `q` positive
frequencies in one dyadic band,

\[
 g_{q,\delta}(z)
 =\sum_{j=1}^q A_j
 \cos\bigl(n_j\delta z-\phi_j\bigr),
 \qquad
 n_j=q+j-1,
 \tag{F.3}
\]

where `A_j>=0` and `phi_j` are real, such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{
  \|g_{q,\delta}\|_{L^\infty(J)}
  +(q\delta+q)^{-1}
    \|g_{q,\delta}'\|_{L^\infty(J)}
 }{
  \|g_{q,\delta}\|_{L^3(I)}
 }
 \ge 2^{q-1}.}
 \tag{F.4}
\]

Consequently any constant valid in F.1 for all such real dyadic `q`-mode
fibres is at least `2^(q-1)`.  A constant uniform or polynomial in `q` is
impossible for this observation geometry.

This is a lower bound for the spatial observation step.  It is not a lower
bound for the complete collar flux in R0.76E.  In particular, it does not
exclude a different proof that avoids F.1 or uses cancellation in the full
space--time flux.

## 1. Exact dyadic construction

Put `z_*=3/2` and define the complex polynomial

\[
 H_{q,\delta}(z)
 :=e^{iq\delta z}\bigl(1-e^{i\delta z}\bigr)^{q-1}.
 \tag{F.5}
\]

The number `H_{q,delta}(z_*)` is nonzero under F.2.  Choose a real `theta`
so that

\[
 e^{i\theta}H_{q,\delta}(z_*)
 =|H_{q,\delta}(z_*)|,
 \tag{F.6}
\]

and set

\[
 g_{q,\delta}(z)
 :=\operatorname {Re}\!\left(
 e^{i\theta}H_{q,\delta}(z)
 \right).
 \tag{F.7}
\]

The binomial expansion gives

\[
 e^{i\theta}H_{q,\delta}(z)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}
 e^{i\theta}(-1)^k{q-1\choose k}
 e^{i(q+k)\delta z}.
 \tag{F.8}
\]

Thus F.3 holds with

\[
 A_{k+1}={q-1\choose k},
 \qquad
 \phi_{k+1}=-\theta-k\pi,
 \qquad 0\le k\le q-1.
 \tag{F.9}
\]

All amplitudes are nonnegative.  The integer frequencies are strictly
ordered and obey

\[
 n_1=q,
 \qquad n_q=2q-1\le2q=2n_1.
 \tag{F.10}
\]

Hence the construction lies inside the same real one-band class used in
R0.76E; no zero mode, repeated exponent, or frequency-gap division is
introduced.

## 2. Observation ratio

For real `z`,

\[
 |H_{q,\delta}(z)|
 =\left(2\left|\sin\frac{\delta z}{2}\right|\right)^{q-1}.
 \tag{F.11}
\]

If `z` belongs to `I`, then F.2 and monotonicity of sine on
`[0,pi/2]` give

\[
 |g_{q,\delta}(z)|
 \le|H_{q,\delta}(z)|
 \le\left(2\sin\frac\delta4\right)^{q-1}.
 \tag{F.12}
\]

Since `|I|=1`,

\[
 \|g_{q,\delta}\|_{L^3(I)}
 \le\left(2\sin\frac\delta4\right)^{q-1}.
 \tag{F.13}
\]

The phase choice F.6 and `z_*=3/2` give

\[
 \|g_{q,\delta}\|_{L^\infty(J)}
 \ge g_{q,\delta}(z_*)
 =\left(2\sin\frac{3\delta}{4}\right)^{q-1}.
 \tag{F.14}
\]

For `x=delta/4` in `(0,pi/6]`, the triple-angle identity yields

\[
 \frac{\sin(3x)}{\sin x}
 =3-4\sin^2x
 \ge2.
 \tag{F.15}
\]

Dividing F.14 by F.13 proves the lower bound `2^(q-1)` for the first term
in F.4.  The derivative term is nonnegative, so F.4 follows.

## 3. Realization as an exact shear fibre

Let `a,R>0` satisfy `delta=aR`, take the frequencies and coefficients in
F.9, and set `B=0`.  The exact heat shear

\[
 F(t,x_2)=\sum_{j=1}^q
 A_je^{-n_j^2t}\cos(n_jx_2-\phi_j)
 \tag{F.16}
\]

is a smooth unforced Navier--Stokes solution after embedding it as
`u=(0,0,F(t,x_2))` with constant pressure.  At the scaled time `s=0`,

\[
 G(0,z)=F(0,aRz)=g_{q,\delta}(z),
 \qquad \alpha=n_1aR=q\delta.
 \tag{F.17}
\]

Thus the counterexample is not an arbitrary trigonometric polynomial
inserted outside the exact-shear class.  It is an actual time fibre of the
same family.  The frozen scale has `delta(L)=a(L)R(L)` tending to zero, so
F.2 holds for all sufficiently large `L`.

## 4. Consequence for the growing-mode route

Let `C_q` be any coefficient that makes the spatial row F.1 valid uniformly
over the stated real dyadic family.  F.4 implies

\[
 \log C_q\ge(q-1)\log2.
 \tag{F.18}
\]

Therefore optimizing the constant inside the same spatial observation
mechanism cannot replace `e^(Cq)` by a polynomial loss.  In particular, if
`q(L)/L^2` has a positive lower limit along a subsequence, the observation
cost contributes a positive order-one amount to the normalized logarithmic
rate on that subsequence.  The exact coefficient `-2/11907` from R0.76E
cannot then be retained by merely sharpening F.1.  A sufficiently small
quadratic mode density could still leave a negative total exponent; that is
different from retaining the exact frozen coefficient.

The upper bound in R0.76E and F.18 match only at the level
`exp(Theta(q))`.  This note does not determine the optimal exponential base
or constant.

## 5. Literature and claim boundary

Nazarov's measurable Turan--Nazarov inequality already has an order
exponent that is sharp in the general class of exponential polynomials.
Sharp Remez inequalities for trigonometric polynomials likewise show
exponential growth in the polynomial degree for fixed observation geometry.
Those are literature facts, not local discoveries.

The local deduction is the explicit restriction of this obstruction to the
specific real, positive-frequency, one-dyadic-band shear fibres and the
fixed nested intervals `I` and `J` used in R0.76E.  The proof is elementary
once F.5 is chosen.  No novelty, priority, or optimal-constant claim is
made.

**Closed here:** uniform or polynomial dependence on `q` in the inherited
spatial observation row; exponential-order sharpness of that row for exact
real dyadic shears.

**Still open:** a matching lower bound for the complete signed collar flux;
the optimal exponential base in F.1; a proof bypassing spatial sup-norm
observation; mode counts comparable with `L^2`; arbitrary packets;
nonconstant shear; arbitrary-field E.24; complete Version-M extraction;
fixed deletion; suitable-weak transfer; regularity; and singularity.

The result is analytic.  Finite fixtures can audit the binomial, dyadic,
triple-angle, and asymptotic ledgers but are not proof of the continuum
statement.  No simulation or formal scientific figure is claimed.
