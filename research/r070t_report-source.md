# R0.70T — Exact frame stretching and a sharp fixed-frame divergence defect

**Status:** internal canonical candidate; not a public theorem chapter

**Release:** R0.70T

**Date:** 2026-08-25

## 1. Decision

R0.70S showed that the positive palinstrophy majorant

\[
 \int c_{r/E}\mathcal G
 \tag{1.1}
\]

cannot be controlled by the four R0.70Q energy-level inputs through a right
side that stays locally bounded at the zero input.  R0.70T therefore returns
to the signed enstrophy-production term

\[
 \mathscr P(t)
 =\int_{\mathbb T^3}\omega\cdot S\omega\,dx.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

The gain is an exact structural decomposition.

1. Complete-frame Parseval converts (1.2) into a covariance contraction plus
   one explicit stretching commutator.
2. The divergence-free condition on every filtered block cancels the naked
   gradient of the top covariance amplitude from the principal line term.
3. After the principal line contraction is integrated by parts and its pieces
   are kept separate, the longitudinal defect in that termwise ledger is

   \[
    \mathcal A_L
    =L\bigl(\nabla\lambda_1+2\lambda_1\operatorname{div}L\bigr),
    \tag{1.3}
   \]

   and satisfies the sharp pointwise block bound

   \[
    \boxed{
    |\mathcal A_L|
    \leq2\sqrt{\lambda_1\mathcal J_P},
    \qquad
    \mathcal J_P
    =\sum_\alpha|\operatorname{div}(P\Omega_\alpha)|^2.}
    \tag{1.4}
   \]

4. A pointwise near-rank ratio does not make \(\mathcal A_L\) small.  An
   explicit family of smooth periodic vorticities, generated by one velocity
   and passed through the fixed pinned frame, attains equality in (1.4) at one
   point while \(r/E\to0\).

This is not an enstrophy estimate.  It identifies the derivative channel
\(\mathcal J_P\) required by the absolute-value ledger (5.5), not an
independent signed obstruction.  Equations (10.2)--(10.3) show that, in the
complete signed integral, the \(\mathcal A_L\) contribution cancels with part
of the residual and stretching-commutator contribution.  The sharpness family
is stronger than an abstract covariance jet: it is a single smooth
mean-zero divergence-free periodic vorticity, its velocity is explicit, and
that velocity starts a global vertical-shear heat solution of Navier--Stokes.
The equality is local at the initial time; the sample has zero vortex
stretching and therefore is not a counterexample to an enstrophy closure.

No DNS or DGX computation is justified for this exact gate.  No public-page
update or GitHub publication is authorized by this report.

## 2. Conventions and covariance splitting

Work on normalized

\[
 \mathbb T^3=(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^3.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

All spatial integrals use normalized Haar measure \(dx\), so
\(\int_{\mathbb T^3}1\,dx=1\).

Let \(u\) be smooth, divergence free, and periodic, and put

\[
 \omega=\nabla\times u,
 \qquad
 B_{ij}=\partial_i u_j,
 \qquad
 S=\frac12(B+B^{\mathsf T}).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Use the pinned real-even complete scalar frame

\[
 \mathscr T=\{T_\star=\Pi_0\}\cup\{T_j:j\in\mathbb Z\},
 \qquad
 \widehat{T_jf}(k)=\varphi(2^{-j}k)\widehat f(k)
 \quad(k\ne0),
 \tag{2.3a}
\]

with zero multiplier at \(k=0\).  The cutoff is real, even, radial, and
smooth, and satisfies

\[
 \operatorname{supp}\varphi
 \subset\{\tfrac12<|\xi|<2\},
 \qquad
 \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}|\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)|^2=1
 \quad(\xi\ne0).
 \tag{2.3b}
\]

Thus

\[
 \sum_{\alpha\in\{\star\}\cup\mathbb Z}T_\alpha^2=I.
 \tag{2.3c}
\]

Every \(T_\alpha\) is self-adjoint, acts componentwise, and commutes with
spatial derivatives.  Define

\[
 \Omega_\alpha=T_\alpha\omega,
 \qquad
 Q=\sum_\alpha\Omega_\alpha\otimes\Omega_\alpha.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{div}\Omega_\alpha=0
 \tag{2.5}
\]

for every block, including the constant block.

At a point where the top eigenvalue is simple, write

\[
 \lambda=\lambda_1>\lambda_2\geq\lambda_3\geq0,
 \qquad
 L=\ell\otimes\ell,
 \qquad
 P=I-L.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

The local orientation \(\ell\) is used only for the proof.  All final
quantities are invariant under \(\ell\mapsto-\ell\).  Put

\[
 a_\alpha=\ell\cdot\Omega_\alpha,
 \qquad
 b_\alpha=P\Omega_\alpha,
 \qquad
 H=PQP.
 \tag{2.7}
\]

The spectral splitting gives

\[
 \boxed{
 Q=\lambda L+H,
 \qquad
 \lambda=\sum_\alpha a_\alpha^2,
 \qquad
 H=\sum_\alpha b_\alpha\otimes b_\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sum_\alpha a_\alpha b_\alpha=0.}
 \tag{2.8}
\]

The total amplitude and residual are

\[
 E=\operatorname{tr}Q=\lambda+r,
 \qquad
 r=\operatorname{tr}H=\sum_\alpha|b_\alpha|^2.
 \tag{2.9}
\]

All pointwise differentiated frame sums below are taken for smooth periodic
\(\omega\).  Its rapid Fourier decay and the finite annular overlap imply
local convergence of
\(\sum_\alpha(|\Omega_\alpha|^2+|\nabla\Omega_\alpha|^2)\), so \(Q\),
\(\nabla Q\), and the differentiated block identities are legitimate.  The
same statements hold under that local summability hypothesis.  They are not
asserted for a bare \(L^2\) vorticity.

For a symmetric tensor \(A\), the row-gradient-compatible divergence
convention is

\[
 (\operatorname{div}A)_j
 =\sum_i\partial_iA_{ij}.
 \tag{2.10}
\]

## 3. Exact complete-frame stretching identity

Define the stretching commutator blocks

\[
 \mathcal E_\alpha^S
 =[T_\alpha,S]\omega
 =T_\alpha(S\omega)-S\Omega_\alpha
 \tag{3.1}
\]

and the scalar defect

\[
 \mathfrak E_S(t)
 =\sum_\alpha
  \int_{\mathbb T^3}\Omega_\alpha\cdot\mathcal E_\alpha^S\,dx.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

### Proposition 3.1 — Parseval stretching ledger

For every smooth periodic field in Section 2,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr P(t)
 =\int_{\mathbb T^3}S:Q\,dx+\mathfrak E_S(t).}
 \tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Self-adjoint tightness gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int\omega\cdot S\omega
 &=\sum_\alpha\int
   T_\alpha\omega\cdot T_\alpha(S\omega)\\
 &=\sum_\alpha\int
   \Omega_\alpha\cdot S\Omega_\alpha
   +\sum_\alpha\int
   \Omega_\alpha\cdot[T_\alpha,S]\omega\\
 &=\int S:Q+\mathfrak E_S.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

For smooth fields, rapid Fourier decay, Parseval, and Cauchy--Schwarz justify
the infinite sums.  The integrated identity itself extends under the weaker
conditions \(\omega,S\omega\in L^2\), \(S\in L^\infty\), whenever the displayed
commutator square is finite.

If

\[
 \mathfrak C_S(t)
 =\sum_\alpha\|[T_\alpha,S]\omega(t)\|_2^2,
 \tag{3.5}
\]

then Cauchy--Schwarz in \(\ell_\alpha^2(L_x^2)\) yields

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathfrak E_S(t)|
 \leq\|\omega(t)\|_2\,\mathfrak C_S(t)^{1/2}.}
 \tag{3.6}
\]

The quantity \(\mathfrak C_S\) is not the projector commutator square
\(\mathfrak C_P\) from R0.70Q.

## 4. Exact amplitude-gradient cancellation

Let

\[
 \mathcal R_L=I-2L.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

It is the orthogonal reflection across \(\operatorname{Ran}P\).  In
particular,

\[
 |\mathcal R_Lz|=|z|.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Define the orientation-free longitudinal defect

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A_L
 =L\bigl(\nabla\lambda+2\lambda\operatorname{div}L\bigr).}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — Reflection form and residual-divergence defect

At every simple-top point,

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{div}(\lambda L)
 =\lambda\mathcal R_L\operatorname{div}L+\mathcal A_L.}
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Moreover, in any local orientation from (2.6),

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A_L
 =-2\ell\sum_\alpha
 a_\alpha\operatorname{div}b_\alpha.}
 \tag{4.5}
\]

Consequently, with

\[
 \mathcal J_P
 =\sum_\alpha|\operatorname{div}(P\Omega_\alpha)|^2,
 \tag{4.6}
\]

one has

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathcal A_L|
 \leq2\sqrt{\lambda\mathcal J_P}.}
 \tag{4.7}
\]

#### Proof

The algebraic product rule gives

\[
 \operatorname{div}(\lambda L)
 =L\nabla\lambda+\lambda\operatorname{div}L.
 \tag{4.8}
\]

Adding and subtracting \(2\lambda L\operatorname{div}L\) proves (4.4).
To use the block origin, expand (2.5):

\[
 0=\operatorname{div}(a_\alpha\ell+b_\alpha)
 =\ell\cdot\nabla a_\alpha
  +a_\alpha\operatorname{div}\ell
  +\operatorname{div}b_\alpha.
 \tag{4.9}
\]

Since \(\lambda=\sum_\alpha a_\alpha^2\),

\[
 \ell\cdot\nabla\lambda
 =-2\lambda\operatorname{div}\ell
  -2\sum_\alpha a_\alpha\operatorname{div}b_\alpha.
 \tag{4.10}
\]

For \(L=\ell\otimes\ell\),

\[
 L\operatorname{div}L
 =(\operatorname{div}\ell)\ell.
 \tag{4.11}
\]

Equations (4.3), (4.10), and (4.11) give (4.5).  Finally,
Cauchy--Schwarz in the block index gives (4.7).

### Rank-one boundary

If \(Q\) has rank one at a simple-top point, positivity of the block sum
forces \(b_\alpha=0\) for every \(\alpha\) at that point.  On its simple-gap
neighbourhood, differentiating the spectral cross identity in (2.8), and then
evaluating at the rank-one point, gives

\[
 \sum_\alpha a_\alpha\partial_kb_\alpha=0.
 \tag{4.12}
\]

Contracting the vector identity (4.12) with the matching coordinate direction
and summing over \(k\) gives
\(\sum_\alpha a_\alpha\operatorname{div}b_\alpha=0\).  Hence
\(\mathcal A_L=0\) at every smooth rank-one simple-top point.  If rank one
holds on an open region, then also \(\mathcal J_P=0\) there.  The second
statement is stronger: rank one at one point does not force every individual
derivative \(\partial_kb_\alpha\), or \(\mathcal J_P\), to vanish.

That distinction is exact already for the local divergence-free block jets

\[
 \Omega_1=(1-x_1,x_2,0),
 \qquad
 \Omega_2=(1+x_1,-x_2,0).
 \tag{4.13}
\]

At the origin, \(Q=2e_1\otimes e_1\), both transverse values vanish, and
\(\mathcal A_L=0\), while the two projected divergences are \(1\) and \(-1\),
so \(\mathcal J_P=2\).  This auxiliary jet is not claimed as a fixed-frame
periodic realization.

## 5. Exact enstrophy-production ledger

Sections 5--6 assume, at the time under consideration, that
\(\lambda_1(x)>\lambda_2(x)\) for every \(x\in\mathbb T^3\).  Compactness and
smoothness then make \(L\), \(P\), and the displayed spatial derivatives
globally smooth and periodic.  Without this global simple-top hypothesis the
pointwise identities remain valid on simple-gap patches, but the following
whole-torus integrals involving \(L\) are not asserted.

Because \(\lambda L\) is symmetric,

\[
 \int_{\mathbb T^3}\lambda L:S\,dx
 =\int_{\mathbb T^3}\lambda L:B\,dx
 =-\int_{\mathbb T^3}
   u_*\cdot\operatorname{div}(\lambda L)\,dx,
 \tag{5.1}
\]

where \(u_*=u-\int u\).  The constant velocity drops out after periodic
integration by parts.

The orientation-free covariance-divergence ledger is

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{div}Q
 =\lambda\mathcal R_L\operatorname{div}L
  +\mathcal A_L+\operatorname{div}H.}
 \tag{5.2}
\]

Combining (2.8), (3.3), and (4.4) gives the equivalent exact signed identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathscr P(t)={}&
 -\int\lambda u_*\cdot
   \mathcal R_L\operatorname{div}L\,dx
 -\int u_*\cdot\mathcal A_L\,dx\\
 &+\int S:H\,dx
 +\mathfrak E_S(t).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Since \(H\) is positive semidefinite and \(\operatorname{tr}H=r\),

\[
 |H|_F\leq r.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore (3.6), (4.2), and (4.7) imply

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 |\mathscr P(t)|\leq{}&
 \int\lambda|u_*|\,|\operatorname{div}L|\,dx
 +2\int|u_*|\sqrt{\lambda\mathcal J_P}\,dx\\
 &+\int|S|_F r\,dx
 +\|\omega\|_2\mathfrak C_S^{1/2}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{5.5}
\]

If \(Q\) is rank one throughout the torus at a fixed time and the top
eigenvalue remains simple, then \(H=0\) and \(\mathcal A_L=0\) pointwise, so
(5.3) reduces to

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr P(t)
 =-\int E u_*\cdot
   \mathcal R_L\operatorname{div}L\,dx
  +\mathfrak E_S(t).}
 \tag{5.6}
\]

Thus exact multiscale alignment removes amplitude-gradient production but
does not remove line bending/divergence or the stretching commutator.

## 6. The new defect remains at derivative level

Define

\[
 \mathcal D_b
 =\sum_{\alpha,k}
  |\partial_k(P\Omega_\alpha)|^2
 \tag{6.1}
\]

and retain

\[
 \mathcal D_P
 =\sum_{\alpha,k}|P\partial_k\Omega_\alpha|^2.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

To fix the curvature convention from R0.70Q, choose at the point any
orthonormal eigenbasis \((\ell,v_2,v_3)\).  If \(\lambda_2=\lambda_3\), the
individual lower projectors need not be selected smoothly; all sums below are
basis invariant.  Define the reduced inverse to be zero on
\(\operatorname{Ran}L\) and the inverse of
\((\lambda I-Q)|_{\operatorname{Ran}P}\) on \(\operatorname{Ran}P\):

\[
 \mathcal R_Q
 =P\bigl((\lambda I-Q)|_{\operatorname{Ran}P}\bigr)^{-1}P
 =\sum_{b=2}^3\frac{L_b}{\lambda-\lambda_b},
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_Q
 =\sum_k\operatorname{tr}
  \bigl(L(\partial_kQ)\mathcal R_Q(\partial_kQ)\bigr).
 \tag{6.2a}
\]

With

\[
 c_{kb}
 =\frac{v_b^{\mathsf T}(\partial_kQ)\ell}{\lambda-\lambda_b},
 \tag{6.2b}
\]

the exact half-curvature normalization is

\[
 \mathcal K_Q
 =\sum_{k,b=2}^3(\lambda-\lambda_b)c_{kb}^2,
 \qquad
 |\nabla P|_F^2=2\sum_{k,b=2}^3c_{kb}^2.
 \tag{6.2c}
\]

The divergence contraction and the product rule give

\[
 \mathcal J_P\leq3\mathcal D_b,
 \tag{6.3}
\]

\[
 \mathcal D_b
 \leq2\mathcal D_P+2E|\nabla P|_F^2.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

Indeed,
\(\partial_k(P\Omega_\alpha)=P\partial_k\Omega_\alpha
+(\partial_kP)\Omega_\alpha\), and

\[
 \sum_{\alpha,k}|(\partial_kP)\Omega_\alpha|^2
 =\sum_k\operatorname{tr}
  ((\partial_kP)Q(\partial_kP))
 \leq E|\nabla P|_F^2.
 \tag{6.4a}
\]

If the relative top gap obeys

\[
 \lambda_1-\lambda_2\geq\gamma E,
 \qquad \gamma>0,
 \tag{6.5}
\]

then (6.2c) gives, with the fixed half-curvature convention,

\[
 E|\nabla P|_F^2
 \leq2\gamma^{-1}\mathcal K_Q.
 \tag{6.6}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal J_P
 \leq6\mathcal D_P+12\gamma^{-1}\mathcal K_Q.}
 \tag{6.7}
\]

This is an upper ledger, not a closure.  Both terms on the right are at the
vorticity-gradient level.  R0.70T therefore does not turn the new defect into
an energy quantity.

## 7. Sharpness inside the fixed frame and a global shear solution

The coefficient \(2\) in (4.7) cannot be reduced even after imposing the
common-origin constraint \(\Omega_\alpha=T_\alpha\omega\) for the fixed pinned
radial frame.

Let

\[
 M=2^m,\qquad m\geq4,
 \tag{7.1}
\]

and define two periodic stream functions

\[
 \psi_L
 =\frac M5\sin(5x_2)
  +\frac M{24}
   \bigl[\cos(3x_1+4x_2)-\cos(3x_1-4x_2)\bigr],
 \tag{7.2}
\]

\[
 \psi_H
 =-\frac1{5M}\sin(5Mx_1)
  +\frac1{25}\cos(5Mx_2).
 \tag{7.3}
\]

Set

\[
 U_M=M^{-1}(\psi_L+\psi_H),
 \qquad
 u_M^0=(0,0,U_M),
 \qquad
 \omega_M=\nabla\times u_M^0.
 \tag{7.4}
\]

Then \(u_M^0\) and \(\omega_M\) are smooth and mean zero, and
\(\operatorname{div}\omega_M=0\).  Split

\[
 \omega_M=\omega_L+\omega_H,
 \tag{7.5}
\]

where direct differentiation gives

\[
 \omega_L=
 \left(
  \cos(5x_2)-\frac13\sin(3x_1)\cos(4x_2),
  \frac14\cos(3x_1)\sin(4x_2),0
 \right),
 \tag{7.6}
\]

\[
 \omega_H=
 \left(
  -\frac15\sin(5Mx_2),
  M^{-1}\cos(5Mx_1),0
 \right).
 \tag{7.7}
\]

Every Fourier mode of \(\omega_L\) has radius \(5\), and every mode of
\(\omega_H\) has radius \(5M\).  If

\[
 I_5=\{j:\varphi(2^{-j}5e_1)\ne0\},
 \tag{7.8}
\]

then the strict support condition in (2.3b) gives

\[
 \varnothing\ne I_5\subset\{2,3\},
 \qquad
 I_{5M}=I_5+m\subset\{m+2,m+3\}.
 \tag{7.9}
\]

The sets are disjoint.  Radiality gives the same real multiplier on every
mode in one shell, dyadic scaling gives the same list of multipliers on the
two shells, and tightness gives

\[
 \sum_{j\in I_5}c_j^2=1,
 \qquad
 c_j=\varphi(2^{-j}5e_1).
 \tag{7.10}
\]

Consequently, without assuming that both possible indices are active,

\[
 T_j\omega_M=c_j\omega_L,
 \qquad
 T_{j+m}\omega_M=c_j\omega_H
 \quad(j\in I_5),
 \tag{7.11}
\]

all other nonzero-mode blocks vanish, \(\Pi_0\omega_M=0\), and

\[
 \boxed{Q[\omega_M]
 =\omega_L\otimes\omega_L+\omega_H\otimes\omega_H.}
 \tag{7.12}
\]

At \(x_0=0\),

\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\omega_L&\omega_H\\ \hline
 \text{value}&e_1&M^{-1}e_2\\
 \partial_1&-e_1&0\\
 \partial_2&e_2&-Me_1\\
 \partial_3&0&0
 \end{array}
 \tag{7.13}
\]

and therefore

\[
 Q(x_0)=\operatorname{diag}(1,M^{-2},0),
 \qquad
 \partial_1Q(x_0)=-2e_1\otimes e_1,
 \qquad
 \partial_2Q(x_0)=\partial_3Q(x_0)=0.
 \tag{7.14}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \lambda=1,
 \quad r=M^{-2},
 \quad E=1+M^{-2},
 \quad L=e_1\otimes e_1,\\
 \nabla L=0,
 \quad\operatorname{div}L=0,
 \quad\mathcal A_L=-2e_1.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{7.15}
\]

For the low-shell blocks,
\(\operatorname{div}(P T_j\omega_M)(x_0)=c_j\); the high-shell block
divergences vanish at \(x_0\).  Thus

\[
 \mathcal J_P(x_0)=\sum_{j\in I_5}c_j^2=1
 \tag{7.16}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathcal A_L(x_0)|
 =2
 =2\sqrt{\lambda(x_0)\mathcal J_P(x_0)}.}
 \tag{7.17}
\]

Meanwhile the local residual ratio is

\[
 \frac rE(x_0)=\frac1{M^2+1}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{7.18}
\]

The accompanying frame-gradient density is

\[
 \mathcal G(x_0)
 =\sum_\alpha|\nabla\Omega_\alpha(x_0)|_F^2
 =M^2+2.
 \tag{7.19}
\]

For the archived anchor \(M=16\),

\[
 E(x_0)=\frac{257}{256},
 \quad r(x_0)=\frac1{256},
 \quad\left(\frac rE\right)(x_0)=\frac1{257},
 \quad\left(\frac{\lambda_1-\lambda_2}{E}\right)(x_0)=\frac{255}{257},
 \quad\mathcal G(x_0)=258.
 \tag{7.20}
\]

This is an exact obstruction inside the fixed frame, not multiplier leakage:
common origin and pointwise near rank one alone neither improve the constant
in (4.7) nor make \(\mathcal A_L\) small relative to
\(\sqrt{\lambda\mathcal J_P}\).  The statement is local at \(x_0\); it does
not assert a uniform near-rank condition on the torus.  Equation (7.19) shows
the derivative price explicitly, so the family does not obstruct estimates
that retain palinstrophy, \(\mathcal J_P\), or a higher Sobolev norm.

The velocity is also Navier--Stokes compatible in a stronger sense.  Because
\(u_M^0\) is independent of \(x_3\) and points in the \(e_3\) direction,

\[
 (u_M^0\cdot\nabla)u_M^0=0.
 \tag{7.21}
\]

Hence \(u_M(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}u_M^0\), with constant pressure, is a smooth
global unforced periodic Navier--Stokes solution.  For every vertical shear
of this form, however, \(S\omega=0\) and the signed vortex stretching is
identically zero.  The family establishes the sharp local defect boundary at
\(t=0\); it is not a stretching-production counterexample and does not show
that equality or near rank is propagated by the evolution.

## 8. Conditional continuation reading

The vorticity enstrophy identity is

\[
 \frac12\frac d{dt}\|\omega\|_2^2
 +\nu\|\nabla\omega\|_2^2
 =\mathscr P(t).
 \tag{8.1}
\]

Let the right side of (5.5) be \(\mathfrak P_L(t)\).  Suppose a maximal
strong solution has \(T_{\max}<\infty\), and suppose
\(\lambda_1(x,t)>\lambda_2(x,t)\) on all of
\(\mathbb T^3\times[0,T_{\max})\), so the Section 5 ledger is defined.  If

\[
 \int_0^{T_{\max}}\mathfrak P_L(t)\,dt<\infty,
 \tag{8.2}
\]

then (8.1) gives a uniform enstrophy bound and the standard periodic
div--curl identity gives \(\|\nabla u\|_2=\|\omega\|_2\).  Together with the
energy inequality this is a uniform \(H^1\) bound, so the standard periodic
\(H^1\) blow-up alternative extends the solution past \(T_{\max}\), a
contradiction to maximality.  Equivalently, under the global simple-top
hypothesis, finite-time breakdown forces the integral in (8.2) to diverge.

This corollary is only a reduction.  Condition (8.2) is not derived from
initial enstrophy or Leray energy here.  In particular:

1. \(\mathcal J_P\) is derivative-sensitive by (6.7);
2. \(\mathfrak C_S\) is a new stretching commutator, not the already isolated
   projector commutator;
3. the principal line term still carries the covariance amplitude \(\lambda\);
4. the fixed-frame sharpness family is pointwise at \(t=0\), does not propagate
   the hypothesis, and has identically zero signed vortex stretching.

## 9. Prior-art boundary

The geometric depletion of vortex stretching through coherence of the
physical vorticity direction begins with
[Constantin--Fefferman (1993)](https://iumj.org/article/3627/) and includes,
among many refinements,
[Beirao da Veiga--Berselli (2002)](https://people.dm.unipi.it/beiraodaveiga/pdf/hbv-79.pdf),
[Ruzmaikina--Grujic (2004)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-004-1072-0),
[Zhou (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446181100008270), and
[Grujic (2009)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-008-0726-8).
Those works concern the physical direction \(\omega/|\omega|\), its spatial
coherence, and the Biot--Savart stretching kernel.  The line \(L\) here is
instead the top eigenspace of a complete filtered covariance.

Multiscale harmonic analysis and vorticity geometry have already been joined:
[Chae (2007)](https://ems.press/journals/rmi/articles/4744) balances regularity
of the physical vorticity direction against magnitude integrability in
Triebel--Lizorkin-type norms.  Frequency-localized Navier--Stokes criteria and
Littlewood--Paley commutators are also established tools; see
[Bradshaw--Grujic (2017)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00205-016-1069-9)
and [Cheskidov--Dai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06611).  Thus the Parseval and
multiplication-commutator step (3.3) is a standard harmonic-analysis ledger,
not a claim of a new mechanism.

The incompressibility conversion of an amplitude derivative into a direction
divergence also has a clear analogue in
[Vasseur (2007)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.2446) for the physical velocity
direction.  Likewise,
[Miller (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02152) gives a criterion using a
space--time-dependent plane and a bounded derivative of its normal.  It does
not construct the covariance \(Q\) or the defect \(\mathcal A_L\), but it is
adjacent projector geometry.  Finally, the phrase “spectral projection of
vorticity” in
[Neustupa--Penel (2012)](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/mathematique/item/10.1016/j.crma.2012.06.008.pdf)
refers to the Hilbert-space spectral resolution of curl, not the pointwise
eigenprojector of a Littlewood--Paley covariance.

Accordingly, (4.4) is recorded as elementary tensor algebra and (4.5) as its
direct divergence-free block consequence.  The route-specific package is the
